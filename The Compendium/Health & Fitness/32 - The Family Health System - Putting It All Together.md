# The Family Health System: Putting It All Together

*Health & Fitness · 2026-10-30*

Thirty-one notes is a lot of knowledge. This one turns it into a system any Indian family can run, at any income level, without becoming health experts: a short list of rules, a few daily and weekly routines, a calendar of tests, a one-page record for each person, an emergency plan, and a 90-day way to start. Print it, adapt it, share it.

---

## 1. The ten rules

If a family remembers nothing else from this series:

1. **No tobacco or areca nut in any form.** The single biggest change for many Indian families ([[16 - Tobacco, Alcohol & Environmental Exposures]]).
2. **Know your numbers.** BP, HbA1c, LDL, waist, and Lp(a) once. "I feel fine" isn't a test ([[24 - Silent Killers - Diseases That Hide Until Late]]).
3. **Half the plate vegetables, protein at every meal, grain as one portion** ([[11 - The Indian Plate - Rebuilding the Thali]]).
4. **Less salt, less sugar, the right oil** in moderate amounts; no vanaspati or reused frying oil.
5. **Move every day**: a brisk 30-minute walk plus a walk after meals ([[13 - Cardio, Mobility & Balance]]).
6. **Strength and balance twice or three times a week** — muscle is medicine at every age ([[12 - Strength Training - Muscle as Medicine]]).
7. **Sleep 7–9 hours on a regular schedule**; treat loud snoring with pauses ([[14 - Sleep & Circadian Rhythm]]).
8. **Stay connected**: talk, share meals, check on each other, ask for help with low mood ([[15 - Stress, Mental Health & Social Connection]]).
9. **Take prescribed medicines every day; don't take unnecessary ones** — no casual antibiotics, painkillers or "cures" ([[28 - Medicines Without Myths]]).
10. **Know the emergency signs and act within minutes**: chest pain, BE FAST for stroke, dengue danger signs, snakebite, heat stroke ([[25 - Fast Killers - Emergencies & Warning Signs]]).

## 2. The daily and weekly routine

### Every day
- [ ] A vegetable-heavy, protein-at-every-meal thali; a katori of curd or chaas
- [ ] 30 minutes of brisk walking (or equivalent), plus 10–15 minutes after lunch and dinner
- [ ] Balance practice for a few minutes (older adults)
- [ ] Medicines taken from the pill organiser
- [ ] Foot check (people with diabetes)
- [ ] Morning daylight; regular bedtime; phone away at night
- [ ] At least one real conversation with family or friends

### Two or three days a week
- [ ] 20–30 minutes of strength exercises (sit-to-stands, wall push-ups, step-ups, bridges, rows, carries)

### Every week
- [ ] Home BP readings (more often if high or on treatment)
- [ ] Weight
- [ ] Glucometer checks as advised (diabetes)
- [ ] Plan the week's dal, vegetables and protein; soak pulses; restock roasted chana, peanuts, fruit
- [ ] Empty and clean water containers to prevent dengue mosquitoes

### Every month
- [ ] Waist measurement
- [ ] Mouth self-check (tobacco or areca nut users, or ex-users)
- [ ] Review BP and sugar logs; check what's due on the testing calendar
- [ ] Refill medicines; check expiry dates; restock the home kit

## 3. The testing calendar

The full schedule is in [[27 - The Testing Calendar]]. In short:
- **At home:** BP, weight, waist, feet, mouth, glucose if diabetic.
- **Quarterly:** only for what's abnormal or being treated (HbA1c until at target, lipids after statin changes, TSH after thyroid dose changes).
- **Yearly from about 30:** HbA1c, fasting glucose, lipids (± ApoB), creatinine with eGFR, urine albumin, urine routine, CBC, LFT, TSH, vitamin D and B12 as needed; ECG and eye checks from 40; dental every 6–12 months.
- **Once in life:** Lp(a), hepatitis B and C, HIV, blood group, thalassaemia carrier status.
- **Cancer screening by age:** mouth, cervix, breast, bowel; lung and prostate by discussion ([[23 - Cancer - How It Starts and How to Catch It Early]]).
- **Vaccines by age** ([[17 - Infections, Hygiene & Vaccines]]).

## 4. The one-page health card (one per person)

Keep a printed copy at home and a photo on family phones.

```
NAME:                      AGE:        BLOOD GROUP:
EMERGENCY CONTACTS:        (name, relation, phone)
NEAREST 24-HR HOSPITAL:    (with ECG/CT/cath lab)    AMBULANCE: 108 / 112

CONDITIONS:                (e.g., diabetes since 2026, high Lp(a))
ALLERGIES:                 (medicines, foods)
CURRENT MEDICINES:         (generic name – dose – time)
PAST SURGERIES / STENTS / HOSPITAL STAYS:
TOBACCO / ALCOHOL:         (current / quit date)
FAMILY HISTORY:            (heart attack <55/65, diabetes, cancers, etc.)

KEY NUMBERS (date – value):
  BP:            HbA1c:          LDL:          Lp(a) (once):
  eGFR:          TSH:            Hb:           Vit D / B12:
  Weight:        Waist:

VACCINES:      Td (date)   Flu (year)   Pneumococcal   Hep B   Shingles   HPV
SCREENING:     Oral (date) Cervical (date) Breast (date) Colorectal (date) Eyes (date) DEXA (date)

INSURANCE:     Insurer, policy no., helpline, TPA, Ayushman card no.
ABHA ID:
```

Alongside the card, keep a **results log** — a notebook or spreadsheet with dates and values of key tests over the years, so trends are visible ([[26 - Reading a Blood Report]]).

## 5. The emergency plan

- **Save** 112, 108 (and 102 for pregnancy and children) on every phone, plus the nearest 24-hour hospital and a nearby family member or neighbour.
- **Know the hospital** that has ECG, CT and, ideally, a cath lab — and how to get there quickly, day or night.
- **Keep a home kit:** BP monitor, glucometer, thermometer, pulse oximeter, ORS, paracetamol, aspirin (for a suspected heart attack only, if advised and not allergic), first-aid supplies, prescribed emergency medicines, a torch.
- **Learn hands-only CPR**, ideally the whole family.
- **Agree a family rule:** for chest pain, stroke signs, a severe snakebite or a confused older adult, **go first, discuss later**.
- **Keep the health card and insurance details** where anyone can find them.

## 6. The system at every income level

Most of the benefit costs nothing. Money adds speed, comfort and protection, not the core.

| Area | Free or almost free | Moderate budget | Comfortable budget |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tobacco** | Quitline 1800-11-2356; family quit date; saunf/elaichi | NRT gum or lozenges | Doctor-guided medicines and counselling |
| **Food** | Rebalance the thali: fewer rotis, more dal, sabzi, curd; mixed atta; home-made paneer and curd | Wider pulses, fruit daily, nuts and seeds, eggs/fish if eaten | Millets, better oils, more variety; a tested protein supplement if food falls short; dietitian sessions |
| **Movement** | Walking, chair and wall exercises, carrying, stairs | Resistance bands, good footwear | Physiotherapy plan, gym or classes |
| **Monitoring** | Free BP and sugar checks at government centres; waist tape | Home BP monitor, glucometer, scale | Wearable step counter; broader monitoring as advised |
| **Testing** | NP-NCD free screening (30+) | Yearly core panel at a NABL-accredited lab | Doctor-tailored additions (ApoB, CAC score, DEXA, etc.) |
| **Medicines** | Free government NCD medicines; Jan Aushadhi generics | Generics from chosen pharmacy; pill organiser | Regular specialist follow-up |
| **Protection** | Ayushman Bharat (eligible families; all 70+) | Private insurance base policy + super top-up | Higher cover, OPD add-ons |
| **Mental health** | Tele-MANAS 14416; family time; community groups | Counselling | Regular therapy if needed |

## 7. Roles in the family

A system runs better when someone owns each part:
- **Health coordinator** (often an adult child, even from a distance): keeps the cards, the logs and the testing calendar; books tests and appointments; reads reports and prepares questions ([[26 - Reading a Blood Report]]).
- **Kitchen lead:** owns the thali rebalancing, the weekly dal and vegetable plan, and the mixed atta.
- **Movement buddy:** walks or exercises with the older members.
- **Medicine checker:** fills the weekly pill organiser and checks refills.
- **Everyone:** watches for emergency signs and knows the plan.

For children living away from parents: a short daily or every-other-day call that asks about BP readings, the walk, food, sleep and mood, plus visits that include a doctor's appointment, keeps the system alive.

## 8. Starting: the first 90 days

Don't try to do everything in week one. A sequence that works for most families:

**Days 1–14: Measure and decide**
- Get baseline tests and a doctor's review (bring all reports and medicines).
- Buy and learn to use a BP monitor; start the BP and weight log; measure waist.
- Make health cards for everyone.
- Set a tobacco quit date; call the quitline; get NRT if needed.
- Save emergency numbers; identify the nearest capable hospital.
- Check insurance; start Ayushman card or private insurance applications.

**Days 15–45: Fix the plate and start moving**
- Rebalance the thali: salad and dal first, 2–3 rotis, dal at both meals, curd daily, protein snacks.
- Mixed atta at the chakki.
- Halve sugar in tea; cut extra salt and achar.
- Daily brisk walk plus walks after meals.
- Start the pill organiser.

**Days 46–90: Build strength and lock in habits**
- Begin strength exercises two or three times a week; add balance practice.
- Physiotherapy assessment for knee pain if present.
- Sleep routine: fixed times, phone out of the bedroom.
- Vaccines due (flu, pneumococcal, Td, shingles, hepatitis B).
- Repeat tests as the doctor advised (HbA1c, lipids, TSH); compare with baseline.
- Review: what's working, what's slipping, what's next.

**After 90 days:** keep the routines; follow the testing calendar; add one improvement every month or two; review the whole system once a year.

## 9. Measuring whether it's working

Track a small set of outcome numbers yearly, per person:
- **BP** at target (usually below 130/80, as advised)
- **HbA1c** at target (normal, or below 7% with diabetes, as advised)
- **LDL** at the target set by the doctor
- **Waist** below 90 cm (men) / 80 cm (women)
- **Tobacco-free** (yes/no)
- **Strength and balance:** 30-second chair stands; one-leg stand
- **Steps per day**
- **Sleep hours**
- **Tests and vaccines up to date** (yes/no)
- **How they feel**: energy, mood, pain, ability to do what matters to them

The last one matters most. The point of the system isn't perfect numbers; it's more good years — working, walking, travelling, playing with grandchildren — and fewer years in hospitals.

## 10. Sharing it

This system is designed to be shared freely. When explaining it to other families:
- Lead with **their** goals and worries, not statistics.
- Start with the **ten rules** and the **first 90 days**; the details can come later.
- Encourage everyone to **see a doctor** with their reports; this system supports medical care, it doesn't replace it.
- Keep it **practical, local and affordable**: dal, curd, mixed atta, a walk after dinner, a BP cuff, and a quit date.

---

## The layer, in one line

A family health system is ten simple rules, a few daily and weekly routines, a testing calendar, a one-page health card and an emergency plan for each person, run by clear roles, started in a 90-day sequence and scaled to any budget — because most of the benefit comes from tobacco-free homes, a rebalanced thali, daily movement, regular measurement and acting quickly when something goes wrong.
