# The Immune System & Inflammation

*Health & Fitness · 2026-10-30*

The immune system is what keeps a body full of bacteria, viruses and parasites from being eaten alive, and inflammation is its main weapon. Used briefly, inflammation heals a wound or clears an infection. Left switched on at a low level for years, the same weapon quietly damages arteries, joints, the liver and the brain. Many modern diseases are, in part, the immune system working when it shouldn't.

---

## 1. Two layers of defence

### Innate immunity: the fast, general response
**Innate immunity** is the defence you're born with. It responds within minutes to hours, attacking anything that looks foreign or damaged, without needing to recognise the specific invader. It includes:

- **Barriers:** skin, stomach acid, the mucus lining of the airways and gut, and the healthy gut microbiome ([[07 - Gut Health & Digestion]]).
- **Neutrophils:** the most common white blood cell. They arrive first at an infection, especially a bacterial one, and swallow and destroy microbes. Pus is largely dead neutrophils.
- **Monocytes and macrophages:** monocytes circulate in the blood and turn into **macrophages** ("big eaters") in tissues. They swallow microbes and debris and send out alarm signals. In arteries, macrophages that swallow too much cholesterol become the foam cells of plaque ([[19 - How Heart Disease Actually Happens]]).
- **Eosinophils:** fight parasites such as worms and are involved in allergies and asthma. A high eosinophil count is a common clue to worms or allergy.
- **Basophils and mast cells:** release **histamine**, which drives allergic reactions (itching, swelling, sneezing).
- **Natural killer (NK) cells:** destroy virus-infected cells and some cancer cells.

### Adaptive immunity: the slow, specific, memorising response
**Adaptive immunity** takes days to ramp up on first contact, but it's precise and it remembers.

- **Lymphocytes** are its cells. **B cells** make **antibodies**, proteins that lock onto one specific target (an **antigen**) and mark it for destruction or neutralise it. **T cells** either directly kill infected cells or coordinate the rest of the response.
- After an infection clears, **memory B and T cells** remain for years or decades. If the same germ returns, the response is fast and strong, often stopping illness entirely.

**Vaccines** use this memory. They show the immune system a harmless version or piece of a germ, so memory cells form without the person going through the disease ([[17 - Infections, Hygiene & Vaccines]]).

## 2. How the white blood count maps to this

On a CBC ([[05 - Blood, Circulation & the Heart]]), the white cell differential is a quick window into what the immune system is doing:

| Cell | Typical share | Commonly high in | Commonly low in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Neutrophils** | ~40–80% | Bacterial infection, stress, steroids | Some viral infections, some medicines, bone marrow problems |
| **Lymphocytes** | ~20–40% | Viral infection | Severe stress, steroids, some infections |
| **Monocytes** | ~2–10% | Chronic infection, recovery phase | Usually not significant on its own |
| **Eosinophils** | ~1–6% | Worms, allergy, asthma | Not usually significant |
| **Basophils** | ~0–2% | Allergy, rare blood disorders | Not usually significant |

A single slightly abnormal value is common and often means nothing. Patterns and persistent changes matter more, and a doctor interprets them alongside symptoms.

## 3. Inflammation: the weapon

**Inflammation** is the immune system's coordinated response to injury or infection. The classic signs, known since ancient times:

- **Redness and heat:** blood vessels widen to bring more blood.
- **Swelling:** vessels become leaky so immune cells and proteins can reach the tissue.
- **Pain:** signalling chemicals sensitise nerves, encouraging you to protect the area.
- **Loss of function:** a swollen joint stiffens, for example.

Immune cells release signalling proteins called **cytokines** that recruit more cells, cause **fever** (higher temperature slows many germs and speeds immune activity), and tell the liver to make **acute-phase proteins** such as **CRP**.

### Acute inflammation: the good kind
Short-lived, targeted and self-limiting: a cut heals, a sore throat clears, muscles repair after exercise. This is essential. Suppressing it too aggressively, for example by routinely taking painkillers after every workout, can blunt healing and adaptation.

### Chronic low-grade inflammation: the harmful kind
Sometimes the inflammatory switch stays partly on for months or years, at a level too low to cause obvious symptoms. This **chronic low-grade inflammation** (sometimes called **"inflammaging"** when linked to ageing) is a shared driver of:

- **Atherosclerosis** ([[19 - How Heart Disease Actually Happens]])
- **Insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes** ([[18 - Blood Sugar, Insulin Resistance & Diabetes]])
- **Fatty liver** progressing to scarring ([[08 - The Liver & Kidneys - Factories and Filters]])
- **Osteoarthritis pain** ([[21 - Joints & Bones - Osteoarthritis, Osteoporosis & Falls]])
- Some **cancers** ([[23 - Cancer - How It Starts and How to Catch It Early]])
- **Depression** and cognitive decline ([[15 - Stress, Mental Health & Social Connection]])

### What keeps the switch on
- **Visceral fat:** belly fat actively releases inflammatory cytokines.
- **Tobacco:** constant irritation of airways and blood vessels.
- **Uncontrolled blood sugar:** sugar-damaged proteins trigger immune responses.
- **A low-fibre, high-ultra-processed diet** that weakens the gut barrier.
- **Gum disease:** chronically inflamed gums are linked with heart disease and diabetes. Brushing twice daily and dental check-ups matter more than most people realise.
- **Chronic infections:** untreated worms, *H. pylori*, hepatitis B or C, TB.
- **Poor sleep and chronic stress.**
- **Inactivity.**
- **Air pollution and chulha smoke** ([[16 - Tobacco, Alcohol & Environmental Exposures]]).

### What turns it down
Exercise (each session produces anti-inflammatory signals), losing belly fat, stopping tobacco, a plant-rich high-fibre diet with fermented foods, good sleep, treating infections and dental problems, and managing stress. Notice this is the same list of levers that appears in almost every note: chronic inflammation is one of the threads that ties them together.

## 4. Measuring inflammation

- **CRP** (C-reactive protein): made by the liver when inflammation is present. Rises sharply with infection or injury.
- **hs-CRP** (high-sensitivity CRP): the same protein measured more precisely at low levels, used to estimate cardiovascular risk. Roughly: below 1 mg/L is low risk, 1–3 average, above 3 higher risk. A value above 10 usually points to an active infection or another acute cause rather than heart risk. Because a cold or a minor injury can raise it, a high hs-CRP should be repeated after a few weeks before drawing conclusions.
- **ESR** (erythrocyte sedimentation rate): an older, non-specific marker of inflammation, still used for some joint and infectious diseases.

None of these tells you *where* the inflammation is. They're signals to look further, not diagnoses.

## 5. When the immune system misfires

### Allergy
The immune system overreacts to something harmless (pollen, dust mites, certain foods, insect stings). Most allergies are a nuisance, but a severe reaction called **anaphylaxis** — swelling of the lips, tongue or throat, difficulty breathing, a sudden drop in BP — is an emergency ([[25 - Fast Killers - Emergencies & Warning Signs]]).

### Autoimmune disease
The immune system attacks the body's own tissues. Examples include **rheumatoid arthritis** (joints), **type 1 diabetes** (pancreas), **Hashimoto's thyroiditis** (the most common cause of an underactive thyroid), **psoriasis** (skin), **lupus** and **coeliac disease** (gut, triggered by gluten). Autoimmune diseases are more common in women. Many respond well to modern treatment when diagnosed early.

### Immune weakness
The body's defences fall with:
- **Age:** older adults respond more weakly to infections and vaccines.
- **Diabetes:** high sugar impairs white cells, so infections such as TB, urinary infections, skin and foot infections are more common and more severe.
- **Malnutrition**, especially protein, zinc, vitamin A and vitamin D deficiency ([[02 - Micronutrients & RDAs]]).
- **Long-term steroid use.**
- **HIV**, cancer treatment and some other medicines.
- **Poor sleep, chronic stress and heavy alcohol.**

## 6. Supporting a healthy immune system

There's no supplement or "immunity booster" that makes a healthy immune system work better than normal, despite heavy marketing, especially in India after COVID. What actually helps:

- **Adequate nutrition**, especially protein, and correcting deficiencies a test has found.
- **Sleep** of 7–9 hours ([[14 - Sleep & Circadian Rhythm]]).
- **Regular moderate exercise.**
- **No tobacco**, little or no alcohol.
- **Controlled blood sugar.**
- **Vaccinations** appropriate for age and risk ([[17 - Infections, Hygiene & Vaccines]]).
- **Hygiene:** handwashing, clean water, safe food.
- **Dental care.**

---

## The layer, in one line

The immune system defends through fast general responses and slow, precise, memorising ones, using inflammation as its weapon; short bursts heal, but low-grade inflammation left switched on by belly fat, tobacco, high sugar, poor diet and poor sleep quietly drives heart disease, diabetes, joint pain and more.
