# Campaigns and Campaign Influence

## What campaigns are for

A Campaign is a marketing program: a trade show, an email sequence, a webinar, a paid advertising push. Its purpose in the data model is **attribution** — connecting marketing spend to revenue.

The question campaigns answer is the one marketing is always asked: *which programs generated pipeline, and what was the return?*

## The objects

**Campaign.** `Name`, `Type`, `Status`, `StartDate`, `EndDate`, `BudgetedCost`, `ActualCost`, `ExpectedRevenue`, `IsActive`, `ParentId` for campaign hierarchies.

Several roll-up fields are maintained by the platform: `NumberOfLeads`, `NumberOfContacts`, `NumberOfResponses`, `NumberOfConvertedLeads`, `NumberOfOpportunities`, `AmountAllOpportunities`, `AmountWonOpportunities`.

**CampaignMember.** The junction linking a Campaign to either a Lead or a Contact, with a `Status` — Sent, Responded, Attended, Registered, and so on.

It has both `LeadId` and `ContactId`, and exactly one is populated. When a Lead converts, its CampaignMember records automatically repoint to the new Contact, which is what makes attribution survive conversion.

`HasResponded` is derived from whether the member's status is flagged as a responded status in the campaign's member status configuration.

```sql
SELECT Campaign.Name, Status, HasResponded, FirstRespondedDate,
       Lead.Company, Contact.Account.Name
FROM CampaignMember
WHERE CampaignId = :campaignId AND HasResponded = true
```

## Campaign hierarchies

`ParentId` builds a tree, and unlike Account hierarchies, campaign hierarchies **do** roll up. Fields such as `HierarchyNumberOfLeads` and `HierarchyAmountWonOpportunities` aggregate across the whole subtree.

This is genuinely useful: a "Q3 Manufacturing Push" parent campaign containing a trade show, an email sequence, and a paid search campaign gives both the individual and combined return.

The hierarchy is limited to five levels.

## The Campaign field on Opportunity

`CampaignId` on Opportunity is the **primary campaign source** — a single field naming the campaign that gets credit.

It is populated automatically during lead conversion if the lead has a campaign member record, and can be set manually otherwise.

Its limitation is obvious: one field, one campaign. A deal touched by four programs credits one. This single-touch model is why campaign influence exists.

## Campaign Influence

Campaign Influence records connect **multiple** campaigns to one opportunity, each with a percentage and an attributed revenue amount.

The mechanism: when an opportunity is created or won, the platform looks at the contacts with **Opportunity Contact Roles** on that deal, finds the campaigns those contacts are members of, and creates influence records for campaigns whose member record falls within a configured time window before the opportunity was created.

Two things follow, and both are the reason campaign influence frequently produces nothing:

**It depends entirely on Opportunity Contact Roles.** No contact roles means no influence records. This is why [[04.6 - Contacts and Contact Roles]] matters more than its low profile suggests, and why enforcing contact roles through validation is worth the friction.

**It depends on a configured time window.** The default influence timeframe is typically a number of days before opportunity creation. Campaign touches outside that window are ignored.

## Attribution models

Salesforce offers several, and **Customizable Campaign Influence** allows more than one model to run simultaneously so the business can compare.

**Primary Campaign Source.** All credit to the one campaign on `CampaignId`. Simple, and wrong in any multi-touch reality.

**Even distribution.** Credit split equally across all influencing campaigns.

**First touch.** All credit to the earliest campaign. Favours demand generation.

**Last touch.** All credit to the most recent campaign before opportunity creation. Favours late-stage programs.

**Custom models** can be built with Apex, writing `CampaignInfluence` records directly with whatever weighting the business wants.

```java
public with sharing class WeightedInfluenceService {

    public static void applyWeightedModel(Set<Id> opportunityIds, Id modelId) {
        Map<Id, List<CampaignMember>> membersByOpportunity = getInfluencingMembers(opportunityIds);
        Map<Id, Opportunity> opps = new Map<Id, Opportunity>([
            SELECT Id, Amount FROM Opportunity WHERE Id IN :opportunityIds
        ]);

        List<CampaignInfluence> influences = new List<CampaignInfluence>();

        for (Id oppId : membersByOpportunity.keySet()) {
            List<CampaignMember> members = membersByOpportunity.get(oppId);
            Decimal amount = opps.get(oppId).Amount == null ? 0 : opps.get(oppId).Amount;

            // 40% to first touch, 40% to last, remainder split across the middle
            for (Integer i = 0; i < members.size(); i++) {
                Decimal weight;
                if (members.size() == 1) {
                    weight = 100;
                } else if (i == 0 || i == members.size() - 1) {
                    weight = 40;
                } else {
                    weight = 20.0 / (members.size() - 2);
                }

                influences.add(new CampaignInfluence(
                    CampaignId = members[i].CampaignId,
                    OpportunityId = oppId,
                    ModelId = modelId,
                    Influence = weight,
                    RevenueShare = amount * weight / 100
                ));
            }
        }

        delete [SELECT Id FROM CampaignInfluence WHERE OpportunityId IN :opportunityIds AND ModelId = :modelId];
        Database.insert(influences, false);
    }
}
```

Deleting the existing influence records for that model before inserting is what makes recalculation idempotent — without it, running the job twice doubles the attribution.

## Campaign member status

Each campaign defines its own member statuses, with one marked as the default and one or more flagged as "Responded".

A trade show might use: Invited, Registered, Attended, No Show — with Attended flagged as responded.
An email campaign might use: Sent, Opened, Clicked, Unsubscribed — with Clicked flagged as responded.

Getting these right per campaign type is what makes response rate a meaningful number. Leaving every campaign on the default Sent and Responded produces response rates that mean nothing.

## Adding members in bulk

Campaign members are usually added by import, by a report-based add, or by integration.

```java
public with sharing class CampaignMemberService {

    public static void addContacts(Id campaignId, Set<Id> contactIds, String status) {
        Set<Id> existing = new Set<Id>();
        for (CampaignMember cm : [
            SELECT ContactId FROM CampaignMember
            WHERE CampaignId = :campaignId AND ContactId IN :contactIds
        ]) {
            existing.add(cm.ContactId);
        }

        List<CampaignMember> toAdd = new List<CampaignMember>();
        for (Id contactId : contactIds) {
            if (!existing.contains(contactId)) {
                toAdd.add(new CampaignMember(
                    CampaignId = campaignId,
                    ContactId = contactId,
                    Status = status
                ));
            }
        }

        if (!toAdd.isEmpty()) {
            Database.insert(toAdd, false);
        }
    }
}
```

The duplicate check matters because the platform enforces uniqueness of campaign plus member, and an insert containing an existing pair fails.

## Return on investment

The reporting that justifies the whole feature:

```sql
SELECT Campaign.Name campaignName, Campaign.ActualCost cost,
       SUM(RevenueShare) attributedRevenue, COUNT(Id) opportunityCount
FROM CampaignInfluence
WHERE Opportunity.IsWon = true
  AND Opportunity.CloseDate = THIS_FISCAL_YEAR
  AND Model.Name = 'Weighted Multi-Touch'
GROUP BY Campaign.Name, Campaign.ActualCost
ORDER BY SUM(RevenueShare) DESC
```

The prerequisites for this query to produce anything useful: `ActualCost` maintained on campaigns, contact roles on opportunities, campaign members created for every touch, and an influence model running. Missing any one produces empty or misleading output, which is why campaign attribution so often disappoints.

## Marketing Cloud and Account Engagement

When a separate marketing platform is in use, campaign membership is usually synchronized from it rather than maintained in Salesforce.

**Account Engagement (Pardot)** has a fairly tight connector that maps its lists and programs onto Salesforce campaigns and syncs member status.

**Marketing Cloud Engagement** is a looser integration, and campaign membership sync typically requires deliberate design or middleware.

Either way, the Salesforce campaign becomes the reporting surface while the marketing platform owns execution. The design question is which system owns member status, and the answer must be one of them, not both.

## Practical guidance

**Enforce Opportunity Contact Roles.** Campaign influence produces nothing without them, and this is the single most common reason attribution fails.

**Maintain `ActualCost`.** Return on investment without cost is just revenue.

**Configure member statuses per campaign type.** Defaults produce meaningless response rates.

**Use campaign hierarchies** to group related programs; the roll-ups genuinely work.

**Pick one attribution model as the official one** and run others for comparison. Multiple models presented as equally true produces arguments rather than decisions.

**Do not let the campaign list grow unmanaged.** Thousands of campaigns with no naming convention and no hierarchy makes reporting impossible.
