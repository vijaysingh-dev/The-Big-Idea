# End to End Enterprise Service Cloud Design

## The brief

Extending the Northwind Industrial architecture from [[04.18 - End to End Enterprise Sales Cloud Design]].

> The same manufacturer. Around 90 support agents across India, EMEA, and APAC. Roughly 80,000 cases a year across email, phone, web portal, WhatsApp, and machine-generated alerts. Service contracts carry contractual response and resolution targets with financial penalties. A field engineering team of 40 handles on-site repairs. SAP owns parts inventory and billing.

Ten times the record volume of the sales side, and time is contractual rather than aspirational. Both change the design.

## The Asset decision, resolved

Section 02 modelled installed equipment as a custom `Machine__c`. Sections 05.5 and 05.13 both argued for the standard **Asset** object instead, from entitlements and from Field Service respectively.

**Resolution: use Asset.** `Machine__c` becomes Asset, with custom fields for serial number, install date, and firmware version.

What that buys: entitlement assignment works natively, Field Service work orders link to it, Case has a standard `AssetId`, and asset hierarchies model a production line containing component machines without a custom self-lookup.

What it costs: less control over the object, and a migration if this decision is made late. Which is exactly why it belongs in the design phase — this is the "prefer the standard object even when the fit is imperfect" principle from [[02.1 - Objects Overview]], and two independent features pointing at it is a strong signal.

`Machine_Coverage__c` remains a custom junction, now between Asset and `Service_Contract__c`.

## Case model

**Three record types**, each with its own Support Process:

```
Technical Support
  New → In Progress → Awaiting Customer → Awaiting Parts → Awaiting Field Visit
  → Resolved → Closed

Warranty Claim
  Submitted → Under Review → Awaiting Evidence → Approved → Rejected → Closed

Commercial Enquiry
  New → In Progress → Awaiting Customer → Resolved → Closed
```

Every process has an **Awaiting Customer** status, and Technical Support adds Awaiting Parts and Awaiting Field Visit. All three stop the milestone clock. Without them the SLA metrics charge the support team for supplier lead times and customer response delays, and with penalties attached that is a commercial problem, not just a reporting one.

`Reason` is required on closure across all three. `Type`, `Origin`, and `AssetId` are populated on creation wherever the channel allows.

## Channels

| Channel | Mechanism | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Email | On-Demand Email-to-Case | Header-based threading; domain fallback for contact matching |
| Phone | Service Cloud Voice | Chosen for transcription and post-call summaries |
| Web portal | Experience Cloud | Authenticated; case deflection against knowledge |
| WhatsApp | Messaging | Consent captured at contract signing |
| Machine alerts | Inbound Email Service | Custom handler parsing serial number and severity |

The machine alert handler is the one worth calling out. Equipment emails a fault code; a custom `InboundEmailHandler` parses the serial number, resolves the Asset, sets the Account from it, maps the fault code to a priority, and creates the case with assignment and auto-response flags set. See [[05.6 - Email-to-Case and Web-to-Case]].

Every channel attaches to a **Case**. Conversations, transcripts, and voice calls are children of it, so an issue that moves from WhatsApp to phone to email is one case with one history.

## Routing

Two layers, as recommended in [[05.3 - Case Assignment and Auto-Response Rules]]:

**Assignment rules place cases in a queue** — by record type, product line, and region. Five entries plus a catch-all.

**Omni-Channel distributes from queue to agent** — Least Active, capacity 10 per agent, cases costing 3, chats 5, voice calls 10.

**Omni-Channel Flow** handles the conditional routing that rules cannot:

- Route to the agent who last handled this contact, if available and within 90 days
- Route Premium-entitlement cases ahead of Standard
- Require the Press Line Certified skill for press-related technical cases, with degradation after 20 minutes

Skill degradation is not optional. Without it, a case needing a rare certification waits indefinitely, which breaches the SLA more reliably than a slightly less qualified agent would.

## Entitlements and SLAs

**Entitlement per covered Asset**, created when a `Machine_Coverage__c` record is created, linked to the Account and the Asset, with the entitlement process chosen by coverage level.

Two entitlement processes:

```
Premium
  First Response    2 business hours    warning at 80%
  Resolution        8 business hours    warning at 75%

Standard
  First Response    1 business day      warning at 80%
  Resolution        3 business days     warning at 75%
```

**Three business hours records** — India, EMEA, APAC — each with local holidays, and the entitlement carries the one matching the customer's region.

Entitlement is assigned in a **before-insert** trigger, because the milestone targets are calculated from the case creation timestamp. Assigning afterwards produces wrong targets, silently.

Asset-level entitlements take precedence over account-level ones, since a customer may have Premium on one production line and Standard on the rest.

## Field service

Adopted, because 40 engineers and genuine scheduling complexity clear the bar set in [[05.13 - Field Service Basics]].

Work orders are created from cases with a **Work Type** carrying estimated duration, required skills, and standard line items. Enhanced Scheduling and Optimization runs with two policies: an emergency policy that ignores travel efficiency for critical faults, and a routine policy that clusters geographically.

Parts consumed on a work order push to SAP, which owns inventory as the system of record. Salesforce holds van stock for visibility; SAP is authoritative for financial accuracy, reconciled nightly.

The Awaiting Field Visit status stops the milestone clock while an appointment is scheduled but not yet due — otherwise the resolution SLA is breached by the customer's own site access constraints.

## Knowledge

Lightning Knowledge with three record types — Troubleshooting, Product FAQ, Internal Procedure — and data categories on product line and audience.

Internal Procedure articles are restricted by data category visibility so they never reach the portal.

Articles are drafted from cases at closure, with a quick action prefilling title, summary, and resolution. Customer-facing articles go through an approval process; internal ones do not, because a two-week approval cycle on internal notes means nobody writes them.

The knowledge gap report runs monthly: high-volume case reasons with no article attached, which directs what gets written next.

## AI

Deployed in the order recommended by [[05.15 - Einstein for Service and Agentforce]] — agent-assist first, customer-facing later.

**Live now:** Einstein Case Classification suggesting Type, Reason, and Product; article recommendations on the case page; work summaries at closure; Voice call transcription and post-call summaries.

**Agentforce on the portal and WhatsApp,** handling three topics: claim status, appointment scheduling, and knowledge questions. Every action verifies that the requester's Contact belongs to the Account on the record before returning anything.

**Not deployed:** autonomous customer-facing email replies. Drafting for agent review only. The bar for sending unreviewed text to a customer under a contract with penalties is higher than the current knowledge base supports.

## Security

Extending the model from 04.18:

| Object | OWD | Reasoning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Case | Private | Customer data, and portal users must see only their own |
| Asset | Private | Equipment location and configuration |
| Entitlement | Controlled by Parent | Follows the Account |
| WorkOrder | Private | Operational |
| Knowledge | Data category controlled | Internal versus customer-facing |

Support agents get Read on Account and Contact, full access to Case, Asset, and WorkOrder, and **no Opportunity access at all** — simpler and more robust than granting the object and restricting records.

Portal users reach their own cases through **Sharing Sets** on the Experience Cloud license, not the role hierarchy, since high-volume portal licenses have no roles. See [[17.9 - Experience Cloud Basics]].

## Volume and archiving

80,000 cases a year, each with email messages, status history rows, and often a work order. Five years is 400,000 cases and several million child records.

The archiving design, planned before it is urgent:

- Cases closed over three years, with no open contractual obligation, move to a big object
- `Case_Status_History__c` older than two years is summarized and archived
- Chat and messaging transcripts are retained 18 months
- Voice recordings follow the per-region legal retention policy, which differs across the three regions
- A Lightning component on the Account queries the big object by index key, so archived history is still visible in context

That last point is what makes archiving acceptable to the business. Without it, archiving feels like data loss. See [[23.4 - Archiving with Big Objects and External Storage]].

## Reporting

**Backlog and SLA snapshots** run daily, because the platform shows current state and leadership wants the trend.

Three dashboard tiers — agent, team lead, executive — with individual agent metrics deliberately absent from the executive view.

**Omni-Channel Supervisor** on a wall screen for team leads.

The metrics that drive decisions: backlog trend, first response and resolution compliance by customer, reopen rate, case volume by reason and product, deflection rate, and CSAT sampled at one case in five.

SLA compliance by customer goes to finance quarterly, because breaches carry penalties. Its accuracy depends entirely on the stopped-milestone configuration being right, which is why that detail is load-bearing rather than cosmetic.

## What was deliberately not built

- **No custom SLA engine.** Entitlements and milestones handle business hours, pausing, and warnings correctly. Rebuilding that calculation by hand is a known source of subtle bugs.
- **No custom routing engine.** Omni-Channel Flows cover the conditional logic.
- **No separate ticketing for machine alerts.** They are Cases with a distinct Origin, so they appear in the same backlog and the same SLA reporting.
- **No custom knowledge search.** The standard component plus Einstein recommendations outperforms a hand-built search.
- **No autonomous customer-facing generation.** Deferred until knowledge quality justifies it.

## Section 05 in one paragraph

Service Cloud is Sales Cloud's data model at ten times the volume with contractual time pressure, and both facts drive the design. The status model is the foundation: statuses describing who you are waiting for are what make escalation, SLA measurement, and every duration metric honest. Routing splits into assignment rules choosing a queue and Omni-Channel choosing a person, with flows handling anything conditional. Entitlements and milestones exist because business-hours-aware time calculation is genuinely hard and getting it wrong has financial consequences. The console is where performance stops being optional, because every second multiplies by fifty interactions a day. And knowledge quality is the constraint on everything AI-related — generative service features are only as good as the articles they are grounded in.
