# End to End Enterprise Sales Cloud Design

## The brief

A reference design pulling section 04 together. The business:

> Northwind Industrial sells industrial machinery across India, EMEA, and APAC. Around 200 sales users. Deals range from spare parts orders worth a lakh to production line installations worth several crore, with sales cycles from two weeks to eighteen months. Machines are sold outright, then covered by annual service contracts that renew. Sales works alongside a service organization that must see equipment and contracts but not deal economics. An SAP system owns products, orders, and invoicing. Roughly 8,000 opportunities a year.

## Data model

**Standard objects, used as intended:**

Account for customers and prospects. No Person Accounts — this is business-to-business.
Contact for people, with Contacts to Multiple Accounts enabled since consultants and group procurement officers span entities.
Lead for unqualified inbound.
Opportunity for all deals, with two record types.
Product2, Pricebook2, PricebookEntry, OpportunityLineItem for the catalogue.
Contract for service agreements.
Campaign and CampaignMember for marketing.

**Custom objects, from section 02:**

`Machine__c` — installed equipment, lookup to Account.
`Service_Contract__c` — lookup to Account, lookup to the originating Opportunity.
`Machine_Coverage__c` — junction, master-detail to both Machine and Service Contract.
`Opportunity_Stage_History__c` — stage timing for cycle analysis.
`Forecast_Snapshot__c` — weekly forecast trend.

**Not built:** an Order object, because SAP owns fulfilment and Salesforce only needs to know a deal closed. This is a deliberate omission rather than an oversight, and it removes an entire object's worth of data entry and sync.

## Record types and processes

Two Opportunity record types, each with its own Sales Process:

```
New Equipment Sale
  Qualification → Site Survey → Technical Proposal → Commercial Proposal
  → Negotiation → Closed Won / Closed Lost

Parts and Service Sale
  Quote Requested → Quote Sent → Negotiation → Closed Won / Closed Lost
```

Two, not five. Every additional record type multiplies the layout assignment grid and the testing surface, and the two sales motions here are genuinely different. Regional variation is handled with Dynamic Forms, not more record types.

Closed Lost Reason is a mandatory picklist on both, enforced by validation.

## Security model

**Organization-wide defaults:**

| Object | Internal OWD | Reasoning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Account | Public Read Only | Everyone benefits from the customer directory |
| Contact | Controlled by Parent | No meaning without the account |
| Opportunity | Private | Commercially sensitive |
| Machine__c | Private | Service owns equipment; not every rep needs every customer's kit |
| Service_Contract__c | Private | Commercial terms |
| Machine_Coverage__c | Controlled by Parent | Master-detail on Machine |
| Case | Private | Service data |

**Role hierarchy:** four levels. VP Sales, Regional Director, Sales Manager, Sales Rep. Parallel branch for service. Deliberately shallower than the org chart, because visibility needs fewer levels than reporting does.

**Territory Management: enabled.** Coverage genuinely overlaps — geographic reps, a national large-accounts team, and product specialists for the press line. This is exactly the case territories exist for. Three territory types: Geographic, Named Account, Product Specialist.

**Sharing rules:**

- Criteria-based on `Machine__c` where `Region__c` matches, shared to the corresponding regional service public group, Read/Write
- Criteria-based on `Service_Contract__c` where `Status__c = 'Active'`, shared to the "Service Delivery" public group, Read Only
- Owner-based on Opportunity, shared to the "Deal Desk" public group, Read Only, for pricing governance

All shares target **public groups**, never roles or individuals, so membership changes require no rule edits.

**The service organization sees machines and contracts, not deal economics.** Achieved by not granting Read on Opportunity to service profiles at all — simpler and more robust than granting the object and restricting records.

**Permission sets, not profiles.** One minimal profile per license type. Capability granted through permission set groups: Sales Representative, Sales Manager, Deal Desk, Service Coordinator, Sales Operations.

## Lead process

Web forms post to a custom Apex REST endpoint rather than Web-to-Lead, giving control over validation and deduplication ([[04.2 - Leads and Lead Management]]).

Inbound leads are deduplicated on email against unconverted leads and existing contacts, then routed. Assignment uses Custom Metadata mapping postcode prefix and industry to a territory queue, applied in a before-insert handler, with a catch-all assignment rule behind it.

Every integration creating leads sets `useDefaultRule = true`. This is on the integration test checklist, because the failure is silent.

Conversion matches to existing accounts by email domain before creating new ones. Custom lead fields are mapped or not created.

A scheduled hygiene batch disqualifies leads with no activity for eighteen months, with a reason.

## Opportunity lifecycle

**Stage validation** is Custom Metadata-driven Apex rather than a validation rule per stage, because there are two processes and around a dozen stage-field requirements between them. Documented prominently in the org, since Apex validation is invisible to admins.

**Contact roles** are enforced: at least one primary contact role before reaching Commercial Proposal. A trigger maintains a count field on Opportunity, since the relationship is not master-detail.

**Stage history** is captured on every stage change, feeding cycle-time reporting.

**Close date slippage** is counted, with the original close date preserved.

**Products** are required on New Equipment Sale opportunities before Commercial Proposal, so product-line revenue reporting works. Not required on Parts and Service, where the line-item overhead is not justified.

## Approvals

Discount approval, with thresholds by both percentage and absolute value:

```
Discount > 15% OR Amount > 50,00,000     → Sales Manager
Discount > 30% OR Amount > 2,00,00,000   → Regional Director
Discount > 50%                            → VP Sales
```

Approvers come from a lookup field populated by Custom Metadata-driven Apex, not from the Manager field — so routing can be changed without deactivating the approval process.

Delegated approvers are configured for every approver. Notifications go to Slack, not only email.

Approval duration is reported weekly. A bottleneck in the approval chain is a bottleneck in the sales cycle.

## Forecasting

Collaborative Forecasts on opportunity revenue, running on **splits**, because solutions engineers carry an overlay quota.

Two split types: a revenue split totalling 100 percent, and an overlay split for specialists.

A weekly batch writes forecast snapshots, since the platform does not retain forecast history and leadership wants the trend.

Custom fiscal year is standard, starting in April to match the Indian financial year — established before go-live, since changing it later is disruptive.

## Renewals

A scheduled batch reads Contracts ending within 120 days with no renewal opportunity, and creates one. The `Renewal_Opportunity__c = null` filter makes the job idempotent, so a rerun does not duplicate.

Renewal opportunities use the Parts and Service record type and link back to the source contract, enabling renewal-rate reporting.

## Integration with SAP

**SAP is the system of record for** products, prices, orders, invoices, and the customer master.

**Salesforce is the system of record for** leads, opportunities, contacts, activities, and forecast.

That division is written down. Ambiguity here produces update loops where two systems overwrite each other.

**Product and price sync:** nightly Bulk API upsert on `ERP_Product_Code__c`, standard price book entries first, then regional books.

**Account sync:** bidirectional on `ERP_Account_Id__c`. SAP owns billing address and credit status; Salesforce owns industry, ownership, and segment.

**Closed-won push:** a Platform Event published when an opportunity reaches a won stage, with a subscriber making the callout. Keeps the HTTP call out of the user's save transaction and gives independent limits and retries.

**Invoice history:** an External Object through Salesforce Connect. Twelve years of invoices are not copied into Salesforce; they are displayed on the account through an indirect lookup on `ERP_Account_Id__c`.

Every integration is idempotent through external IDs, and every failure is logged to `Integration_Log__c` with enough context to replay.

## Automation architecture

One trigger per object, routing to a handler, which dispatches to services taking collections. Services never read `Trigger` context, so they are callable from flows, batch jobs, and tests.

```
OpportunityTrigger
  └── OpportunityTriggerHandler
        ├── OpportunityStageValidator.validateStageRequirements()
        ├── OpportunityStageService.recordStageEntry()
        ├── ApproverAssignmentService.assignDiscountApprover()
        ├── ContactRoleRollupService.updateRoleCounts()
        └── RenewalOpportunityService.createRenewals()
```

Flows handle the simple, admin-owned automation: field defaults, task creation, notifications. Apex handles anything needing queries, loops, or cross-object logic.

A documented rule: **no flow and no trigger on the same object doing overlapping work.** Where both exist, the order between them is not fully controllable, and debugging becomes guesswork.

## What was deliberately not built

Worth listing, because restraint is most of good architecture:

- **No custom deal object.** Opportunity, with record types.
- **No Order object.** SAP owns fulfilment.
- **No CPQ.** The pricing rules here are percentage discounts on a catalogue of a few hundred products. If subscription terms or bundle configuration appear later, that is a licensing conversation, not a build.
- **No custom sharing engine.** Territories plus criteria-based rules cover the requirement.
- **No custom approval framework.** Native approvals give locking, history, and a mobile approver experience for free.
- **No custom activity logging.** Standard Tasks and Events, with Shared Activities enabled.

## The order this was built in

Sequence matters, because some decisions constrain others:

1. Fiscal year, State and Country Picklists, multi-currency — all effectively permanent
2. OWD for every object, before any data was loaded
3. Data model and relationships
4. Role hierarchy and profiles
5. Record types and sales processes
6. Products and price books, loaded in dependency order
7. Account and contact migration, with external IDs
8. Permission sets and permission set groups
9. Automation
10. Territory model, built and tested in Planning state before activation
11. Integration
12. Forecasting and reporting

Items 1 and 2 are the ones that cannot be revisited cheaply. Everything else can be adjusted.

## Section 04 in one paragraph

Sales Cloud is a set of standard objects with platform-managed behaviour: stages drive `IsClosed` and `IsWon`, line items make `Amount` read-only, conversion is a platform operation, and price books enforce a load order. The highest-consequence decisions are the stage model, the OWD, and whether to enable irreversible features like Person Accounts and territory management. Most implementation failures are adoption failures, so features that reduce friction — Path guidance, Kanban, quick actions, Dynamic Forms, automatic activity capture — matter as much as the data model. And nearly every reporting disappointment traces back to a missing prerequisite: campaign attribution needs contact roles, product revenue analysis needs line items, forecast trends need snapshots nobody built.
