# The Brain & Nervous System

*Health & Fitness · 2026-10-30*

The brain is 2% of body weight but uses about a fifth of the body's energy, and it can't store fuel or oxygen. Cut its blood supply for a few minutes and its cells start to die. That single fact explains why strokes are so devastating, why blood pressure and blood sugar matter so much for the mind, and why the habits that protect the heart also protect memory.

---

## 1. The layout

The **nervous system** has two parts:

- The **central nervous system** (CNS): the brain and spinal cord. It processes information and makes decisions.
- The **peripheral nervous system** (PNS): the nerves branching out to the rest of the body. They carry signals in (sensation) and out (movement, organ control).

Within the brain, a few regions are worth knowing:
- **Cerebrum:** the large outer brain, responsible for thinking, memory, language, voluntary movement and sensation. Its left half controls the right side of the body and vice versa, which is why a stroke on one side of the brain weakens the opposite side of the body.
- **Cerebellum:** at the back, coordinates balance and fine movement.
- **Brainstem:** connects the brain to the spinal cord and runs automatic functions: breathing, heart rate, blood pressure, swallowing, sleep and wakefulness.
- **Hypothalamus:** a small area controlling hunger, thirst, temperature and hormones via the pituitary ([[04 - Hormones - The Body's Messaging System]]).
- **Hippocampus:** essential for forming new memories; among the first areas affected in Alzheimer's disease.

## 2. How nerves send signals

Nerve cells are called **neurons**. Each has a cell body, short branches that receive signals (**dendrites**) and a long fibre that sends them (the **axon**).

- Signals travel along the axon as a brief electrical pulse.
- Many axons are wrapped in **myelin**, a fatty insulating sheath that makes signals travel much faster. Vitamin B12 is essential for making and maintaining myelin, which is why B12 deficiency can cause numbness, tingling, balance problems and memory issues ([[02 - Micronutrients & RDAs]]).
- At the end of an axon, the signal crosses a tiny gap to the next cell (a **synapse**) using chemical messengers called **neurotransmitters**:
  - **Serotonin** — mood, sleep, appetite; much of the body's serotonin is actually made in the gut ([[07 - Gut Health & Digestion]]).
  - **Dopamine** — motivation, reward, movement. Parkinson's disease involves loss of dopamine-producing neurons. Nicotine and other addictive substances hijack dopamine reward circuits ([[16 - Tobacco, Alcohol & Environmental Exposures]]).
  - **GABA** — the main calming, inhibiting signal.
  - **Glutamate** — the main exciting signal, important for learning.
  - **Acetylcholine** — muscle contraction and memory.

## 3. The autonomic nervous system: the body's autopilot

Part of the peripheral system runs organs without conscious control. It has two opposing branches:

- **Sympathetic ("fight or flight"):** raises heart rate and BP, releases adrenaline, raises blood sugar, slows digestion. Useful in danger; harmful when switched on all the time by chronic stress ([[15 - Stress, Mental Health & Social Connection]]).
- **Parasympathetic ("rest and digest"):** slows the heart, promotes digestion, repair and sleep. The **vagus nerve**, mentioned in [[01 - Human Body & Nutrition Basics]] as the main gut-brain link, is its largest nerve.

Slow breathing, especially long exhales, activates the parasympathetic side. That's the physiological basis of breathing exercises and pranayama for stress.

## 4. What the brain needs

- **A constant blood supply.** The brain gets about 15% of the heart's output. It has no reserve of oxygen and only a few minutes' tolerance for interruption.
- **Steady glucose.** The brain runs mainly on glucose, switching partly to ketones in long fasts ([[03 - Energy Metabolism - How the Body Makes and Uses Fuel]]). Low blood sugar quickly causes confusion, sweating, shakiness and, if severe, seizures or unconsciousness. People on insulin or some diabetes tablets must know these signs.
- **Sleep.** During deep sleep, the brain clears waste products through a fluid-flushing system, and memories are consolidated. Chronic short sleep impairs memory and attention and is linked to higher dementia risk ([[14 - Sleep & Circadian Rhythm]]).
- **Nutrients:** B12, folate, omega-3 fats, iron and iodine are particularly important. Iodine deficiency during pregnancy and childhood can permanently lower intelligence, which is why India mandates iodised salt.
- **Use.** The brain changes its wiring throughout life in response to learning and activity. This ability is called **neuroplasticity**. Learning new skills, social interaction and physical exercise all strengthen brain networks.

## 5. What damages the brain

### Stroke
A **stroke** is sudden loss of brain function due to interrupted blood supply:
- **Ischaemic stroke** (about 80% of strokes): a blood vessel is blocked by a clot, often from a ruptured plaque ([[19 - How Heart Disease Actually Happens]]) or a clot that travelled from the heart (for example, in atrial fibrillation).
- **Haemorrhagic stroke**: a weakened blood vessel bursts and bleeds into the brain. Long-term high blood pressure is the main cause. Haemorrhagic strokes are relatively more common in India than in many Western countries.

About 1.9 million brain cells die every minute an ischaemic stroke goes untreated. Clot-dissolving treatment works only within a few hours of symptom onset, so recognising the signs (**BE FAST**: Balance, Eyes, Face, Arm, Speech, Time) and reaching a hospital that can do a CT scan quickly is lifesaving ([[25 - Fast Killers - Emergencies & Warning Signs]]).

**High blood pressure is the single biggest modifiable cause of stroke** ([[20 - Blood Pressure, Stroke & Kidney Disease]]).

### Silent small-vessel damage
Long before any stroke, high BP, diabetes, tobacco and high cholesterol damage the brain's small blood vessels, causing tiny silent strokes and white-matter damage visible on MRI. Over years this contributes to slower thinking, poor balance, depression and **vascular dementia**.

### Dementia
**Dementia** is a decline in memory, thinking and daily functioning serious enough to interfere with independent life. The most common types are **Alzheimer's disease** and **vascular dementia**, and many people have both. Age and genes matter, but the *Lancet Commission on Dementia* (2024) estimated that a large share of dementia worldwide — around 45% — may be linked to modifiable risk factors, including:

- High blood pressure in midlife
- Diabetes
- Smoking
- Physical inactivity
- Obesity
- Excess alcohol
- Hearing loss
- Untreated vision loss
- High LDL cholesterol
- Depression
- Social isolation
- Low education in early life
- Air pollution
- Traumatic brain injury

The last items explain two under-appreciated levers: **treating hearing loss** (hearing aids) and **cataract surgery** for vision loss both appear protective, probably by keeping the brain engaged and socially connected.

### Nerve damage (neuropathy)
**Peripheral neuropathy** — damage to the nerves of the feet and hands — causes numbness, tingling, burning and loss of sensation. Common causes in India: **diabetes**, **B12 deficiency** (especially in vegetarians and long-term users of metformin or acid-suppressing tablets), alcohol, and some medicines. Numb feet can't feel cuts or blisters, which is why foot ulcers are a major risk in diabetes ([[18 - Blood Sugar, Insulin Resistance & Diabetes]]).

### Head injury
Road accidents are a leading cause of brain injury in India. Helmets on two-wheelers (for the rider and the pillion) and seatbelts in cars are among the most effective brain-protecting habits there are.

### Other important conditions
- **Parkinson's disease:** tremor, stiffness, slow movement, small handwriting, shuffling walk.
- **Epilepsy:** recurrent seizures. Very treatable with medicines; it's a medical condition, not a curse or possession.
- **Migraine:** common, often under-treated, more frequent in women.

## 6. Protecting the brain: the same levers

The striking thing about brain health is how much it overlaps with heart health:

- **Control blood pressure** — the most important single step for preventing stroke and dementia.
- **Control blood sugar.**
- **No tobacco.**
- **Move every day**; exercise increases blood flow to the brain and growth factors that support neurons. Aerobic exercise is linked to a larger hippocampus.
- **Sleep 7–9 hours.**
- **Stay socially connected and keep learning** — conversation, reading, games, new skills, community roles ([[15 - Stress, Mental Health & Social Connection]]).
- **Check and correct hearing and vision** in older adults.
- **Correct B12 and vitamin D deficiency** if found.
- **Wear helmets and seatbelts.**
- **Limit alcohol.**

## 7. Warning signs to act on

**Emergency (go to hospital immediately):** sudden face drooping, arm or leg weakness, slurred or confused speech, sudden vision loss, sudden severe headache ("worst ever"), sudden loss of balance, a seizure, sudden confusion ([[25 - Fast Killers - Emergencies & Warning Signs]]).

**See a doctor soon:** memory problems that are getting worse or affecting daily life, new numbness or tingling in the feet, tremor, frequent headaches, persistent low mood ([[15 - Stress, Mental Health & Social Connection]]).

---

## The layer, in one line

The brain is a power-hungry organ with no fuel reserve, wired by neurons that need B12, glucose, oxygen and sleep, and run partly on autopilot by the stress and rest branches of the nervous system; the same things that protect the heart — BP, sugar, no tobacco, movement, sleep and social life — also protect against stroke and dementia.
