# Blood Pressure, Stroke & Kidney Disease

*Health & Fitness · 2026-10-30*

High blood pressure is called the silent killer for a reason: it rarely causes symptoms, yet it's the leading risk factor for death worldwide, behind most strokes, many heart attacks and much kidney failure. Roughly one in four Indian adults has it, and a large share don't know. A BP machine costs less than a month of most families' tea, and it may be the single most useful health device a home can own.

---

## 1. What the numbers mean

[[05 - Blood, Circulation & the Heart]] explained the two numbers: **systolic** (pressure when the heart contracts) over **diastolic** (pressure between beats), in mmHg.

Different guidelines draw the lines slightly differently:

| Category | Systolic | | Diastolic |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Normal** | below 120 | and | below 80 |
| **Elevated / high-normal** | 120–129 (up to 139 in some guidelines) | and | below 80 (up to 89) |
| **Hypertension (US ACC/AHA 2017)** | 130 or more | or | 80 or more |
| **Hypertension (Indian and European guidelines)** | 140 or more | or | 90 or more |
| **Severe** | 180 or more | or | 110 or more |

The practical takeaway: the lower your BP within the healthy range, the lower your risk. Risk rises steadily from around 115/75 upward. Many guidelines now aim for **below 130/80** in most treated adults, if it can be reached safely.

**A single reading doesn't make a diagnosis.** BP varies with activity, stress, caffeine, a full bladder, talking and the time of day. Diagnosis needs repeated readings, ideally including home readings over several days.

## 2. Measuring BP properly at home

A validated **automatic upper-arm BP monitor** (not a wrist device) is affordable and reliable. To get accurate readings:

1. **No tea, coffee, tobacco or exercise** for 30 minutes before.
2. **Empty your bladder.**
3. **Sit quietly for 5 minutes**, back supported, feet flat on the floor, legs uncrossed.
4. **Rest the arm on a table** with the cuff at heart level, on bare skin, at the right size (a cuff that's too small reads falsely high).
5. **Don't talk** during the reading.
6. **Take 2 readings one minute apart**, in the morning before medicines and in the evening, for 7 days, and average them (ignoring the first day).

An average home BP of **135/85 or more** generally indicates hypertension. Keep a simple log to show the doctor.

**White-coat hypertension** is high BP only in the clinic, from anxiety. **Masked hypertension** is the opposite: normal in clinic, high at home or during the day. Home readings catch both.

## 3. Causes and risk factors

About 90–95% of adult hypertension is **primary** (essential), with no single cause, built up from:
- **Age** — arteries stiffen
- **Excess salt** ([[11 - The Indian Plate - Rebuilding the Thali]])
- **Excess weight**, especially belly fat
- **Physical inactivity**
- **Alcohol**
- **Tobacco** ([[16 - Tobacco, Alcohol & Environmental Exposures]])
- **Chronic stress and poor sleep**, including sleep apnoea ([[14 - Sleep & Circadian Rhythm]])
- **Diabetes and insulin resistance**
- **Family history**
- **Low potassium intake** (fruit, vegetables and pulses are rich in potassium)

**Secondary hypertension** has a specific cause, such as kidney disease, sleep apnoea, thyroid or adrenal hormone problems, or medicines (painkillers like NSAIDs, steroids, some cold remedies). It's suspected in young people, very high BP, or BP that resists treatment.

## 4. What untreated high BP does

Over years, high pressure damages the "target organs":
- **Brain:** stroke, both clot-type and bleeding-type; silent small-vessel damage and vascular dementia ([[10 - The Brain & Nervous System]]).
- **Heart:** thickened heart muscle, heart attack, **heart failure** (the heart can't pump enough blood), atrial fibrillation.
- **Kidneys:** chronic kidney disease (section 7).
- **Eyes:** damage to the retina's blood vessels.
- **Arteries:** speeds atherosclerosis everywhere, including the legs; can cause a bulge in the aorta (aneurysm).

Lowering systolic BP by 10 mmHg reduces the risk of major cardiovascular events by roughly a fifth and stroke by over a quarter, according to large analyses of treatment trials.

## 5. Bringing BP down

### Lifestyle (works for everyone; sometimes enough for mild BP)
Approximate typical reductions in systolic BP, from guideline summaries:
- **Cutting salt** towards less than 5 g a day: about 5 mmHg, more in people with high BP.
- **The DASH-style eating pattern** — lots of vegetables, fruit, pulses, low-fat dairy and whole grains, little salt and sugar: up to about 11 mmHg. The Indian plate in note 11 is close to this.
- **Losing excess weight:** roughly 1 mmHg per kg lost.
- **Regular aerobic exercise:** about 5–8 mmHg ([[13 - Cardio, Mobility & Balance]]).
- **Strength training:** a few mmHg.
- **Limiting alcohol.**
- **Stopping tobacco** — important for the arteries even though its effect on resting BP readings is smaller.
- **Treating sleep apnoea**, managing stress, slow breathing practice.

### Medicines
When BP stays high, or overall risk is high, medicines are added. The main first-line groups:
- **ACE inhibitors** (such as ramipril, enalapril) and **ARBs** (such as telmisartan, losartan): relax blood vessels by blocking the renin system; also protect the kidneys, especially in diabetes.
- **Calcium channel blockers** (such as amlodipine): relax artery walls.
- **Thiazide-type diuretics** (such as chlorthalidone, indapamide, hydrochlorothiazide): help the kidneys remove salt and water.

Often two medicines at low doses, sometimes in a single combination tablet, work better than one at a high dose. **Beta-blockers** are used mainly when there's a specific heart reason.

**Key rules:**
- **Take BP medicine every day**, even when you feel fine. High BP has no symptoms, so "feeling fine" isn't a sign it's controlled.
- **Don't stop on your own** when the readings come down. They're down *because* of the medicine.
- **Keep checking at home** and share the log at follow-up.
- **Report side effects** rather than quitting; there are many alternatives (for example, ankle swelling with amlodipine, or a dry cough with ACE inhibitors, usually means switching).
- **Avoid regular NSAID painkillers**, which raise BP and harm the kidneys.

The **India Hypertension Control Initiative** provides free BP medicines and follow-up at many government health centres.

## 6. Stroke

### Two types
- **Ischaemic stroke:** a clot blocks a brain artery, either from local atherosclerosis or a clot travelling from the heart (often due to atrial fibrillation).
- **Haemorrhagic stroke:** a weakened artery bursts and bleeds into the brain. Long-term uncontrolled BP is the main cause. These are relatively more common in India than in many Western populations.

A **TIA** (transient ischaemic attack, or "mini-stroke") is a brief episode of stroke symptoms that resolves within minutes to hours. It's a major warning: a significant share of people have a full stroke within days to weeks afterwards. Treat it as an emergency, even if symptoms have gone.

### Recognising a stroke: BE FAST
- **B**alance: sudden loss of balance or coordination
- **E**yes: sudden loss of vision in one or both eyes, or double vision
- **F**ace: one side drooping when smiling
- **A**rm: weakness or numbness of one arm (or leg); one arm drifts down when both are raised
- **S**peech: slurred, confused or unable to speak
- **T**ime: call **108 or 112** or go to a hospital with a CT scanner immediately

For ischaemic stroke, clot-dissolving medicine (**thrombolysis**) generally needs to be given within about **4.5 hours** of symptom onset, and clot removal through a catheter (**thrombectomy**) works within a somewhat longer window in selected patients. Note the **time symptoms started**, don't give food, water or tablets (swallowing may be impaired), and don't wait to "see if it gets better." Details in [[25 - Fast Killers - Emergencies & Warning Signs]].

### Preventing stroke
- **Control BP** — the most important step.
- **Detect and treat atrial fibrillation**: check the pulse for irregularity; blood thinners (anticoagulants) sharply cut stroke risk in AF.
- **Control diabetes and LDL; stop tobacco; limit alcohol; stay active.**
- After a stroke or TIA: antiplatelet or anticoagulant medicines, statins, BP control and rehabilitation (physiotherapy, speech therapy) are all essential.

## 7. Chronic kidney disease (CKD)

**Chronic kidney disease** is a long-term loss of kidney function or evidence of kidney damage (such as albumin in the urine) lasting three months or more. The two biggest causes are **diabetes** and **high BP**, and the three conditions feed each other: high BP damages the kidneys, and damaged kidneys raise BP ([[08 - The Liver & Kidneys - Factories and Filters]]).

### Stages (by eGFR, in mL/min/1.73 m²)
| Stage | eGFR | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | 90 or more | Normal filtration, but other signs of damage (such as albumin in urine) |
| G2 | 60–89 | Mildly reduced |
| G3a | 45–59 | Mild to moderately reduced |
| G3b | 30–44 | Moderately to severely reduced |
| G4 | 15–29 | Severely reduced |
| G5 | below 15 | Kidney failure; dialysis or transplant may be needed |

Doctors also stage CKD by the amount of albumin in the urine (**UACR**), because albumin leakage predicts both kidney decline and heart risk.

### It's silent until late
Most people have no symptoms until stage 4 or 5. That's why **yearly creatinine with eGFR and UACR** for everyone with diabetes or high BP is essential ([[27 - The Testing Calendar]]).

### Slowing CKD
- **Tight BP control**, often with an ACE inhibitor or ARB.
- **Blood sugar control** in diabetes; newer medicines such as **SGLT2 inhibitors** (for example, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin) slow kidney decline and protect the heart, even in some people without diabetes ([[28 - Medicines Without Myths]]).
- **Less salt**; protein in moderation as advised by the doctor.
- **Avoid NSAIDs** and unregulated herbal or "detox" products.
- **Stop tobacco.**
- **Adjust medicine doses** to kidney function; always tell doctors about kidney disease.
- **Stay hydrated**, especially in heat.

**Dialysis** (a machine or fluid exchange that filters the blood when the kidneys can't) and **transplant** are expensive and life-changing. India's Pradhan Mantri National Dialysis Programme provides free dialysis at many district hospitals for poor patients, but prevention is far better.

---

## The layer, in one line

High blood pressure is silent, common and the biggest driver of stroke, much heart disease and kidney failure; it's found only by measuring (properly, at home), lowered by salt reduction, a DASH-style plate, exercise, weight loss and daily medicines when needed, and stroke must be recognised with BE FAST and treated within hours.
