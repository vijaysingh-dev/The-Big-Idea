# Chain of Thought and Reasoning

## Why LLMs Need Explicit Reasoning

LLMs generate tokens left-to-right. Each token is conditioned only on previous tokens — there's no internal "scratchpad" or separate reasoning process.

For simple tasks (classify sentiment, translate a sentence), this is fine. For complex tasks (multi-step math, logical reasoning, plan generation), the model needs to _externalize_ its reasoning into the output sequence — otherwise it's essentially forced to "answer from the hip" in one step.

Chain of Thought addresses this: by generating intermediate reasoning steps, the model gives itself visible computation to build on.

---

## Chain of Thought (CoT)

**Definition**: Prompting the model to produce intermediate reasoning steps before giving a final answer.

### Zero-Shot CoT

Add "Let's think step by step" (or equivalent) to the prompt:

```
Q: A store has 48 apples. They sell 13 on Monday and 15 on Tuesday. 
   How many are left?
   
Without CoT: A: 20  ← often wrong for complex variants
With CoT:    A: Let me work through this step by step.
                Start: 48 apples
                Monday: 48 - 13 = 35 apples
                Tuesday: 35 - 15 = 20 apples
                Answer: 20 apples
```

The reasoning chain gives the model intermediate state to refer back to. For harder problems, this dramatically improves accuracy.

### Few-Shot CoT

Provide examples that include the reasoning chain:

```
Q: Roger has 5 tennis balls. He buys 2 more cans, each with 3 balls. How many?
A: Roger starts with 5 balls. 2 cans × 3 balls = 6 new balls. 5 + 6 = 11 balls.

Q: [new problem]
A: [model follows the reasoning pattern from the example]
```

Few-shot CoT is more reliable than zero-shot for complex tasks.

### When CoT Helps Most

- Arithmetic and mathematical reasoning
- Multi-step logical deductions
- Code debugging (explain the bug before fixing)
- Complex instruction following
- Any task requiring planning

### When CoT Hurts or Doesn't Help

- Simple factual recall ("What's the capital of France?") — adds tokens without benefit
- Tasks where intermediate reasoning can mislead (some classification tasks)
- When you need short, structured outputs — CoT adds verbose preamble
- Very high latency requirements — CoT increases output tokens significantly

## Reflection and Self-Correction

**Definition**: The model generates an initial answer, then critiques and revises it.

```
Step 1: Generate initial answer
Step 2: "Review your answer. Check for errors or missing considerations."
Step 3: Generate revised answer
```

### Does It Work?

Mixed evidence. Some research shows that reflection can improve accuracy on reasoning tasks. Other research shows that models often _confirm_ their initial answer rather than genuinely critique it — especially for factual questions where the model has a confident wrong belief.

Reflection works better when:

- The model has reasoning traces to check (not just final answers)
- There are explicit criteria to check against ("Does your answer satisfy all conditions stated in the problem?")
- The model is on the edge of confidence (not very sure or very wrong)

**Production use**: Implement as optional post-processing only if you measure a quality improvement. Don't assume reflection helps — verify with your eval set.

## Self-Consistency

**Definition**: Generate N independent answers at temperature > 0, then take a majority vote.

```python
def self_consistent_answer(prompt, n=5, temperature=0.7):
    answers = []
    for _ in range(n):
        response = llm.call(prompt, temperature=temperature)
        answers.append(extract_final_answer(response))
    
    # Return most common answer
    return Counter(answers).most_common(1)[0][0]
```

**Why it works**: Correct answers tend to be consistent across samples (there's usually one right path). Hallucinated or wrong answers tend to vary (many ways to be wrong). Majority vote filters noise.

**Cost**: N× more expensive. Use only when:

- High-stakes decisions where accuracy matters more than cost
- Task is complex enough that single-sample variance is high
- Validated on your eval set that it actually improves accuracy

## Tree of Thoughts (ToT)

**Definition**: Instead of one reasoning chain, explore a tree of reasoning paths. Evaluate each path's quality, backtrack from dead ends, and choose the best path.

```
Problem
├── Approach A
│   ├── Sub-step A1 [score: 0.8]
│   │   └── Sub-step A1a [score: 0.4] ← dead end
│   └── Sub-step A2 [score: 0.9]
│       └── Final answer [score: 0.95] ← winner
└── Approach B
    └── Sub-step B1 [score: 0.3] ← pruned
```

More powerful than CoT but much more expensive (multiple LLM calls per search step). Best for complex planning tasks. Less practical for most production use cases.

## Reasoning Models (o1, o3, Deepseek-R1)

A newer paradigm: **inference-time compute scaling**. These models are fine-tuned to produce long internal reasoning traces ("thinking tokens") before generating the visible response.

Key properties:

- Dramatically better on math, logic, and complex reasoning
- Much slower and more expensive (can use 10–100× more tokens)
- The internal reasoning chain is often hidden (o1) or visible (o3, R1)
- Not always better — for simple tasks, they're expensive overkill

**Production use**:

- Route complex reasoning tasks to reasoning models
- Route simple tasks to faster/cheaper models
- [[../10 - Production AI Engineering/10.4 - Model Routing and Multi-Model Architecture|Model routing]] becomes important when mixing these

## ReAct (Reasoning + Acting)

The bridge between CoT and agents. The model interleaves:

- **Thought**: explicit reasoning about what to do
- **Action**: calling a tool or taking a step
- **Observation**: receiving the result of the action

```
Thought: I need to find the current CEO of Apple.
Action: web_search("Apple CEO 2024")
Observation: "Tim Cook is the CEO of Apple..."
Thought: Now I have the information. I can answer.
Answer: Tim Cook is the CEO of Apple.
```

This pattern is foundational for agentic systems. Covered in [[../08 - Agents/08.1 - What Agents Actually Are]].

## Prompt Strategies Summary

|Strategy|When|Cost|
|---|---|---|
|Zero-shot CoT|Complex reasoning, limited examples|1.5–2× output tokens|
|Few-shot CoT|New task formats, reliable improvement|+example tokens|
|Reflection|After initial answer for high-stakes tasks|+50-100% tokens|
|Self-consistency|When accuracy >> cost|N× total cost|
|Tree of Thoughts|Complex planning problems|5–20× cost|
|Reasoning models|Math, hard logic, agent planning|10–100× cost|
