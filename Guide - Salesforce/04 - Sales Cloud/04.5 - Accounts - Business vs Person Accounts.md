# Accounts - Business vs Person Accounts

## Account as the centre

In business-to-business Salesforce, Account is the object everything else points at. Contacts, Opportunities, Cases, Contracts, Assets, and most custom objects reference it.

That centrality has a consequence worth stating early: **Account data quality determines the quality of everything built on it.** Duplicate accounts split a customer's history in two, break roll-ups, and make territory assignment wrong. Most CRM data quality work is really account data quality work.

## The standard fields worth knowing

`Name`, `Type`, `Industry`, `AnnualRevenue`, `NumberOfEmployees`, `Website`, `Phone`, `Ownership`, `Rating`, `Description`.

`BillingAddress` and `ShippingAddress` are **compound fields**. In the UI they appear as one; in the API and SOQL they are separate components — `BillingStreet`, `BillingCity`, `BillingState`, `BillingPostalCode`, `BillingCountry`, `BillingLatitude`, `BillingLongitude`.

```sql
SELECT Id, Name, BillingCity, BillingCountry, BillingAddress
FROM Account
WHERE BillingCountry = 'India'
```

Selecting the compound `BillingAddress` returns a structured object in Apex, which is convenient for display but cannot be filtered or sorted on directly.

**State and Country Picklists** are worth enabling in any org that operates internationally. They replace free-text state and country with validated picklists, which is the difference between reportable geography and a field containing "India", "IN", "Bharat", and "india". Enabling them later requires converting existing data, so it is a day-one decision.

`ParentId` builds account hierarchies. See [[04.7 - Account Hierarchies and Account Teams]].

## Business Accounts versus Person Accounts

The standard model assumes you sell to companies. Account is a company, Contact is a person at that company.

Some businesses sell to individuals. A consumer bank, a retailer, a healthcare provider. Modelling an individual as a company with one contact is awkward and produces reports full of accounts named after people.

**Person Accounts** solve this by making a single record behave as both Account and Contact simultaneously. Under the surface Salesforce still creates both records and links them, but they present and are managed as one.

Identifying them:

- `IsPersonAccount = true`
- Fields from Contact appear with a `__pc` suffix on Account: `PersonEmail`, `PersonMobilePhone`, `PersonBirthdate`, and custom ones like `Loyalty_Tier__pc`
- The Name is composed from `FirstName` and `LastName` rather than being a single text field

```sql
SELECT Id, Name, IsPersonAccount, PersonEmail, PersonMobilePhone
FROM Account
WHERE IsPersonAccount = true
```

## The decision, and why it is permanent

**Enabling Person Accounts cannot be undone.** Salesforce enables it through a support request, and there is no way back. This makes it one of the most consequential decisions in an implementation.

Consider it when:

- You genuinely sell to individuals rather than organizations
- Individuals are the customer, not a contact at a customer
- There is no meaningful company for most records

Avoid it when:

- The business is business-to-business with occasional individual customers — a single "Individual Customers" account with contacts underneath handles that case adequately
- You are unsure

The complications it introduces are real and worth knowing before committing:

**Code must handle both types.** An account may or may not have contacts. `Name` may or may not be writeable. Every trigger, every component, every integration needs branching.

```java
for (Account acc : accounts) {
    if (acc.IsPersonAccount) {
        emailAddress = acc.PersonEmail;
    } else {
        // business account: email lives on the related contacts
    }
}
```

**Record types are mandatory.** Person Accounts require at least one Account record type for people and one for companies.

**Field counts double.** Every person-account field exists on both Account and Contact internally, consuming the field limit faster.

**Some features behave differently.** Account hierarchies, account teams, and duplicate matching all have person-account-specific behaviour.

**Many managed packages do not fully support them.** This is a genuine practical constraint and worth checking against the packages the org uses.

**Storage cost is higher**, because each person account is two underlying records.

## Account types and segmentation

`Type` is a standard picklist commonly used for Customer, Prospect, Partner, Competitor, Reseller. It is often too blunt for real segmentation, and orgs typically add fields for tier, segment, and lifecycle stage.

A pattern worth avoiding: encoding several dimensions into one picklist — "Enterprise Customer EMEA". Segment, region, and lifecycle are three fields, and combining them makes filtering and reporting painful.

## Duplicates and merging

Accounts duplicate through the same routes as leads: inbound forms, imports, integrations, and manual creation.

**Duplicate Rules and Matching Rules** apply to Account and should be enabled. Fuzzy name matching plus website domain catches most cases.

**Account merge** combines up to three accounts. What happens:

- One is designated the **master**, and its record ID survives
- Field values can be chosen from any of the records
- Related records — contacts, opportunities, cases, activities — move to the master
- The non-master records are deleted and go to the recycle bin
- Merged record IDs are recorded, so external references can be reconciled

The two things that go wrong:

**External systems hold the dead IDs.** An ERP referencing the merged-away account now points at a deleted record. This is why an external ID field and a reconciliation process matter.

**Custom object children may not move.** Standard relationships move automatically. Whether custom lookups follow depends on the relationship type, and lookup children generally do move but this is worth verifying rather than assuming.

Merging in Apex:

```java
public with sharing class AccountMergeService {

    public static void mergeDuplicates(Id masterId, List<Id> duplicateIds) {
        Account master = [SELECT Id FROM Account WHERE Id = :masterId];
        List<Account> duplicates = [SELECT Id FROM Account WHERE Id IN :duplicateIds LIMIT 2];

        // merge is limited to two duplicates per call and fires triggers on all records involved
        Database.MergeResult[] results = Database.merge(master, duplicates, false);

        for (Database.MergeResult result : results) {
            if (!result.isSuccess()) {
                System.debug('Merge failed: ' + result.getErrors()[0].getMessage());
            }
        }
    }
}
```

`Database.merge` fires delete triggers on the losing records and update triggers on the master, which means merge-aware logic is sometimes needed. See [[10.3 - Upsert, Merge and External IDs]].

## Account ownership and its consequences

`OwnerId` on Account drives a great deal: role hierarchy visibility, territory assignment, implicit sharing to child records, and often commission.

Two operational realities:

**Transferring an account can cascade.** The transfer UI offers to move opportunities, cases, and contacts with it. Doing this in bulk triggers substantial sharing recalculation.

**Ownership skew is a genuine performance risk.** If a single user — often an integration user or a placeholder "House Accounts" user — owns hundreds of thousands of accounts, every sharing recalculation involving that user's role becomes expensive, and record locking on that owner becomes a bottleneck. See [[10.9 - Data Skew]].

The standard mitigation is to distribute ownership of unassigned accounts across several placeholder users, or to place them in a role with no subordinates so hierarchy recalculation is bounded.

## Practical guidance

**Enable State and Country Picklists on day one.** Retrofitting is painful.

**Decide on Person Accounts before go-live, and lean toward not enabling them** unless the business is genuinely consumer-facing.

**Add an external ID field for the system of record.** Usually the ERP account number. It makes integration idempotent and merges reconcilable.

**Enable duplicate rules.** Account duplicates cost more than lead duplicates because of everything attached to them.

**Watch ownership concentration.** A single owner with a very large share of accounts is a scaling problem waiting to surface.

**Keep the Account field count under control.** It is the most requested object for new fields and the one most likely to hit the field limit first.
