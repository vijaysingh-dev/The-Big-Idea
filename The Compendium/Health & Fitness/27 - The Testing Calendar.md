# The Testing Calendar

*Health & Fitness · 2026-10-30*

"Should we do a full body checkup every three months?" is a common question, and the honest answer is no. Healthy people don't need a 40-test panel every quarter; it costs money, generates false alarms and rarely changes decisions. What works is a calendar: cheap home checks often, a focused set of lab tests yearly, a few once-in-a-lifetime tests, and frequent checks only for what's actually abnormal or being treated.

---

## 1. The logic of how often to test

A test is worth repeating when its result could have **changed enough to change a decision**. That depends on:
- **How fast the thing changes.** BP and blood sugar can drift within months; Lp(a) and blood group never change; bone density changes over years.
- **Whether it's being treated.** After starting or changing a medicine, the result needs checking to adjust the dose.
- **How cheap and safe the test is.** Home BP is free once you own the cuff; a CT scan involves radiation.

The calendar below follows that logic. It's a general guide for Indian adults; a doctor should personalise it, especially for people with existing disease.

## 2. At home

| Check | Who | How often |
|---|---|---|
| **Blood pressure** | All adults 40+, and anyone with high BP, diabetes, kidney or heart disease | Monthly if normal; weekly or as advised if high or on treatment (7-day morning-and-evening logs before doctor visits) ([[20 - Blood Pressure, Stroke & Kidney Disease]]) |
| **Weight** | Everyone | Weekly or monthly, same time, same scale |
| **Waist circumference** | Everyone | Monthly; warning line for South Asians about 90 cm (men) and 80 cm (women) |
| **Pulse regularity** | Adults 65+ | At each BP check |
| **Blood glucose (glucometer)** | People with diabetes, especially on insulin or sulfonylureas | As advised by the doctor; more often when doses change or during illness |
| **Feet** | People with diabetes | Daily look for cuts, blisters, colour changes |
| **Mouth** | Tobacco and areca nut users | Monthly mirror check for patches, ulcers, lumps ([[23 - Cancer - How It Starts and How to Catch It Early]]) |
| **Breasts** | Adult women | Regular breast awareness; report changes |
| **Skin** | Everyone | Notice changing moles or non-healing spots |
| **Fitness markers** | Older adults | Every 2–3 months: 30-second chair stand, one-leg balance, walking speed ([[12 - Strength Training - Muscle as Medicine]]) |
| **Steps** | Everyone | Daily, via phone or pedometer |

## 3. Every three months (quarterly): only for what's being managed

Quarterly testing is useful when something is **abnormal or under treatment**, not as a routine for healthy people:

- **HbA1c** every 3 months for people with diabetes **not yet at target** or after a treatment change; every 6 months once stable at target.
- **Lipid profile** about **6–12 weeks after starting or changing a statin** or other cholesterol medicine, then every 6–12 months.
- **TSH** about **6–8 weeks after starting or changing thyroid medicine**, then every 6–12 months once stable.
- **Haemoglobin and ferritin** about 2–3 months after starting iron treatment.
- **Vitamin D** about 3 months after starting high-dose treatment.
- **Kidney function and potassium** a few weeks after starting or changing certain BP or diabetes medicines (ACE inhibitors, ARBs, diuretics, SGLT2 inhibitors), as the doctor advises.
- **Doctor review with home BP log** every 3 months while BP isn't controlled.
- **Recheck of a borderline abnormal value** (for example, TSH, hs-CRP) at the interval the doctor suggests.

## 4. Every year: the core panel for adults

For most Indian adults from about **age 30** (earlier with family history, belly fat, PCOS or other risk factors):

| Test | What it catches |
|---|---|
| **HbA1c + fasting glucose** | Prediabetes and diabetes |
| **Lipid profile** (ideally with ApoB) | Cholesterol and triglyceride problems |
| **Creatinine with eGFR** | Kidney function |
| **Urine albumin (UACR)** | Early kidney damage (essential with diabetes or high BP) |
| **Urine routine** | Infection, protein, sugar, blood |
| **CBC** | Anaemia, infection clues, platelet problems |
| **LFT** | Liver damage, fatty liver clues |
| **TSH** | Thyroid (every 1–3 years for most; yearly for women over 35 and anyone with symptoms or on treatment) |
| **Vitamin D and B12** | Deficiencies (B12 especially in vegetarians and people on metformin or acid suppressants; vitamin D every 1–2 years or as advised) |
| **Clinic BP, weight, waist, BMI** | Silent hypertension and metabolic risk |

**From about 40–50, add yearly:**
- **ECG** — common Indian practice for adults 40+ and anyone with risk factors; it's cheap and gives a baseline.
- **Eye examination** with eye pressure and optic nerve check (glaucoma), every 1–2 years.
- **Dental check-up** every 6–12 months (all ages).

**For people with diabetes, add yearly:**
- **Dilated retinal examination**
- **Foot examination** by a doctor (sensation and pulses)
- **UACR** (urine albumin)

## 5. Once in a lifetime (or rarely)

| Test | Why | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Lp(a)** | Genetic heart risk; doesn't change much over life | Once, in adulthood. Test first-degree relatives if high ([[19 - How Heart Disease Actually Happens]]) |
| **Hepatitis B (HBsAg) and C (anti-HCV)** | Silent liver infections | Once; repeat if at ongoing risk |
| **HIV** | Treatable when found early | At least once; more often if at risk |
| **Blood group** | Emergencies, pregnancy | Once |
| **Thalassaemia carrier status** (haemoglobin electrophoresis or HPLC) | Prevents thalassaemia major in children | Before marriage or pregnancy, especially if small red cells on CBC |
| **Abdominal aortic aneurysm ultrasound** | Hidden aortic bulge | Once, men 65–75 who have ever smoked |
| **DEXA bone scan** | Osteoporosis | Women 65+, men 70+, earlier with risk; repeat every 2–5 years depending on result ([[21 - Joints & Bones - Osteoarthritis, Osteoporosis & Falls]]) |
| **CT coronary calcium score** | Hidden calcified plaque | Selected intermediate-risk adults (usually 40–75) when the decision about a statin is unclear; repeat only after several years if zero |
| **Abdominal ultrasound** | Fatty liver, gallstones, kidney issues | Once as a baseline in middle age if there's diabetes, obesity, abnormal LFT, or in high gallbladder-cancer regions with symptoms |

## 6. Cancer screening

| Screening | Who | How often |
|---|---|---|
| **Oral examination** | Adults 30+, especially tobacco or areca nut users | Yearly (free under NP-NCD) |
| **Cervical (HPV test, Pap or VIA)** | Women 30–65 (25+ in some guidelines) | Every 5 years with HPV or VIA; every 3 years with Pap |
| **Clinical breast exam** | Women 30+ | Every 1–3 years |
| **Mammography** | Women from about 40–50 to 74 | Every 1–2 years, depending on guideline and risk |
| **Colorectal (FIT or colonoscopy)** | Adults 45/50–75 | FIT yearly or colonoscopy every 10 years |
| **Low-dose CT chest** | Adults 50–80 with heavy smoking history | Yearly, after discussion with a doctor |
| **PSA** | Men 55–69 | Shared decision with a doctor |
| **Liver ultrasound ± AFP** | People with cirrhosis or chronic hepatitis B | Every 6 months |

Details and caveats in [[23 - Cancer - How It Starts and How to Catch It Early]].

## 7. Screenings by life stage, summarised

**Children and teens:** growth, vaccinations, vision and hearing checks, haemoglobin, deworming, dental checks, BP from about age 3 as advised by the paediatrician ([[29 - Health Across Life Stages]]).

**20s and 30s:**
- BP every 1–2 years
- HbA1c, lipids, CBC, TSH (women), vitamin D/B12 every 1–3 years — yearly from 30
- Lp(a), hepatitis B/C, HIV, blood group, thalassaemia status once
- Cervical screening from 25–30 for women
- HPV and hepatitis B vaccines if not done
- Dental every 6–12 months

**40s:**
- Core annual panel (section 4)
- ECG, eye pressure check
- Home BP
- Breast exam (women); mammography discussion from 40
- Oral exam for tobacco users

**50s and 60s:**
- Everything above
- Colorectal screening from 45–50
- Mammography (women)
- Bone density for postmenopausal women with risk factors; DEXA at 65
- Shingles, flu and pneumococcal vaccines
- Hearing check from 60
- PSA discussion (men 55–69)

**70+:**
- Continue core tests and vaccines
- Medicine review at least yearly (fewer pills, fewer side effects)
- Falls, memory, hearing and vision checks
- Cancer screening decisions individualised to health and life expectancy

## 8. Choosing a lab and package

- **Government options:** NP-NCD screening for BP, diabetes and oral, breast and cervical cancer is free at Ayushman Arogya Mandirs (health and wellness centres) and many government hospitals for adults 30+.
- **Accredited labs:** prefer **NABL-accredited** labs (National Accreditation Board for Testing and Calibration Laboratories).
- **Same lab over time** makes trends easier to compare.
- **Packages:** a basic annual package covering the core panel is usually enough. "Premium" packages with dozens of vitamins, tumour markers and heavy metals add cost and false alarms with little benefit for most people ([[26 - Reading a Blood Report]]).
- **Preparation:** 10–12 hours fasting for glucose and lipids (water allowed); take usual medicines unless told otherwise; avoid heavy exercise and alcohol the day before; stop biotin supplements a couple of days before hormone tests.

## 9. A sample family calendar

**Two parents in their late 50s, one with diabetes and high TSH, one with high Lp(a), both using tobacco:**

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **Weekly** | Home BP for both (log); glucometer as advised for the parent with diabetes; foot check |
| **Monthly** | Weight and waist; mouth self-check (tobacco users) |
| **Every 3 months** | HbA1c for the diabetic parent until at target; doctor review with BP logs; LDL 6–12 weeks after any statin change; TSH 6–8 weeks after any thyroid change |
| **Every 6 months** | Dental check; HbA1c once stable |
| **Yearly** | Core panel for both (HbA1c, fasting glucose, lipids ± ApoB, creatinine/eGFR, UACR, CBC, LFT, TSH, vitamin D and B12); ECG; eye exam (dilated retina for diabetes); oral cancer exam; flu vaccine |
| **Every 2–5 years** | Cervical screening (mother); mammography (mother, 1–2 yearly); DEXA as advised |
| **Once** | Lp(a) for the children and siblings of the parent with high Lp(a); hepatitis B/C for both; pneumococcal and shingles vaccines; colorectal screening plan |

---

## The layer, in one line

Test often at home (BP, weight, waist, feet), test quarterly only what's abnormal or being treated, run a focused core panel yearly from about 30, do a handful of tests once in a lifetime (Lp(a), hepatitis B and C, thalassaemia, blood group), and follow age-based cancer, bone and eye screening — rather than repeating a 40-test panel every few months.
