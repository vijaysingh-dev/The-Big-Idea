# Path, Kanban and Sales Productivity

## Why these matter more than they look

Every feature in this note exists to reduce the friction of using the CRM. That sounds cosmetic and is not: the most common cause of a failed Sales Cloud implementation is reps not entering data, and the most common cause of that is the system being tedious.

A developer who understands this pushes back on requirements that add friction and reaches for these features before building custom UI.

## Path

Path renders the stage progression as a horizontal bar across the top of a record, with the current stage highlighted. Clicking a stage advances the record.

Its real value is what sits underneath each stage:

**Key fields.** Up to five fields per stage, editable inline. The fields relevant to closing *this* stage, not the full layout.

**Guidance for success.** Rich text per stage, describing what needs to happen. This is where the exit criteria from [[04.8 - Opportunities and Sales Stages]] should live — not in a training document nobody opens, but on the record at the moment the rep is working it.

**Celebrations.** Configurable confetti on reaching a stage. Easy to dismiss as frivolous; sales organizations genuinely respond to it.

Path is available on Opportunity, Lead, Case, Quote, Contract, Campaign, and custom objects with a picklist to drive it. Configuration is per record type, so different sales processes get different paths.

The practical impact: putting the three fields required to advance directly on the path removes the need to scroll a long layout. Reps fill them in because they are in front of them.

## Kanban

A list view rendered as columns, one per picklist value, with records as draggable cards. Dragging a card changes the field.

For a sales pipeline this is the view most reps prefer, because it matches how they think about the pipeline. Column headers show the sum of a numeric field — usually Amount — so pipeline value per stage is visible without a report.

Two features worth knowing:

**Alerts** can be configured to flag records needing attention — no activity in a set period, past close date — showing a warning on the card.

**Dragging a card runs the full save pipeline.** Validation rules fire, triggers fire, and a validation failure produces an error message on the card. A validation rule that requires fields the rep has not filled in makes the Kanban view unusable, which is worth thinking about when designing stage validation.

## Activity capture and logging

Activities — Tasks and Events — are how customer interactions are recorded, and manual logging is the single biggest data-entry burden on reps.

**Einstein Activity Capture** connects a user's email and calendar and logs interactions automatically, matching them to Contacts, Leads, and Opportunities. It reduces manual work substantially.

The caveats are important and frequently discovered late:

- Captured activities are stored partly outside the standard Activity objects, in a separate store
- That means **standard reporting on captured activities is limited**, and they are not fully available to SOQL
- Data retention is limited by license tier
- Deleting the connection can remove the captured history

So a business that wants "log everything automatically" and "report on activity volume by rep" may find those two requirements in tension. Establishing which matters more, early, avoids a difficult conversation later.

The alternative is Salesforce Inbox or an email-integration product that writes standard Task records, which are fully reportable at the cost of more explicit user action.

## Tasks, Events and the activity model

Both use the two-part relationship pattern:

- `WhoId` — a Contact or a Lead
- `WhatId` — an Account, Opportunity, Case, or custom object

Both are **polymorphic**, so Apex cannot assume a type.

```sql
SELECT Id, Subject, ActivityDate, Status, Priority,
       Who.Name, What.Name,
       TYPEOF What WHEN Opportunity THEN Amount, StageName WHEN Account THEN Industry END
FROM Task
WHERE WhatId IN :opportunityIds AND IsClosed = false
ORDER BY ActivityDate
```

`LastActivityDate` on Account, Contact, Opportunity, and Lead is maintained by the platform from related activities. It is the field most "stale record" reports are built on:

```sql
SELECT Id, Name, Amount, CloseDate, Owner.Name, LastActivityDate
FROM Opportunity
WHERE IsClosed = false
  AND (LastActivityDate < LAST_N_DAYS:30 OR LastActivityDate = null)
ORDER BY Amount DESC
```

A deal worth two crore with no activity in six weeks is the most actionable output a pipeline report can produce.

**Shared Activities** allows a Task or Event to relate to up to ten contacts rather than one, through the `TaskRelation` and `EventRelation` objects. Enabling it is one-way and worth doing in any business-to-business org, since meetings genuinely involve several people.

## List views and productivity

Underrated, and often the right answer instead of building something.

**List view filters** with `MINE`, `MY_TEAMS`, and role-based scoping. **Inline editing** directly in the list. **Mass actions** on selected rows. **List view charts**. **Filter by related record fields**, which removes the need for many custom reports.

Before building a custom component that shows a filtered set of records, check whether a list view does it. It costs nothing to maintain and works on mobile.

## Quick actions

A quick action creates a record, updates a record, sends an email, or launches a flow, from a button on a record page — without navigating away.

For developers this is the correct replacement for the JavaScript buttons that Lightning does not support ([[01.9 - Classic vs Lightning Experience]]). The options:

**Create a record action** with prefilled fields from the parent.
**Update a record action** exposing a small set of fields.
**Flow action**, for anything requiring logic or several steps.
**Lightning Web Component action**, for full control.

```xml
<!-- LWC exposed as a quick action on Opportunity -->
<targets>
    <target>lightning__RecordAction</target>
</targets>
<targetConfigs>
    <targetConfig targets="lightning__RecordAction">
        <actionType>ScreenAction</actionType>
    </targetConfig>
</targetConfigs>
```

`ScreenAction` opens the component in a modal. The alternative, `Action`, runs headlessly with no UI, which is right for a one-click operation. See [[14.19 - Deploying and Exposing Components]].

## Dynamic Forms and Dynamic Actions

**Dynamic Forms** place individual fields on a Lightning record page with visibility rules, instead of a fixed page layout block. A section appears only when the stage is past a certain point, or only for a particular record type.

**Dynamic Actions** apply the same idea to buttons: the "Submit for Approval" action appears only when a discount threshold is exceeded.

Together they remove much of the historical reason for creating extra record types purely to vary layout. Where available, they are usually the better tool, and they reduce the record type proliferation described in [[02.11 - Record Types and Page Layouts]].

## Sales Engagement and cadences

Sales Engagement (formerly High Velocity Sales) adds structured outreach sequences — a cadence of calls, emails, and tasks over a defined schedule — with a work queue that tells reps what to do next.

Relevant mostly to inside sales and high-volume prospecting. For a complex enterprise sale with a nine-month cycle, cadences add little. Knowing when it does and does not apply is the useful part.

## Practical guidance

**Use Path guidance to carry exit criteria.** It is the only place reps will actually read them.

**Design stage validation with Kanban in mind.** Rules that block a drag make the most-used view frustrating.

**Decide the Einstein Activity Capture tradeoff explicitly.** Automatic capture versus full reportability is a real choice.

**Check whether a list view solves it** before building a component.

**Reach for quick actions and Dynamic Forms** before custom UI. Less code, less maintenance, works on mobile automatically.

**Report on `LastActivityDate`.** It is the cheapest high-signal pipeline metric available.
