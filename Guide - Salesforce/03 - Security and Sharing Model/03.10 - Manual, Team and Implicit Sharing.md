# Manual, Team and Implicit Sharing

## Three mechanisms that surprise people

Sharing rules and the role hierarchy are configured deliberately. The three mechanisms in this note grant access in ways that are easy to overlook — one because a user did it, one because a feature was enabled, and one because the platform does it silently.

Between them they account for most "why can this person see this record" questions where the configured rules do not explain the answer.

## Manual sharing

A user with access to a record can share it with a specific person or group using the Sharing button, choosing Read Only or Read/Write.

Who can do it: the record owner, anyone above the owner in the role hierarchy, and administrators.

Where it fits: the genuine one-off. A rep hands a deal to a colleague for a week while on leave. A coordinator brings in a specialist on one claim.

Two behaviours that matter:

**Manual shares are deleted when ownership changes.** Transfer the record and every manual share on it disappears. This is deliberate, and it regularly surprises people who assumed the access was permanent.

**Manual sharing only exists on objects with restrictive OWD.** If the object is Public Read/Write there is nothing to grant, and the button does not appear.

Manual sharing is not a design tool. If the same manual share is being created repeatedly, that is a sharing rule waiting to be written. Orgs where manual sharing is the primary access mechanism are unauditable — nobody can answer who has access to what.

## Account and Opportunity Teams

Teams let a record have a defined set of collaborators with specified access, separate from ownership.

**Account Teams.** Users added to an account with a role — Account Manager, Executive Sponsor, Technical Lead — and an access level for the Account and, optionally, for its Opportunities, Cases, and Contacts.

**Opportunity Teams.** The same idea per opportunity, with roles like Sales Rep, Sales Engineer, Channel Manager. Related but distinct from **Opportunity Splits**, which allocate revenue credit rather than access. See [[04.9 - Opportunity Teams and Opportunity Splits]].

**Case Teams.** Users or contacts attached to a case with a defined role, used heavily in Service Cloud for escalations and multi-specialist cases.

Teams are the right tool when a record genuinely has multiple owners in practice. Enterprise selling is the classic case: a deal involves a rep, a solution architect, a legal reviewer, and an executive sponsor, and all four need access to that one deal without needing access to everything else the rep owns.

**Default teams** are the feature that makes this practical. A user can define a default team that is added automatically to every account or opportunity they own, so a sales engineer who always works with a particular rep gets access without manual effort.

Teams produce share rows with `RowCause = 'Team'`, so they are visible in the same diagnostic queries as everything else.

```sql
SELECT AccountId, UserId, TeamMemberRole, AccountAccessLevel, OpportunityAccessLevel, CaseAccessLevel
FROM AccountTeamMember
WHERE AccountId = :accountId
```

## Implicit sharing

This is the one that causes the most confusion, because it is not configured anywhere and does not appear in any sharing rules list. The platform grants it automatically to make the standard CRM function.

**Parent implicit sharing.** A user with access to a child record gets **Read access to the parent Account**. If you can see an Opportunity, you can read its Account, even if Account OWD would otherwise hide it. Without this, the standard user interface would be broken — an opportunity page cannot render without its account name.

**Child implicit sharing.** A user with access to an Account gets access to its Contacts, Opportunities, and Cases, at the level configured in the Account Team or role settings. The exact behaviour depends on the child object's OWD and the account's configuration.

**Portal implicit sharing.** Experience Cloud users get access to records related to their own Account or Contact, according to sharing sets.

**High-volume portal user sharing** works differently again, using sharing sets rather than the normal model, because maintaining share rows for a million external users is not feasible.

The practical effect: **Account OWD being Private does not mean a user cannot see an account.** If they can see any opportunity or case on it, they can read the account. This is correct behaviour and a frequent source of "the sharing model is not working" reports that are actually working exactly as designed.

Implicit shares appear with `RowCause` values like `ImplicitChild` and `ImplicitParent`:

```sql
SELECT AccountId, UserOrGroupId, AccountAccessLevel, OpportunityAccessLevel, RowCause
FROM AccountShare
WHERE AccountId = :accountId
```

## Ownership itself

Worth stating explicitly because it is easy to skip: **the owner always has full access**, including delete and transfer, provided they have the corresponding object permissions.

Changing ownership therefore changes access, and it deletes manual shares. On objects with many child records, a transfer can also cascade — transferring an Account can optionally transfer its Opportunities and Cases, which is a configurable behaviour and a common source of bulk sharing recalculation.

## Putting the mechanisms in order

When debugging, the mechanisms that can grant access to a record, roughly in the order worth checking:

1. Ownership
2. Role hierarchy above the owner
3. Sharing rules, owner-based and criteria-based
4. Team membership
5. Manual shares
6. Implicit sharing from a parent or child
7. Apex managed sharing
8. View All / Modify All on the object
9. View All Data / Modify All Data at org level

The share object's `RowCause` tells you which of 1 through 7 applied. Items 8 and 9 do not create share rows at all, which is why a user can have access that appears nowhere in the share table — a genuinely confusing situation until you know to check permissions as well as shares.

```sql
SELECT ParentId, UserOrGroupId, AccessLevel, RowCause
FROM Warranty_Claim__Share
WHERE ParentId = :recordId
```

If that query returns nothing for a user who clearly has access, check View All and Modify All next.

## Design guidance

**Do not build a model on manual sharing.** It is unauditable and it evaporates on ownership change.

**Use teams when collaboration is structural, not occasional.** A deal that always involves four roles is a team. A deal that occasionally needs one extra person is a manual share.

**Expect implicit sharing and design around it.** Assuming Account can be hidden from someone who can see its opportunities will not work.

**Be careful with team access levels.** An Account Team member with Read/Write on Opportunities gets that on *every* opportunity for that account, including ones they were not involved in.

**Remember teams have a cost.** Every team member on every record is a share row. Account teams across a hundred thousand accounts is a large share table and a slow recalculation.
