# Cancer: How It Starts and How to Catch It Early

*Health & Fitness · 2026-10-30*

In India, cancer is often diagnosed late — a lump ignored for months, a mouth ulcer treated with home remedies, bleeding blamed on piles. That's the main reason survival rates are lower than they need to be. Many common cancers are either largely preventable or curable when caught early, and early detection is usually cheap: a look inside the mouth, a hand on the breast, a simple cervical test, or paying attention to a few warning signs.

---

## 1. What cancer is

Cells constantly divide to replace old ones, following tight instructions written in their DNA. Each division risks small copying errors (**mutations**), and DNA can also be damaged by tobacco chemicals, alcohol, radiation, some infections and chronic inflammation ([[06 - The Immune System & Inflammation]]). The body repairs most damage and destroys most faulty cells.

**Cancer** begins when a cell accumulates enough mutations to:
- Divide without stopping
- Ignore signals to die
- Avoid the immune system
- Grow its own blood supply
- Invade nearby tissue and spread (**metastasise**) through the blood or lymph system to other organs

A **tumour** is a lump of abnormal cells. **Benign** tumours grow but don't invade or spread. **Malignant** tumours (cancers) invade and can spread.

Because it takes many mutations, most cancers develop over **years or decades**, often through visible **pre-cancerous** stages: a white patch in the mouth, abnormal cells on the cervix, a polyp in the bowel. These pre-cancer stages are the best moment to catch it.

### Staging
Cancers are staged roughly from **1** (small, confined to where it started) to **4** (spread to distant organs). Survival is dramatically better at stage 1–2 than at stage 4 for nearly every cancer. That single fact is the whole case for early detection.

## 2. The cancers that matter most in India

According to India's National Cancer Registry Programme (ICMR-NCDIR), the most common cancers are:
- **In men:** mouth/lip, lung, oesophagus, stomach, and others including colorectal and prostate.
- **In women:** breast, cervix, ovary, and others including mouth and gallbladder.

**Regional patterns matter:**
- **Mouth cancer** is driven by tobacco and areca nut use ([[16 - Tobacco, Alcohol & Environmental Exposures]]).
- **Gallbladder cancer** is unusually common in the **Gangetic belt** (Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, Assam), especially in women. Gallstones are a known risk factor; possible contributors under study include contaminated water and heavy metals.
- **Stomach and oesophageal cancers** are more common in the northeast.
- **Lung cancer** is rising, including in non-smokers exposed to air pollution and chulha smoke.

## 3. What causes cancer, and what can be changed

A large share of cancers worldwide are linked to modifiable factors. In India the biggest are:

1. **Tobacco in any form** — the single largest preventable cause: mouth, throat, oesophagus, lung, stomach, pancreas, bladder, kidney, cervix and more.
2. **Areca nut (supari)**, with or without tobacco.
3. **Infections:**
   - **HPV** (human papillomavirus) → cervical cancer, some mouth, throat and anal cancers. **Preventable by vaccine** ([[17 - Infections, Hygiene & Vaccines]]).
   - **Hepatitis B and C** → liver cancer. Hepatitis B is vaccine-preventable; C is curable.
   - ***H. pylori*** → stomach cancer ([[07 - Gut Health & Digestion]]).
4. **Alcohol** → mouth, throat, oesophagus, liver, bowel, breast.
5. **Obesity, especially belly fat, and inactivity** → breast (after menopause), bowel, womb, kidney, liver, pancreas and others.
6. **Diet:** low fibre; lots of processed and red meat; very hot drinks (linked with oesophageal cancer); aflatoxin-contaminated grains and peanuts (liver).
7. **Air pollution and household smoke** → lung.
8. **Occupational and environmental exposures:** asbestos, some pesticides and chemicals, arsenic in groundwater ([[16 - Tobacco, Alcohol & Environmental Exposures]]).
9. **Excess sun** → skin cancer (less common in darker skin, but still occurs).

**Factors that can't be changed:** age (most cancers are more common after 50), family history and inherited genes. A strong family history — several close relatives with the same or related cancers, especially at young ages (for example, breast and ovarian cancer, or bowel and womb cancer) — is worth discussing with a doctor, as earlier or more intensive screening and genetic testing may be appropriate.

## 4. Warning signs worth acting on

Most of these have harmless explanations. But any of them lasting **more than 2–3 weeks**, or getting worse, deserves a doctor's visit — not home remedies, not waiting:

- **A lump** anywhere: breast, armpit, neck, testicle, abdomen
- **A mouth ulcer, white or red patch** that doesn't heal in 2 weeks, or difficulty opening the mouth
- **Unexplained weight loss** (several kilos without trying)
- **Persistent cough or hoarseness**, or coughing blood
- **Difficulty swallowing**, or food sticking
- **Change in bowel habit** for more than a few weeks, **blood in stool** or black stools
- **Blood in urine**
- **Abnormal vaginal bleeding:** between periods, after intercourse, or **any bleeding after menopause**
- **Breast changes:** a lump, skin dimpling, nipple turning inwards, nipple discharge (especially bloody), redness or thickening
- **A mole or skin spot** that changes in size, shape or colour, bleeds or itches
- **Persistent indigestion or upper abdominal pain**, especially after 45
- **Unexplained anaemia** in men and post-menopausal women ([[22 - Thyroid, Anaemia & Common Deficiencies]])
- **Yellowing of eyes or skin** (jaundice), especially without pain — can be a sign of gallbladder or pancreatic cancer
- **Persistent tiredness, fevers or night sweats** without a clear cause
- **Bone pain** that doesn't settle

## 5. Screening: finding cancer before symptoms

**Screening** means testing healthy people with no symptoms to find cancer or pre-cancer early. Good screening tests exist for only some cancers. India's **National Programme for Prevention and Control of Non-Communicable Diseases (NP-NCD)** offers free population-based screening for adults 30+ for **oral, breast and cervical cancer** (along with BP and diabetes) at government health and wellness centres (Ayushman Arogya Mandirs).

### Recommended screenings
| Cancer | Who | Test | How often |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Oral** | Adults 30+, especially tobacco or areca nut users | Visual examination of the mouth by a doctor, dentist or trained health worker; self-checks in a mirror | Every visit / at least yearly for users |
| **Cervical** | Women 30–65 (25+ in some guidelines) | **HPV test** (preferred where available), **Pap smear**, or **VIA** (visual inspection with acetic acid, used in government programmes) | Every 5 years with HPV or VIA; every 3 years with Pap alone |
| **Breast** | Women 30+ for clinical breast examination; women 40–50+ for mammography | **Clinical breast exam** by a trained health worker; **mammography** (a low-dose breast X-ray) | Clinical exam every 1–3 years; mammography every 1–2 years from about 40–50 to 74, depending on guideline and risk |
| **Colorectal** (bowel) | Adults 45/50–75 | **FIT** (faecal immunochemical test, detects hidden blood in stool) yearly, or **colonoscopy** every 10 years | As per test |
| **Lung** | Adults 50–80 with heavy smoking history (around 20 pack-years) who smoke now or quit within 15 years | **Low-dose CT scan** | Yearly; discuss with a doctor |
| **Prostate** | Men 55–69 | **PSA blood test**, as a shared decision with a doctor, because it can find slow cancers that would never cause harm and lead to over-treatment | Individual decision |
| **Liver** | People with cirrhosis or chronic hepatitis B | Ultrasound, often with AFP blood test | Every 6 months |

Breast self-awareness — knowing how your breasts normally look and feel and reporting changes promptly — is valuable at all ages.

### What tumour markers can't do
Blood tests called **tumour markers** — such as **CEA**, **CA-125**, **CA 19-9**, **AFP** and **PSA** — are often included in "full body checkup" packages. Except for PSA (with caveats) and AFP in high-risk liver patients, **they are not good screening tests for healthy people**:
- They can be raised by non-cancer causes (smoking, inflammation, liver disease, infection, benign conditions).
- They're often normal in early cancer.

Their real use is **monitoring known cancers** during and after treatment. A slightly raised CEA in a smoker, for example, isn't a cancer diagnosis; a normal CEA doesn't rule cancer out. Discuss abnormal values with a doctor rather than panicking or ignoring them.

### Whole-body scans
Full-body CT or MRI "screening" in people without symptoms isn't recommended. It exposes people to radiation (CT), finds many harmless incidental findings that lead to anxiety and unnecessary procedures, and hasn't been shown to save lives.

## 6. Prevention: the highest-value steps

1. **Quit tobacco and areca nut completely** ([[16 - Tobacco, Alcohol & Environmental Exposures]]).
2. **HPV vaccination** for girls (and ideally boys) aged 9–14.
3. **Hepatitis B vaccination**; test for hepatitis B and C once.
4. **Limit or avoid alcohol.**
5. **Keep a healthy waist**, stay active ([[12 - Strength Training - Muscle as Medicine]], [[13 - Cardio, Mobility & Balance]]).
6. **High-fibre, plant-rich diet**; limit processed meat; avoid very hot drinks; store grains dry ([[11 - The Indian Plate - Rebuilding the Thali]]).
7. **Clean cooking fuel** instead of chulha smoke.
8. **Treat *H. pylori*** if found.
9. **Protective equipment** for pesticides and industrial chemicals.
10. **Take part in screening** and act on warning signs.

## 7. If cancer is diagnosed

- **Get treatment at a recognised cancer centre** where possible. Treatment usually combines surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, targeted therapy or immunotherapy, planned by a team (a **tumour board**).
- **Avoid delays and unproven "alternative cures,"** which cost precious time while early-stage cancer progresses. Complementary practices like yoga and good nutrition can support standard treatment, not replace it.
- **Financial support:** Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY, state health schemes, the Health Minister's Discretionary Grant and charitable trusts at major cancer hospitals cover many treatments ([[30 - Health Insurance & Money for Health]]).
- **Palliative care** — specialist care focused on relieving pain and symptoms at any stage — greatly improves quality of life and is available at many centres.

---

## The layer, in one line

Cancer is the end result of many cell mutations over years, often through visible pre-cancer stages; in India most of the burden comes from tobacco, areca nut, infections like HPV and hepatitis, alcohol, obesity and smoke, and the biggest wins are prevention, a few cheap screenings (mouth, cervix, breast, later bowel) and acting on warning signs within weeks, not months — while tumour markers and full-body scans are poor screening tools.
