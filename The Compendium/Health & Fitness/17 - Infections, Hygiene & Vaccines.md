# Infections, Hygiene & Vaccines

*Health & Fitness · 2026-10-30*

India is going through a double burden: chronic diseases like diabetes and heart disease are rising fast, while old infections — tuberculosis, dengue, typhoid, rabies — haven't gone away. Infections are also much more dangerous in people with diabetes, in older adults and in children. A family health system that ignores them is missing half the picture.

---

## 1. How infections spread

Germs — bacteria, viruses, parasites and fungi — travel by a handful of routes. Knowing the route tells you how to block it:

| Route | Examples | Main protection |
|---|---|---|
| **Air and droplets** | TB, flu, COVID, measles, common cold | Ventilation, covering coughs, masks when sick, vaccines |
| **Food and water** | Typhoid, cholera, hepatitis A and E, diarrhoeal infections, worms | Safe water, handwashing, food hygiene |
| **Mosquitoes** | Dengue, malaria, chikungunya, Japanese encephalitis | Removing standing water, nets, repellents, covering skin |
| **Blood and body fluids** | Hepatitis B and C, HIV | Sterile needles, safe blood, safe sex, vaccine for hepatitis B |
| **Animals** | Rabies, brucellosis, leptospirosis, scrub typhus | Vaccination, boiled milk, footwear, protective clothing |
| **Soil and wounds** | Tetanus, hookworm | Footwear, wound cleaning, tetanus vaccine |

## 2. The infections that matter most in India

### Tuberculosis (TB)
India has the world's largest number of TB cases. TB usually affects the lungs and spreads through the air when an infected person coughs.

**Suspect TB with:**
- A cough lasting **more than two weeks**
- Fever, especially in the evenings
- Night sweats
- Weight loss and loss of appetite
- Coughing blood

People with **diabetes**, malnutrition, HIV, tobacco use, or living with someone who has TB are at higher risk. Diagnosis and treatment are **free** at government health facilities under the National TB Elimination Programme, including the Ni-kshay Poshan Yojana nutritional support for patients. TB is curable, but the full course (usually six months or more) must be completed; stopping early causes drug-resistant TB.

### Mosquito-borne diseases
- **Dengue:** sudden high fever, severe body and joint pain, headache, pain behind the eyes, rash. Most people recover, but some develop severe dengue with leaking blood vessels and bleeding, usually around days 3–7 as the fever comes down. Warning signs are in [[25 - Fast Killers - Emergencies & Warning Signs]]. There's no specific antiviral; treatment is fluids and close monitoring. **Avoid aspirin and NSAID painkillers** (ibuprofen, diclofenac) in suspected dengue because they raise bleeding risk; paracetamol is the usual choice.
- **Malaria:** fever with chills and shivering in cycles. Rapid tests and treatment are available free at government facilities. *Falciparum* malaria can become severe quickly.
- **Chikungunya:** fever with severe joint pain that can last weeks or months.
- **Japanese encephalitis:** brain infection in parts of UP, Bihar, Assam and elsewhere; included in childhood vaccination in endemic districts.

**Prevention:** the dengue mosquito (*Aedes*) breeds in **clean standing water** around the house — coolers, buckets, flower pots, tyres, water tanks, plant saucers — and bites mainly in the daytime. Empty, scrub and cover containers at least weekly; use mosquito nets, window screens and repellents; wear full sleeves.

### Typhoid and gut infections
Spread through contaminated food and water ([[07 - Gut Health & Digestion]]). Typhoid causes prolonged fever, headache, abdominal pain and weakness. Increasing antibiotic resistance makes prevention important: safe water, handwashing and the **typhoid conjugate vaccine**.

### Hepatitis
- **Hepatitis A and E** (food and water): acute jaundice. Hepatitis E can be dangerous in pregnancy.
- **Hepatitis B and C** (blood and body fluids): often silent for years, then cirrhosis and liver cancer ([[08 - The Liver & Kidneys - Factories and Filters]]). Everyone should be tested at least once. Hepatitis B is preventable by vaccine; hepatitis C is curable with tablets.

### Infections from animals and farming
Especially relevant to farming families:
- **Rabies:** almost 100% fatal once symptoms start, but **fully preventable** after exposure. For any dog, cat, monkey or other animal bite or scratch: wash the wound immediately with soap and running water for **15 minutes**, then go to a health facility the same day for **post-exposure rabies vaccination** (and immunoglobulin for severe bites). Free at government hospitals. Never apply chilli, turmeric, oil or other home remedies to the wound. Pet dogs should be vaccinated yearly.
- **Brucellosis:** a long-lasting fever with joint pain caught from infected cattle, goats and sheep, mainly through **raw milk** and handling birthing animals. **Boil milk** before drinking or making curd and paneer, wear gloves when handling animal births or abortions, and vaccinate livestock where the veterinary programme offers it.
- **Leptospirosis:** spread through water or soil contaminated with animal urine (rats, cattle), especially during monsoon flooding and in paddy fields. Fever, muscle pain (especially calves), red eyes; can damage the kidneys and liver. Wear boots and gloves in floodwater and fields; see a doctor promptly for fever after wading in water.
- **Scrub typhus:** spread by mite bites in grassy and bushy areas; fever with a small black scab (eschar) where the mite bit. Easily treated if recognised early.
- **Tetanus:** bacteria in soil and manure enter wounds and cause painful muscle spasms that can be fatal. Fully preventable by vaccine.
- **Worms:** see [[07 - Gut Health & Digestion]].

### Respiratory infections
- **Influenza (flu)** and **pneumonia** cause serious illness and death in older adults and people with diabetes, heart, lung or kidney disease. Vaccines exist for both.
- **COVID-19** remains a risk for the elderly and people with chronic conditions.

### Other common infections
- **Urinary tract infections (UTIs):** burning, frequent urination, fever. More common in women and people with diabetes. Older adults may show only confusion or weakness.
- **Skin and foot infections**, especially in diabetes ([[18 - Blood Sugar, Insulin Resistance & Diabetes]]).
- **Sexually transmitted infections**, including HIV, syphilis and HPV.

## 3. Why diabetes and age make infections worse

High blood sugar weakens white blood cells and slows wound healing ([[06 - The Immune System & Inflammation]]). People with diabetes get more TB, UTIs, foot and skin infections, fungal infections and severe flu and COVID. Older adults have weaker immune responses and often show fewer warning signs — an older person with pneumonia may not have high fever, only confusion, weakness or loss of appetite. Any sudden change in an older adult deserves medical attention.

## 4. Antibiotics and resistance

**Antimicrobial resistance (AMR)** — germs evolving so that medicines no longer work — is one of the most serious health threats in India, which has some of the highest antibiotic use in the world.

- **Antibiotics don't work against viruses** — colds, most sore throats, flu, most diarrhoea, dengue.
- **Don't take antibiotics without a prescription**, don't pressure doctors for them, and don't share leftovers.
- **Complete the prescribed course** and take it at the right times.
- Many antibiotics are used in livestock; using them only under veterinary guidance helps slow resistance.

## 5. Hygiene basics that prevent the most illness

- **Handwashing with soap** for about 20 seconds: before cooking and eating, after the toilet, after handling animals, dung, soil or pesticides, and after caring for sick people.
- **Safe drinking water:** boil (a rolling boil for at least one minute), use a reliable filter, or use treated piped water.
- **Food safety:** wash raw produce, cook meat and eggs thoroughly, keep cooked food covered, refrigerate leftovers, and avoid food that has been sitting out for hours in the heat.
- **Boil milk**, especially raw milk from animals.
- **Sanitation:** use toilets; keep the area around the house clean and free of standing water.
- **Footwear outdoors**, especially in fields.
- **Cough and sneeze into a tissue or elbow**; wear a mask when sick, and in crowded places during outbreaks.
- **Ventilation:** open windows; TB and other airborne infections spread most in closed, crowded rooms.
- **Dental hygiene:** brush twice daily ([[06 - The Immune System & Inflammation]]).

## 6. Vaccines for every age

Vaccines are among the most cost-effective health measures available. India's **Universal Immunisation Programme** (UIP) provides free vaccines for children and pregnant women at government facilities, tracked through the **U-WIN** digital platform.

### Children
Follow the UIP schedule and the Indian Academy of Pediatrics schedule, which includes BCG, polio, hepatitis B, pentavalent (diphtheria, pertussis, tetanus, hepatitis B, Hib), rotavirus, pneumococcal, measles-rubella, Japanese encephalitis (in endemic areas), and boosters. Optional vaccines worth discussing with the paediatrician: typhoid conjugate, hepatitis A, chickenpox and influenza.

### HPV vaccine
The **HPV vaccine** protects against the virus that causes most **cervical cancer** — one of the most common cancers in Indian women — and some other cancers. It works best given to **girls aged 9–14**, before any exposure; it can also be given to boys and young adults. India has developed an indigenous vaccine (Cervavac) and has moved to include HPV vaccination in its national programme. Check with the local health centre for current availability ([[23 - Cancer - How It Starts and How to Catch It Early]]).

### Adults
Commonly recommended adult vaccines (discuss with a doctor, as needs vary by age and health):

| Vaccine | Who | How often |
|---|---|---|
| **Tetanus–diphtheria (Td)** | All adults | A booster every 10 years, and after a dirty wound if more than 5 years since the last dose |
| **Influenza (flu)** | Adults over 50 (or 65), and anyone with diabetes, heart, lung or kidney disease; pregnant women | Every year, ideally before the flu season |
| **Pneumococcal** | Adults 65+, and younger adults with diabetes, heart, lung, kidney or liver disease, or tobacco use | Once or twice, depending on the vaccine type |
| **Hepatitis B** | All adults not already immune, especially healthcare workers and people with diabetes or kidney disease | Course of doses, once |
| **Shingles (herpes zoster)** | Adults 50+ | Two doses |
| **Typhoid conjugate** | Children and adults in high-risk areas | As advised |
| **Hepatitis A** | Those not immune, particularly with liver disease | As advised |
| **COVID-19** | Older and high-risk adults | As per current guidance |
| **Rabies pre-exposure** | People at high risk (vets, those handling animals often) | Short course; still need boosters after a bite |
| **Td/Tdap in pregnancy** | Pregnant women | Each pregnancy |

**Shingles** is a painful rash caused by reactivation of the chickenpox virus, more common after 50; it can leave long-lasting nerve pain. The **pneumococcal** and **flu** vaccines are particularly valuable for older parents with diabetes or heart disease.

---

## The layer, in one line

Infections remain a major threat in India, especially for people with diabetes, older adults and children; knowing each germ's route — air, water, mosquitoes, blood, animals and soil — shows how to block it with hygiene, boiled milk, safe water, mosquito control, footwear, careful antibiotic use and a lifetime of vaccines.
