# Certification Roadmap

## What certifications are actually worth

**They open doors and they do not prove capability.**

A recruiter filtering CVs uses them. A partner needing certified headcount for a tier requires them. An interviewer who has spent twenty minutes with you does not care.

**The honest position:** get the ones that unlock opportunities, and do not mistake collecting them for developing. Someone with three certifications who has built a real integration is more employable than someone with nine who has not.

---

## The path

**Foundation**

```
Administrator                 the declarative platform. Take it first, even as a developer.
Platform App Builder          data model, automation, UI. Overlaps heavily with Admin.
```

**The Administrator certification is worth it for a developer**, and this is not obvious. It forces you to learn the declarative surface — sharing, validation rules, approval processes, reporting — which is exactly the knowledge that produces good "flow or Apex" answers. Developers who skip it consistently over-reach for code.

**Developer**

```
Platform Developer I          Apex, triggers, SOQL, LWC basics, testing
Platform Developer II         a multi-part credential: an exam plus superbadges
```

**PD I is the working baseline.** Most developer roles list it.

**PD II is more work than the others combined** — the exam plus Advanced Apex Specialist, Data Integration Specialist, and Aura or Lightning Web Components Specialist superbadges. The superbadges are genuine build exercises, and they teach more than the exam does.

**Architect**

```
Application Architect         earned by combining:
    Platform App Builder
    Platform Developer I
    Data Architect
    Sharing and Visibility Architect

System Architect              earned by combining:
    Development Lifecycle and Deployment Architect
    Integration Architect
    Identity and Access Management Architect

Technical Architect (CTA)     both of the above, plus the review board
```

**Sharing and Visibility Architect and Data Architect are the two most useful individually**, regardless of whether you pursue the full track. They cover the material that causes production failures — sharing recalculation, skew, large data volumes — and the knowledge transfers directly.

**Consultant**

```
Sales Cloud Consultant
Service Cloud Consultant
Experience Cloud Consultant
```

**Worth it for consultancy roles**, less so elsewhere. They test business process design rather than technical depth.

**Specialist**

```
JavaScript Developer I        genuinely about JavaScript, not Salesforce
Data Cloud Consultant
Agentforce Specialist
Marketing Cloud credentials
```

## The order that makes sense

**For a developer starting out:**

```
1. Administrator          the declarative foundation
2. Platform Developer I   the technical baseline
3. Platform App Builder   fills the gaps between them
```

Three to six months, and that combination covers most job requirements.

**Then, depending on direction:**

```
Integration-heavy work    → Integration Architect
Large complex orgs        → Sharing and Visibility, then Data Architect
Frontend focus            → JavaScript Developer I
Consultancy               → Sales or Service Cloud Consultant
Depth                     → Platform Developer II
```

**For the architect track**, Sharing and Visibility first — it is the hardest of the domain architect exams and the most useful, so passing it early tells you whether the track suits you.

## Preparing

**The exams test breadth, not depth.** They cover features you may never use, which is why building alone is insufficient preparation.

**What works:**

**Trailhead trails for the exam.** Salesforce publishes an exam guide with the topic weighting, and it is accurate. Weight your preparation to it rather than studying evenly.

**A practice org.** Reading about entitlement processes is not the same as configuring one, and the exams ask about behaviour rather than definitions.

**Focus Forward Academy or Trailhead Academy** if the budget exists. Not necessary, and useful for the exams with awkward material — the architect ones particularly.

**Practice exams, with caution.** Useful for identifying gaps and pacing. **Dumps are a bad idea** — they teach the questions rather than the material, they violate the exam agreement, and an interview exposes the gap immediately.

**A realistic estimate:**

```
Administrator             4-6 weeks with no experience, 2 with some
Platform Developer I      6-8 weeks with Apex experience
Platform App Builder      2-3 weeks after Administrator
Domain Architect          6-10 weeks each
Platform Developer II     3-4 months including superbadges
```

## Maintenance

**Certifications require maintenance**, released with each Salesforce release.

```
Maintenance modules on Trailhead, per credential, per release cycle
Free, and takes an hour or so
Missing the deadline suspends the certification
```

**The maintenance modules are worth reading properly** rather than clicking through. They are the most efficient summary available of what changed in a release, and keeping current with releases is otherwise a chore nobody does. See [[01.11 - Release Cycle, Trust and Status]].

**Several credentials means several maintenance modules**, three times a year — which is a real ongoing cost and an argument against collecting them indiscriminately.

## What the exams do not cover

Being direct, because it affects how much weight to give them.

**Writing maintainable code.** PD I tests syntax and concepts, not whether your service layer is well designed.

**Debugging.** No exam tests reading a debug log, which is a substantial part of the job.

**Production judgement.** When to say a requirement should be questioned.

**Scale.** The architect exams touch it; the developer ones barely do.

**Working with people.** Which decides more outcomes than any technical skill.

**A certified developer who has never debugged a production incident is a junior developer with a certificate.** The certification is a filter, not a qualification.

---

## The alternative signal

**What actually demonstrates capability:**

**Something you built.** A real org solving a real problem, with an integration and a data model you can defend. That is worth more in an interview than three certifications.

**A contribution.** An open-source Salesforce project, a well-answered Stack Exchange question, a written explanation of something non-obvious.

**A specific story.** "We had duplicate payments on 3% of approved claims because the handler didn't compare against oldMap — it ran for eighteen months before finance noticed" demonstrates more than any credential.

**Superbadges**, more than the exams they support. They are build exercises with real requirements and automated verification, and the Advanced Apex Specialist one in particular is a genuine test.

---

## A realistic plan

**If you have six months and want to be employable:**

```
Month 1-2    Administrator. Build a practice org alongside it.
Month 3-4    Platform Developer I. Write real Apex, not just exercises.
Month 5      Platform App Builder. Fills the gaps.
Month 6      Build something end to end — a data model, automation,
             an integration, a component, and tests.
```

**The last month matters most.** It produces the stories that behavioural rounds want and the specifics that technical rounds reward.

**If you are already working:**

Take the certifications your role's next step requires, and spend the rest of the time on depth. Reading the Large Data Volumes guide properly is worth more than a certification you will not use.

## Section 25 in one paragraph

Interviews assess four things, and only the first is knowledge: whether you know the platform, whether you reason about tradeoffs, whether you have actually built things, and whether you would be pleasant to work with. The tell for seniority is unprompted tradeoff discussion — "I'd use a trigger, because the flow would need a query per record and this object saves in bulk from an integration" is a different answer from "a trigger". Scenario and design rounds reward clarifying before designing, reaching for declarative where it fits, naming the volume assumption, and saying what the design gives up. Coding rounds reward narrating a bulk-safe approach more than silently producing perfect code. And certifications open doors without proving capability — the Administrator credential is genuinely worth a developer's time because it produces better flow-versus-Apex judgement, the domain architect exams cover the material that causes production failures, and beyond that, something you built and can defend is worth more than any additional credential.
