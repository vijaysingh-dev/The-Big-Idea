# What Is High Level Design (HLD)?

> **Revision tags:** #fundamentals #hld-basics

---

## Definition

High Level Design is the process of defining **what components a system needs and how they interact** — without specifying the internal logic of each component.

It answers:
- What services/processes exist?
- How do they communicate?
- Where does data live?
- How does the system scale, fail, and recover?

It does **not** answer:
- How is a function implemented?
- What class hierarchy to use?
- Which algorithm handles a loop?

---

## Why HLD Exists

Software systems are too large to reason about all at once. HLD provides a map of the territory before you build individual rooms.

Without HLD you get:
- Services that can't talk to each other
- Databases that can't scale
- No clear ownership boundaries
- Failures that cascade instead of isolate

---

## What HLD Produces

A completed HLD typically delivers:

1. **Architecture diagram** — boxes (services) and arrows (communication)
2. **Data flow description** — how a request moves through the system
3. **Database choices** — which DB type, why, per service
4. **Scaling strategy** — how each component handles 10x traffic
5. **Failure modes** — what breaks and what the fallback is
6. **API contracts** — what each service exposes (not internal impl)
7. **Non-functional decisions** — latency budgets, consistency choices, SLAs

---

## HLD in the Real World vs in Interviews

| Context | Goal |
|---|---|
| Real project | Align team before building; avoid costly rewrites |
| Architecture review | Validate choices before committing infra |
| HLD interview | Demonstrate you can think at systems scale, not just code |

In interviews, the process and reasoning matters more than the final diagram. A senior engineer who explains tradeoffs clearly outperforms one who draws a perfect diagram without justification.

---

## Mental Model

Think of HLD like **city planning**:
- Zoning decisions = architecture style (monolith vs microservices)
- Road network = communication patterns (sync vs async)
- Water/electricity = shared infrastructure (databases, caches, queues)
- Emergency routes = failure paths and redundancy

You design the city layout before building individual buildings (LLD).

---

## What Makes a Good HLD

- Matches the actual requirements (not over-engineered for requirements that don't exist)
- Makes tradeoffs explicit and justified
- Handles failure cases, not just happy paths
- Is understandable to other engineers without a 2-hour explanation
- Scales in the right dimensions (read-heavy? write-heavy? storage-heavy?)
