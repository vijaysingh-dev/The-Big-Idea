# The Liver & Kidneys: Factories and Filters

*Health & Fitness · 2026-10-30*

The liver and kidneys do the body's processing and cleaning work, and both are famously quiet when they're failing. A liver can lose most of its function and a kidney can lose more than half before a person feels anything. That makes them organs you protect by habit and check by blood test, not by waiting for symptoms.

---

## 1. The liver: the body's chemical factory

The **liver** is the largest internal organ, sitting under the right ribs. Everything absorbed from the gut flows to it first through the **portal vein** ([[07 - Gut Health & Digestion]]), so it gets the first look at every nutrient, drug and toxin you swallow.

### What it does
- **Fuel management.** Stores glucose as glycogen after meals and releases it between meals; makes new glucose during fasting; turns excess carbohydrate into fat; makes ketones in long fasts ([[03 - Energy Metabolism - How the Body Makes and Uses Fuel]]).
- **Cholesterol and lipoproteins.** Makes most of the body's cholesterol and packages fats into VLDL and LDL particles. It also clears LDL from the blood. Statins work here, by making the liver pull more LDL out ([[19 - How Heart Disease Actually Happens]]).
- **Protein production.** Makes **albumin** (the main protein in blood, which keeps fluid inside vessels), clotting factors, and transport proteins.
- **Detoxification.** Breaks down drugs, alcohol, hormones and toxins so they can be removed in bile or urine.
- **Bile.** Makes bile, which helps digest fat and carries waste such as **bilirubin** (the yellow pigment from recycled red blood cells) out through the gut.
- **Storage.** Holds reserves of vitamins A, D and B12, and iron.
- **Immune role.** Filters bacteria coming from the gut.

The liver has a remarkable ability to **regenerate**; it can regrow after part of it is removed. But repeated injury over years causes **scarring** (fibrosis) instead of clean repair, and widespread scarring is **cirrhosis**: a stiff, failing liver that can no longer regenerate properly.

### What harms the liver
- **Fatty liver (MASLD)**, from insulin resistance and excess refined carbohydrate and sugar ([[07 - Gut Health & Digestion]], [[18 - Blood Sugar, Insulin Resistance & Diabetes]]). Now one of the most common liver diseases in India.
- **Alcohol.** The liver can only process a limited amount at a time; regular heavy drinking causes fatty liver, alcoholic hepatitis and cirrhosis ([[16 - Tobacco, Alcohol & Environmental Exposures]]).
- **Viral hepatitis.** **Hepatitis B** and **C** spread through blood (unsafe injections, transfusions, shared razors, tattoos, from mother to baby, and sexually) and can cause silent long-term damage, cirrhosis and liver cancer. Hepatitis B has an effective vaccine; hepatitis C is now curable with tablets. **Hepatitis A and E** spread through contaminated food and water and cause acute jaundice ([[17 - Infections, Hygiene & Vaccines]]).
- **Medicines and "herbal" products.** **Paracetamol** is safe at the right dose but one of the most common causes of serious liver damage in overdose. Some unregulated herbal, ayurvedic and bodybuilding supplements have caused liver injury.
- **Aflatoxin**, a toxin from mould on badly stored grains and peanuts. Store grain dry, and discard visibly mouldy food.

### Warning signs of liver trouble (usually late)
Yellow eyes or skin (**jaundice**), dark urine, pale stools, a swollen belly (fluid build-up called **ascites**), easy bruising, swollen legs, confusion, vomiting blood.

### The liver panel (LFT)
A **liver function test** (LFT) groups several markers:

| Marker | What it reflects |
|---|---|
| **ALT (SGPT)** | An enzyme inside liver cells; rises when liver cells are damaged. The most liver-specific marker. |
| **AST (SGOT)** | A similar enzyme also found in muscle and heart; rises with liver damage and after heavy exercise. |
| **ALP** (alkaline phosphatase) | Rises with blocked bile flow, and also with bone conditions. |
| **GGT** | Rises with bile problems and often with alcohol use. |
| **Bilirubin** (total, direct, indirect) | High in jaundice. Indirect (unconjugated) bilirubin can be mildly high in a common harmless condition called Gilbert's syndrome. |
| **Albumin and total protein** | Low albumin can reflect poor liver production, poor nutrition or kidney protein loss. |

Normal LFTs don't rule out fatty liver; many people with fatty liver have normal enzymes. An **ultrasound of the abdomen** detects moderate fatty liver, and scores or scans such as FibroScan estimate scarring.

## 2. The kidneys: the body's filters and regulators

The two **kidneys** sit at the back of the abdomen, one on each side of the spine, just below the ribs. Each contains about a million tiny filtering units called **nephrons**.

### What they do
- **Filter the blood.** The kidneys filter roughly 150–180 litres of blood plasma a day, then reabsorb almost all the water and useful substances, leaving about 1–2 litres of urine carrying waste such as **urea** (from protein breakdown) and **creatinine** (from normal muscle activity).
- **Balance water and salts.** They fine-tune sodium, potassium, calcium and phosphate. Potassium in particular must stay in a narrow range; too high or too low can cause dangerous heart rhythms.
- **Balance acid.** They keep the blood's acidity stable.
- **Control blood pressure.** Through salt and water handling and the **renin** hormone system ([[04 - Hormones - The Body's Messaging System]]). This is why kidney disease and high BP feed each other in a vicious cycle ([[20 - Blood Pressure, Stroke & Kidney Disease]]).
- **Make hormones.** **EPO** tells bone marrow to make red blood cells; the kidneys also activate vitamin D. Failing kidneys therefore cause anaemia and weak bones.

### What harms the kidneys
- **Diabetes and high BP:** the two biggest causes of chronic kidney disease (CKD) in India and worldwide. Both damage the tiny filtering blood vessels.
- **Painkillers (NSAIDs).** **NSAIDs** (non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, such as ibuprofen, diclofenac, aceclofenac and nimesulide) reduce blood flow in the kidneys. Taken occasionally they're fine for most people, but regular long-term use, very common for joint and back pain in India, can damage the kidneys, especially in older adults, people with diabetes or BP, and with dehydration.
- **Dehydration**, especially in heat and with diarrhoea.
- **Unregulated herbal and traditional products**, some of which contain heavy metals such as lead, mercury or arsenic.
- **Kidney stones and urinary obstruction**, including an enlarged prostate in older men.
- **Repeated or untreated kidney infections.**

**Chronic kidney disease of unknown cause** has also been reported in some Indian farming regions, possibly linked to heat stress, dehydration and agrochemical exposure; it's an area of ongoing research.

### Warning signs (usually late)
Swelling of the feet, ankles or face; foamy urine; reduced urine; tiredness; poor appetite; itching; nausea; breathlessness. By the time these appear, kidney function is often badly reduced.

### The kidney panel (KFT)
| Marker | What it reflects |
|---|---|
| **Creatinine** | A muscle waste product cleared by the kidneys. Rises as kidney function falls. Also depends on muscle mass: older or less muscular people can have a "normal" creatinine with reduced kidney function, and muscular people can be slightly high with healthy kidneys. |
| **eGFR** (estimated glomerular filtration rate) | An estimate of how much blood the kidneys filter per minute, calculated from creatinine, age and sex. Above 90 is normal; it falls with age. Persistently below 60 for three months or more indicates chronic kidney disease. |
| **Urea / BUN** (blood urea nitrogen) | Another waste product; affected by kidney function, protein intake and hydration. |
| **UACR** (urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio) | Measures tiny amounts of albumin protein leaking into urine. The **earliest** sign of kidney damage from diabetes or BP, often years before creatinine rises. Should be checked yearly in anyone with diabetes or high BP. |
| **Electrolytes** (sodium, potassium, chloride) | Salt balance. |
| **Uric acid** | High levels can cause gout and kidney stones and are linked to metabolic disease. |
| **Urine routine** | Protein, sugar, blood, infection. A quick, cheap screen, but misses early albumin leaks. |

## 3. Water and salt

**How much water?** There's no single number for everyone. Needs depend on heat, activity, body size and health. A practical guide: drink enough that urine is pale yellow, more in summer and during heavy physical work, and don't wait for thirst in very hot weather, especially older adults, whose thirst signal weakens with age. People with heart failure or advanced kidney disease may need to *limit* fluids on medical advice.

**Salt** matters for both organs: high salt raises BP, which damages the kidneys, and damaged kidneys handle salt less well ([[11 - The Indian Plate - Rebuilding the Thali]]).

**Low sodium** on a blood report (hyponatraemia) is common in older adults. Causes include some BP medicines (especially diuretics), drinking very large amounts of water, vomiting or diarrhoea, and some hormone and heart problems. Mildly low values are often harmless, but significantly low sodium can cause confusion and falls, so it's worth discussing with a doctor.

## 4. Protecting both organs: the habits

- **Control blood sugar and BP**; these protect the kidneys more than anything else.
- **Keep belly fat down and limit sugar and refined carbohydrate**, the main drivers of fatty liver.
- **Little or no alcohol.**
- **Use painkillers carefully.** Avoid long-term daily NSAID use without a doctor's advice; for chronic joint pain, exercise and physiotherapy are safer first steps ([[12 - Strength Training - Muscle as Medicine]]).
- **Avoid unregulated herbal and "body-building" products**, and tell doctors about everything you take.
- **Hepatitis B vaccination**, safe injection practices, and testing for hepatitis B and C at least once.
- **Stay hydrated**, especially in heat and during farm work.
- **Test yearly:** LFT, kidney panel with eGFR, and UACR for anyone with diabetes or high BP ([[27 - The Testing Calendar]]).

---

## The layer, in one line

The liver is the body's chemical factory — managing fuel, making cholesterol and proteins and breaking down drugs and toxins — and the kidneys are its filters and regulators of water, salt, acid and blood pressure; both fail silently, mostly from high sugar, high BP, fatty liver, alcohol and careless use of medicines, so they're protected by habits and watched by blood tests.
