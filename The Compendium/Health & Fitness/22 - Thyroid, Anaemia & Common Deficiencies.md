# Thyroid, Anaemia & Common Deficiencies

*Health & Fitness · 2026-10-30*

A huge number of Indians feel tired, weak, cold, low or achy for years and are told it's age, stress or "kamzori." Very often the real cause is a cheap, testable, fixable problem: a sluggish thyroid, too little haemoglobin, or a shortage of vitamin D or B12. These conditions rarely kill directly, but they quietly lower quality of life and make other diseases worse.

---

## 1. The thyroid

[[04 - Hormones - The Body's Messaging System]] explained the feedback loop: the pituitary releases **TSH** to tell the thyroid to make **T4** and **T3**, the hormones that set the body's metabolic speed. TSH rises when thyroid output falls.

### Hypothyroidism (underactive thyroid)
The most common thyroid problem in India, much more frequent in women. The usual cause is **Hashimoto's thyroiditis**, an autoimmune condition in which the immune system slowly damages the thyroid ([[06 - The Immune System & Inflammation]]). Iodine deficiency, once a major cause, has fallen greatly since iodised salt became mandatory, though it still exists in some areas.

**Symptoms** come on slowly and are easy to dismiss:
- Tiredness, sluggishness, sleepiness
- Weight gain, swelling of the face
- Feeling cold
- Constipation
- Dry skin, hair thinning, brittle nails
- Slowed thinking, poor memory, low mood
- Heavy or irregular periods; difficulty conceiving
- Muscle aches, joint stiffness
- Raised LDL cholesterol

**Diagnosis:** a high **TSH** with a low **free T4** is **overt hypothyroidism**. A high TSH with a **normal** T4 is **subclinical hypothyroidism**, an earlier or milder stage. The **anti-TPO antibody** test (antibodies against thyroid peroxidase, an enzyme in the thyroid) shows whether autoimmune thyroiditis is the cause; a positive result means a higher chance of progressing to overt hypothyroidism over time.

**Subclinical hypothyroidism: what doctors generally do**
- **Repeat TSH** (with free T4) after about 6–12 weeks, because TSH can be temporarily raised by recent illness, some medicines, or lab variation.
- **Treatment is usually recommended** if TSH stays at or above about 10, in pregnancy or when trying to conceive, and often considered with milder elevations when there are symptoms, positive anti-TPO antibodies, a goitre, or high cardiovascular risk. Older adults with mildly raised TSH are often just monitored, since TSH rises naturally a little with age.
- **Monitor** TSH every 6–12 months if not treated.

**Treatment:** **levothyroxine**, a synthetic version of T4, taken once daily. It's cheap, safe and usually lifelong. To work properly:
- Take it on an **empty stomach**, with water, **30–60 minutes before breakfast and tea** (or at bedtime, at least 3–4 hours after dinner).
- Keep it **4 hours apart** from calcium, iron, antacids and some supplements, which block its absorption.
- **Recheck TSH** about 6–8 weeks after starting or changing the dose, then every 6–12 months once stable.
- Don't stop it on your own because you feel better.

### Hyperthyroidism (overactive thyroid)
Less common. Causes include **Graves' disease** (autoimmune) and thyroid nodules. Symptoms: weight loss despite good appetite, fast or irregular heartbeat, tremor, sweating, heat intolerance, anxiety, poor sleep, frequent bowel movements, sometimes bulging eyes. **TSH is low** and T4/T3 high. It can trigger atrial fibrillation and bone loss, so it needs prompt treatment.

### Goitre and nodules
A **goitre** is an enlarged thyroid, visible as a swelling at the front of the neck. **Nodules** are lumps in the thyroid, very common and mostly harmless, but a new, growing or hard lump, especially with hoarseness or difficulty swallowing, needs an ultrasound and medical review.

### Thyroid and diet
- **Iodised salt** is enough for most people; iodine supplements aren't needed unless advised.
- **Goitrogens** — compounds in raw cruciferous vegetables (cabbage, cauliflower), soya and some millets such as bajra — matter mainly in iodine deficiency or at very high intakes. Normal cooked portions are fine; rotate millets rather than eating one daily ([[11 - The Indian Plate - Rebuilding the Thali]]).

## 2. Anaemia

**Anaemia** means too little haemoglobin to carry enough oxygen ([[05 - Blood, Circulation & the Heart]]). Roughly: below 12 g/dL in non-pregnant women, below 13 g/dL in men, and below 11 g/dL in pregnancy (WHO thresholds). India has one of the highest anaemia rates in the world. National surveys (NFHS-5, 2019–21) found more than half of women and children anaemic. The government's **Anaemia Mukt Bharat** programme provides iron and folic acid supplements and deworming.

**Symptoms:** tiredness, weakness, breathlessness on climbing stairs, palpitations, dizziness, headaches, pale skin, inner eyelids and nails, brittle or spoon-shaped nails, restless legs, poor concentration. Mild anaemia often causes no obvious symptoms.

### Types and causes, read from the blood count
| Clue on the CBC | Likely type | Common causes |
|---|---|---|
| Small red cells (low **MCV**), low MCH, high **RDW** | **Iron deficiency** | Low dietary iron, poor absorption, blood loss (heavy periods, worms, stomach or bowel bleeding), pregnancy |
| Small red cells, normal or high RBC count, **Mentzer index** (MCV ÷ RBC count) below 13 | Possible **thalassaemia trait** | Inherited; harmless on its own but important before marriage and pregnancy (a haemoglobin electrophoresis test confirms it) |
| Large red cells (high MCV) | **B12 or folate deficiency** | Vegetarian diet low in dairy, long-term metformin or acid-suppressants, poor absorption, alcohol |
| Normal-sized cells | **Anaemia of chronic disease**, kidney disease, mixed causes | Long-term inflammation, infection, chronic kidney disease, cancer |

A mix of iron and B12 deficiency can produce normal-sized cells with a high RDW, which is why doctors look at the whole picture.

### Iron studies
- **Ferritin:** the body's iron store. **Low ferritin confirms iron deficiency.** But ferritin rises with inflammation, so a "normal" ferritin doesn't rule it out when inflammation is present.
- **Serum iron, TIBC** (total iron-binding capacity), **transferrin saturation:** further pieces of the picture.

### Treating iron deficiency
- **Find the cause.** Especially in men and post-menopausal women, unexplained iron deficiency must be investigated for **hidden bleeding** from the stomach or bowel, including ulcers and cancer ([[07 - Gut Health & Digestion]], [[23 - Cancer - How It Starts and How to Catch It Early]]). Deworming, treating heavy periods and addressing diet matter too.
- **Iron tablets** as prescribed. Taking them on alternate days can be as effective and better tolerated. Take with water or a source of **vitamin C** (lemon, amla, guava), **not with tea, coffee, milk or calcium**, which block absorption. Expect dark stools; constipation and stomach upset are common and can be managed by adjusting dose or timing.
- **IV iron** for severe deficiency, pregnancy or when tablets fail.
- **Iron-rich foods:** green leafy vegetables, pulses, jaggery in small amounts, dates, sesame, ragi, bajra, meat, fish and eggs. Cooking in iron utensils adds a little iron.

## 3. Vitamin B12 deficiency

**Vitamin B12** is found almost only in animal foods: milk, curd, paneer, eggs, meat and fish. It's essential for red blood cells and nerves ([[10 - The Brain & Nervous System]]).

**At risk:** strict vegetarians and vegans, especially those with little dairy; long-term users of **metformin** and **acid-suppressing medicines** (PPIs); older adults (stomach absorption declines); people with gut conditions.

**Symptoms:** tiredness, anaemia, numbness and tingling in hands and feet, balance problems, memory problems, low mood, a sore red tongue. Nerve damage can become permanent if left untreated for long.

**Treatment:** B12 tablets or injections as prescribed. People on long-term metformin should have B12 checked periodically. A B12 level at the low end of the normal range with symptoms may need further testing (homocysteine or methylmalonic acid).

## 4. Vitamin D deficiency

Very common across India in all ages, even in sunny regions: indoor work, covered skin, darker skin, pollution, and few dietary sources ([[02 - Micronutrients & RDAs]]).

- **Levels (25-OH vitamin D):** below 20 ng/mL is usually called deficient; 20–30 insufficient; 30–100 sufficient; very high levels (above roughly 100–150) risk toxicity. Cut-offs vary between guidelines.
- **Effects:** weak bones and muscles, bone pain, higher fall and fracture risk ([[21 - Joints & Bones - Osteoarthritis, Osteoporosis & Falls]]).
- **Sources:** sunlight on arms and legs for 15–30 minutes around mid-morning to mid-afternoon several times a week (more for darker skin, older people); fortified milk and oils; eggs; fatty fish.
- **Supplements:** commonly a weekly high dose (such as 60,000 IU) for some weeks to correct deficiency, then a lower maintenance dose. **Avoid stacking** prescribed doses with multivitamins and fortified supplements without telling the doctor; vitamin D accumulates in fat and excess raises blood calcium, causing nausea, kidney stones and confusion. Recheck levels after 3 months.

## 5. Other common deficiencies

- **Zinc:** important for immunity, wound healing, taste and skin. Low intake is common in grain-heavy vegetarian diets, and phytates in grains reduce absorption. Sources: pulses, nuts, seeds (pumpkin, sesame), whole grains (soaked or sprouted), dairy, eggs. Supplements only if a doctor finds deficiency; long-term excess zinc causes copper deficiency.
- **Folate:** needed for red blood cells and in early pregnancy to prevent birth defects. Sources: green leafy vegetables, pulses, citrus. Women planning pregnancy should take folic acid as advised, ideally starting before conception.
- **Calcium:** see [[21 - Joints & Bones - Osteoarthritis, Osteoporosis & Falls]].
- **Iodine:** covered by iodised salt for most.
- **Protein:** very common in Indian diets, causing weakness and muscle loss ([[11 - The Indian Plate - Rebuilding the Thali]]).
- **Magnesium:** found in nuts, seeds, pulses, whole grains and green vegetables. Long-term acid suppressants and diuretics can lower levels.

## 6. The rule for supplements

- **Test, don't guess.** Supplement what a test shows is low.
- **Recheck** after treatment to confirm it worked and to avoid overdose.
- **Food first** for maintenance once levels are normal.
- **Tell doctors about everything you take**, including whey, multivitamins and herbal products.
- **More isn't better.** Vitamins A, D, E, iron, zinc and selenium can all cause harm in excess.

---

## The layer, in one line

A sluggish thyroid, iron or B12 deficiency anaemia and low vitamin D are common, cheap to test and easy to treat, and they're behind much of the tiredness, weakness and "kamzori" dismissed as ageing; the rules are test, find the cause, treat as prescribed, recheck, and avoid stacking supplements.
