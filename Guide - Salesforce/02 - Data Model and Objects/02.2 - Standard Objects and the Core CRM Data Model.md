# Standard Objects and the Core CRM Data Model

## Why to learn this properly

The standard objects are not a starting point you customize away from. They are the vocabulary of the entire ecosystem — every feature, every report type, every managed package, every interview question assumes them.

More practically: when you understand how they connect, most requirements resolve into "add a field here" rather than "build a new object".

## The core graph

```
                 Campaign
                     |
                 (CampaignMember)
                     |
Lead ---convert---> Account <---- Contact
                     |    \          |
                     |     \         |
              Opportunity   Case ----+
                  |   \
        OpportunityLineItem   Quote
                  |             |
             PricebookEntry   QuoteLineItem
                  |
              Product2
```

Read it as: companies (Account) have people (Contact), deals (Opportunity), and problems (Case). Deals contain products. Marketing programs (Campaign) generate interest, which arrives as Leads and becomes the rest.

## Account

The company. In business-to-business Salesforce this is the centre of the model — nearly everything else points at it.

Fields worth knowing: `Name`, `Type`, `Industry`, `AnnualRevenue`, `NumberOfEmployees`, `BillingAddress` and `ShippingAddress` (compound fields, addressed as `BillingStreet`, `BillingCity` and so on), `ParentId` for hierarchies, `OwnerId`.

**Person Accounts** are a variant for business-to-consumer models, where a single record represents an individual and behaves as both Account and Contact simultaneously. Enabling them is irreversible and changes behaviour across the org, so it is one of the most consequential decisions in an implementation. See [[04.5 - Accounts - Business vs Person Accounts]].

## Contact

A person at a company. `AccountId` is a lookup to Account.

One structural detail matters a lot in enterprise work: a Contact belongs to exactly one Account through `AccountId`. Real people often relate to several companies — a consultant working with three clients, a director sitting on multiple boards. Salesforce solves this with **Contacts to Multiple Accounts**, which creates `AccountContactRelation` junction records while `AccountId` remains the primary. If a requirement mentions people belonging to more than one company, this is the feature, not a custom object.

## Lead

An unqualified prospect. Deliberately disconnected from Account and Contact — it holds `Company` as free text, not a lookup.

Lead is a staging area, and its design exists so unverified data cannot pollute the clean customer database. `Status` drives the qualification process, `IsConverted` marks the transition, and `ConvertedAccountId`, `ConvertedContactId`, `ConvertedOpportunityId` link back to what was created.

A frequent bug: querying Leads without excluding converted ones.

```sql
SELECT Id, Name, Company, Status, LeadSource
FROM Lead
WHERE IsConverted = false AND Status != 'Disqualified'
```

Converted Leads stay in the database forever and are invisible in most list views, so code that omits `IsConverted = false` quietly processes records nobody expects. See [[04.4 - Lead Conversion]].

## Opportunity

A potential sale. This is where revenue reporting comes from, which makes it the most politically loaded object in any org.

`AccountId`, `Amount`, `CloseDate`, `StageName`, `Probability`, `ForecastCategory`, `IsClosed`, `IsWon`, `Type`, `LeadSource`.

Two derived-field behaviours to know. `IsClosed` and `IsWon` are set by the platform from the stage's configuration, not by you — a stage is defined as closed and won or closed and lost in Setup. And `Probability` and `ForecastCategory` default from the stage but can be overridden per record, which is a common source of "why does the forecast not match the pipeline report".

## Product2, Pricebook2, PricebookEntry, OpportunityLineItem

This chain confuses almost everyone the first time, so hold the logic:

- **Product2** is the catalogue item. A product exists once.
- **Pricebook2** is a named price list. There is always a Standard Price Book, plus optional custom ones for regions, currencies, or customer tiers.
- **PricebookEntry** is a product's price *in* a price book. It is the junction between the two, and it is why the same product can cost different amounts in different lists.
- **OpportunityLineItem** is a product on a specific deal, referencing a PricebookEntry.

A product must have an entry in the Standard Price Book before it can be added to any custom price book. This ordering requirement causes more data-loading failures than any other part of Sales Cloud. See [[04.10 - Products, Price Books and Pricing]].

## Case

A customer problem. `AccountId`, `ContactId`, `Subject`, `Description`, `Status`, `Priority`, `Origin`, `Type`, `IsEscalated`, `OwnerId`, `ParentId` for case hierarchies.

`OwnerId` on Case is polymorphic — it can point at a User *or* a Queue, which is how case routing works. This surprises people writing Apex, because you cannot assume `Owner.Name` refers to a person. See [[03.12 - Public Groups, Queues and Territory Management]].

## Campaign and CampaignMember

Campaign is a marketing program. **CampaignMember** is a junction record linking a Campaign to either a Lead or a Contact, tracking status such as Sent, Responded, Attended.

CampaignMember is unusual: it has both `LeadId` and `ContactId`, and exactly one is populated. When a Lead converts, its CampaignMember records automatically switch to pointing at the new Contact, which is how campaign attribution survives conversion.

## Task and Event (Activities)

Interactions. **Task** is something to do or that was done — a call, an email, a to-do. **Event** is a calendar entry with a start and end time.

Both use the same two-part relationship pattern, and this pattern appears throughout the platform:

- `WhoId` — the person, either a Contact or a Lead
- `WhatId` — the related record, which can be an Account, Opportunity, Case, or a custom object

Both are **polymorphic lookups**: one field that can point at several different object types. The virtual `Activity` object exists for reporting across both.

```sql
SELECT Id, Subject, ActivityDate, Who.Name, What.Name, TYPEOF What WHEN Opportunity THEN Amount, StageName END
FROM Task
WHERE WhatId IN :opportunityIds AND IsClosed = false
```

`TYPEOF` is SOQL's syntax for querying fields that only exist on some of the possible types. See [[08.2 - Relationship Queries]].

## User, Group, and the identity objects

**User** is a login. Also referenced by every `OwnerId`, `CreatedById`, and `LastModifiedById` in the org.

**Group** holds public groups, queues, and role-derived groups. **UserRole** is the role hierarchy. **Profile** and **PermissionSet** define permissions. These are **setup objects**, and they behave differently in one important way: you cannot perform DML on a setup object and a normal object in the same transaction. See [[10.7 - Mixed DML and Setup Objects]].

## Fields every object has

Learning these once saves a lot of lookup:

`Id`, `Name` (or a compound of `FirstName` and `LastName` on person-like objects), `OwnerId`, `CreatedDate`, `CreatedById`, `LastModifiedDate`, `LastModifiedById`, `SystemModstamp`, `IsDeleted`, `LastActivityDate`, `LastViewedDate`, `RecordTypeId` where record types are enabled.

`SystemModstamp` is worth singling out. It updates on system-level changes as well as user edits, and it is indexed and reliable, which makes it the correct field for incremental integration syncs — "give me everything changed since my last run". Using `LastModifiedDate` for this is a common and subtle integration bug, because some platform updates do not touch it.

```sql
SELECT Id, Name, SystemModstamp
FROM Account
WHERE SystemModstamp > :lastSyncTimestamp
ORDER BY SystemModstamp ASC
LIMIT 10000
```

## What experienced engineers do with this

**They map requirements onto the standard model first.** "We need to track service agreements" maps to Contract or Entitlement before it maps to a new custom object.

**They know which fields the platform maintains.** Writing to `IsClosed` on Opportunity does not work; changing `StageName` does. Confusing derived fields with editable ones produces bugs that look like the platform is broken.

**They watch for polymorphic fields.** `WhoId`, `WhatId`, `OwnerId` on Case, and `ParentId` on several objects can all point at multiple types. Apex that assumes one type fails at runtime on the others.

**They read the Object Reference rather than guessing.** Standard object behaviour has many small exceptions, all documented, and nearly none of it is guessable.
