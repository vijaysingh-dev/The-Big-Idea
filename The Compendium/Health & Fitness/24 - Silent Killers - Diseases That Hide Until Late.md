# Silent Killers: Diseases That Hide Until Late

*Health & Fitness · 2026-10-30*

The most dangerous diseases are often the ones that don't hurt. High blood pressure, diabetes, blocked arteries, failing kidneys, a scarring liver and thinning bones can all progress for ten or twenty years without a single symptom, and then announce themselves with a heart attack, a stroke, dialysis or a broken hip. The only defence is to go looking for them before they find you.

---

## 1. Why some diseases stay silent

The body has enormous **reserve capacity**:
- An artery can be about 70% blocked before it causes chest pain on exertion, and a soft plaque that blocks much less can rupture without warning ([[19 - How Heart Disease Actually Happens]]).
- The kidneys can lose more than half their function before blood creatinine rises noticeably, and much more before symptoms appear ([[08 - The Liver & Kidneys - Factories and Filters]]).
- The liver can function with much of it scarred.
- The pancreas can compensate for insulin resistance for a decade ([[18 - Blood Sugar, Insulin Resistance & Diabetes]]).
- Bones have no nerves that signal thinning.

Silent diseases also tend to **cluster and feed each other**. Insulin resistance, high BP, high triglycerides with low HDL, fatty liver and belly fat often appear together (this cluster is called **metabolic syndrome**), and together they drive heart attacks, strokes and kidney disease. Finding one is a reason to check for the others.

## 2. The list: silent killers and how to catch them

| Condition | Why it's dangerous | Late signs (when it's often too late) | How to catch it early | How often |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **High blood pressure** | Stroke, heart attack, heart failure, kidney failure | Stroke, chest pain, breathlessness, kidney failure | **BP measurement** at home or clinic | Every 1–2 years if normal (adults 18+); monthly at home if high or 40+ |
| **Type 2 diabetes / prediabetes** | Heart attack, stroke, blindness, kidney failure, amputation | Thirst, frequent urination, weight loss, infections, numb feet, blurred vision | **HbA1c + fasting glucose** | Yearly from 30 (earlier with family history, belly fat or PCOS) |
| **High LDL / ApoB** | Silent plaque in arteries | Heart attack, stroke | **Lipid profile** (+ ApoB) | Every 1–5 years from 20s; yearly from 40 or if abnormal |
| **High Lp(a)** | Genetic heart attack and stroke risk | Early heart attack | **Lp(a)** | **Once in life** |
| **Coronary artery disease** | Heart attack, sudden death | Chest pain, breathlessness, heart attack | Risk factor control; **ECG**; **CT calcium score** or stress test in selected people | As per risk; ECG yearly from 40–50 is common practice in India |
| **Chronic kidney disease** | Kidney failure, heart disease | Swelling, foamy urine, tiredness, breathlessness | **Creatinine + eGFR**, **urine albumin (UACR)** | Yearly with diabetes, BP, or age 60+ |
| **Fatty liver / liver scarring** | Cirrhosis, liver cancer | Jaundice, swollen belly, vomiting blood | **LFT**, **abdominal ultrasound**; fibrosis scores or FibroScan if at risk | Ultrasound with diabetes, obesity or abnormal LFT; as advised |
| **Hepatitis B or C** | Silent cirrhosis and liver cancer | Jaundice, liver failure | **HBsAg** and **anti-HCV** blood tests | **Once in life**, repeat if at risk |
| **Cancers (mouth, cervix, breast, bowel)** | Spread before symptoms | Lumps, bleeding, weight loss | Mouth exam, HPV/Pap/VIA, breast exam/mammogram, FIT/colonoscopy | See [[23 - Cancer - How It Starts and How to Catch It Early]] |
| **Osteoporosis** | Hip and spine fractures | Fracture from a minor fall, loss of height | **DEXA scan**; FRAX risk | Women 65+, men 70+, earlier with risk |
| **Glaucoma** | Permanent blindness, side vision lost first | Tunnel vision, blindness | **Eye pressure and optic nerve check** by an eye doctor | Every 1–2 years from 40 |
| **Diabetic eye disease** | Blindness | Blurred vision | **Retinal (dilated eye) examination** | Yearly with diabetes |
| **Sleep apnoea** | High BP, AF, stroke, accidents | Hard-to-control BP, daytime sleepiness | Ask the bed partner about snoring and pauses; **sleep study** | If symptoms |
| **Atrial fibrillation** | Stroke | Stroke | **Pulse check** for irregularity; ECG | Pulse check at every BP check from 65 |
| **TB (early)** | Spreads to family; lung damage | Weight loss, coughing blood | Ask about cough over 2 weeks; sputum test | When symptoms; contacts of TB patients |
| **COPD** | Progressive breathlessness, lung failure | Breathlessness on mild effort | **Spirometry** (breathing test) | Smokers and chulha-exposed adults with any breathlessness or cough |
| **Thyroid disease** | Heart rhythm problems, high cholesterol, low mood | Variable | **TSH** | Every 1–3 years for adult women; when symptoms |
| **Anaemia** | Heart strain, fatigue, poor pregnancy outcomes | Breathlessness, palpitations | **CBC** | Yearly |
| **Hearing loss** | Isolation, depression, dementia risk | Withdrawal, misunderstanding | **Hearing check** | From 60, or when family notices |
| **Abdominal aortic aneurysm** | Sudden fatal rupture | Sudden severe pain, collapse | **Ultrasound** of the abdomen | Once for men 65–75 who have ever smoked |
| **Gum disease and dental decay** | Tooth loss, infections, linked with heart disease and diabetes | Pain, loose teeth | **Dental check** | Every 6–12 months |

## 3. The pattern behind the list

Look across that table and a few lessons stand out:

1. **A handful of cheap tests catch most of it.** A BP cuff, HbA1c and fasting glucose, a lipid profile, creatinine with eGFR, urine albumin, LFT, CBC and TSH cost a few thousand rupees a year in total, or nothing at government NP-NCD screening, and cover most of the deadliest silent diseases ([[27 - The Testing Calendar]]).
2. **Symptoms are the wrong signal.** For these diseases, "I feel fine" carries almost no information.
3. **Risk factors are the real early warning.** A growing waist, a family history of early heart attacks or diabetes, tobacco use, inactivity and poor sleep tell you more about future risk than how you feel today.
4. **Results need action, not just a file.** Many Indian families have years of reports showing gradually rising sugar or BP with nothing done. Finding the problem is only useful if it changes what happens next.

## 4. Family history: a free test

Ask parents, grandparents, uncles and aunts what they had and at what age, and write it down:
- Heart attack or stroke, especially before 55 in men or 65 in women
- Diabetes
- High cholesterol or Lp(a)
- Cancers (which type, what age)
- Kidney disease
- Thyroid disease
- Thalassaemia or other blood disorders
- Glaucoma
- Mental illness or dementia

A strong family history means testing earlier and more often, and sometimes specific tests (such as Lp(a) for relatives of someone with high Lp(a), or genetic testing for certain cancers).

## 5. Signs that a silent disease may be speaking up

None of these alone proves anything, but they're reasons to get checked soon rather than waiting for the annual test:
- New **breathlessness or chest discomfort** on effort
- **Swelling** of ankles, face or around the eyes
- **Foamy urine**, less urine, or getting up often at night to urinate
- **Excessive thirst and urination**, unexplained weight loss
- **Numbness or burning** in the feet
- **Blurred vision**, or noticing side vision narrowing
- **Unusual tiredness**, especially with pale skin
- **Loss of height**, stooping
- **Loud snoring with pauses**, daytime sleepiness
- **Irregular pulse**, palpitations
- **Unexplained weight loss**, lumps, persistent cough, bleeding
- **Repeated infections** or slow-healing wounds
- **Memory changes** noticed by family

## 6. The household system

For each adult in the family, keep:
1. **A one-page health card**: diagnoses, medicines and doses, allergies, family history, emergency contacts.
2. **A results log**: a simple notebook, spreadsheet or app with the date and value of each key test (BP, HbA1c, LDL, eGFR, TSH, Hb, weight, waist) so trends are visible.
3. **A calendar**: when each test is due ([[27 - The Testing Calendar]]).
4. **A rule for every abnormal result:** who to show it to, and by when.

The **ABHA** (Ayushman Bharat Health Account) digital health ID can link records across hospitals and labs in India, which helps when seeing different doctors.

---

## The layer, in one line

High BP, diabetes, artery disease, kidney and liver disease, early cancers, glaucoma and osteoporosis all progress for years without symptoms because the body compensates until it can't; a few cheap, regular tests, a written family history and a habit of acting on trends catch nearly all of them while they can still be stopped.
