# Tobacco, Alcohol & Environmental Exposures

*Health & Fitness · 2026-10-30*

Diet and exercise get the most attention, but some of the biggest health risks in India come from things people put in their mouths without thinking of them as food — gutkha, khaini, supari, beedi, alcohol — and from the air and water around them. Removing one of these often does more good than any diet change.

---

## 1. Tobacco in India: not just cigarettes

India has one of the largest numbers of tobacco users in the world, and most of it isn't smoked. The Global Adult Tobacco Survey (GATS-2, 2016–17) found that more than a quarter of Indian adults used tobacco in some form, with **smokeless tobacco** used by more people than smoking.

Common forms:
- **Smoked:** cigarettes, **beedi** (cheap hand-rolled tobacco in a leaf, often more harmful than cigarettes per stick), hookah, chillum.
- **Smokeless:**
  - **Khaini** (tobacco with lime, rubbed in the palm and placed in the mouth)
  - **Gutkha** (tobacco with areca nut, lime and flavourings)
  - **Zarda** and tobacco added to **paan**
  - **Mishri** and **gul** (tobacco pastes rubbed on teeth)
  - **Chewing tobacco** in general

### Smokeless doesn't mean safe
Smokeless tobacco delivers nicotine and thousands of chemicals, including powerful carcinogens (cancer-causing substances) called **tobacco-specific nitrosamines**, directly to the mouth lining for hours a day. It causes:

- **Mouth cancer.** Oral cancer is among the most common cancers in Indian men, largely because of chewed tobacco and areca nut ([[23 - Cancer - How It Starts and How to Catch It Early]]).
- **Heart attacks and strokes.** Nicotine raises heart rate and BP, narrows blood vessels and makes blood more likely to clot; smokeless tobacco is linked to higher risk of heart disease and death after a heart attack ([[19 - How Heart Disease Actually Happens]]).
- **Worse diabetes** and insulin resistance ([[18 - Blood Sugar, Insulin Resistance & Diabetes]]).
- **Gum disease, tooth loss** and **pre-cancerous mouth patches**.
- **Harm in pregnancy:** low birth weight and stillbirth.

### Areca nut (supari) on its own
**Areca nut** — supari, the nut in paan, gutkha and many "mouth fresheners" such as sweet supari and pan masala — is classified by the WHO's cancer agency (IARC) as a **Group 1 carcinogen**, meaning there's sufficient evidence it causes cancer in humans, **even without tobacco**. It also causes:
- **Oral submucous fibrosis**: a progressive stiffening and scarring of the inner cheeks that makes it gradually harder to open the mouth, and which can turn into cancer.
- Links with metabolic syndrome and heart disease.

"Pan masala without tobacco" and sweet supari aren't safe alternatives.

### Smoking
Smoking damages the lungs (**COPD**, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, a permanent narrowing and damage of the airways; and lung cancer), the heart and blood vessels, and raises the risk of at least a dozen cancers. **Second-hand smoke** harms everyone in the house, especially children.

## 2. Warning signs in tobacco and supari users

See a doctor or dentist promptly for:
- A **white or red patch** in the mouth that doesn't go away within two weeks
- A **mouth ulcer** that doesn't heal within two weeks
- A **lump** in the mouth, cheek or neck
- **Difficulty opening the mouth** fully, or burning with spicy food
- **Persistent hoarseness**, cough or coughing blood
- **Difficulty swallowing**

A regular **oral examination** by a dentist or doctor is a free, quick and lifesaving screen for every tobacco or areca nut user.

## 3. Quitting: what actually works

Nicotine is genuinely addictive: it acts on the brain's dopamine reward system within seconds ([[10 - The Brain & Nervous System]]), and withdrawal causes cravings, irritability, poor concentration, low mood and increased appetite for a few weeks. Most people need several attempts. Each attempt raises the chance of success.

**The benefits start quickly.** Heart rate and BP begin to fall within hours to days; within about a year, the excess risk of coronary heart disease roughly halves; cancer and stroke risk fall steadily over the following years. It's never too late; quitting after a heart attack sharply reduces the chance of another.

### Steps that help
1. **Set a quit date**, ideally within the next two weeks. Quitting completely on a set day tends to work better than slowly cutting down, though gradual reduction toward a quit date also helps some people.
2. **Tell family and friends**, and ask them not to offer tobacco.
3. **Remove all tobacco**, pouches, lighters, spittoons and supari from the home and pockets.
4. **Know your triggers** — after meals, with tea, with stress, with certain friends, at the farm or shop — and plan a replacement for each: saunf, elaichi, laung (clove), roasted chana, sugar-free chewing gum, a glass of water, a short walk.
5. **Quit together** if two people in the house use tobacco.
6. **Use medicines** — they roughly double the chance of success:
   - **Nicotine replacement therapy (NRT)**: nicotine gum, lozenges or patches, available at pharmacies in India. They supply nicotine without the toxic chemicals and are gradually reduced over 8–12 weeks.
   - **Prescription medicines** such as **varenicline** and **bupropion**, which reduce cravings. These need a doctor's advice.
7. **Get counselling support**:
   - **National Tobacco Quitline**: toll-free **1800-11-2356**, with counsellors in many languages and follow-up calls.
   - **mCessation**: give a missed call to **011-22901701** to register for text-message support.
   - Tobacco cessation centres at many government hospitals and medical colleges.
8. **Expect a slip** and treat it as learning, not failure. Restart immediately.

**Weight gain** of a few kilos is common after quitting because nicotine suppresses appetite. The health benefit of quitting vastly outweighs it; use the eating plan in [[11 - The Indian Plate - Rebuilding the Thali]] and keep moving.

## 4. Alcohol

### There's no completely safe level
The World Health Organization stated in 2023 that **no level of alcohol consumption is safe** for health, mainly because alcohol is a cause of at least seven types of cancer even at low intake. Risk rises with the amount.

Alcohol is broken down in the liver into **acetaldehyde**, a toxic, cancer-causing chemical. Regular drinking causes:
- **Liver disease**: fatty liver, alcoholic hepatitis and cirrhosis ([[08 - The Liver & Kidneys - Factories and Filters]])
- **Cancers**: mouth, throat, oesophagus, liver, bowel and breast
- **High blood pressure** and **atrial fibrillation** (irregular heartbeat)
- **Pancreatitis** (painful inflammation of the pancreas)
- **Poor sleep**, depression and anxiety ([[14 - Sleep & Circadian Rhythm]], [[15 - Stress, Mental Health & Social Connection]])
- **Accidents, violence and road deaths**
- **Weight gain** (alcohol has about 7 calories per gram) and raised triglycerides

The old idea that moderate drinking protects the heart has largely fallen apart under better research.

### If people do drink
- **Less is better.** Fewer drinking days and smaller amounts.
- **A standard drink** contains about 10 g of pure alcohol — roughly one small peg (30 ml) of whisky, a small glass of wine, or a third to half a bottle of regular beer. Large bottles of strong beer contain several standard drinks.
- **Avoid binge drinking** (several drinks in one sitting).
- **Never drink and drive.**
- **Never drink unlicensed or illicit country liquor**, which can contain **methanol** and has caused repeated mass poisonings in India, leading to blindness and death.
- **Don't drink at all** if pregnant, if taking medicines that interact with alcohol (including some sleeping pills, painkillers and diabetes medicines), or with liver disease.

**Signs of problem drinking:** needing alcohol to start the day, drinking more over time, failed attempts to cut down, shaking or sweating when not drinking, or drinking affecting work and family. Treatment works, and sudden stopping after heavy daily drinking can be dangerous (causing seizures or delirium), so it should be medically supervised.

## 5. Air pollution

India has some of the most polluted air in the world, especially across the Indo-Gangetic plain in winter.

**PM2.5** — fine particles smaller than 2.5 micrometres, about a thirtieth of a hair's width — pass deep into the lungs and into the bloodstream. They cause:
- Lung disease (asthma, COPD, lung cancer)
- Heart attacks and strokes, by inflaming blood vessels ([[06 - The Immune System & Inflammation]])
- Possible links with diabetes, dementia and low birth weight

Studies estimate air pollution contributes to well over a million deaths a year in India.

### Household air pollution: the chulha
Cooking on a **chulha** (traditional stove) with wood, dung cakes, crop waste or coal fills the kitchen with smoke, often at levels many times higher than outdoor pollution. Women and young children near the cooking fire bear the heaviest exposure. It's a major cause of **COPD in non-smoking Indian women**, pneumonia in children, and heart disease.

**What helps:**
- **Switch to LPG** or electric cooking wherever possible (subsidised connections through the Pradhan Mantri Ujjwala Yojana).
- If a chulha must be used, use an **improved cookstove** with a chimney, cook in a well-ventilated area or outdoors, and keep children away.
- **Never burn plastic or crop residue** near the house.

### Outdoor pollution
- **Check the AQI** (air quality index) on phone apps such as SAMEER (from the Central Pollution Control Board).
- On "poor" or worse days: exercise indoors or at lower intensity, keep windows closed during peak hours, and consider an **N95 mask** outdoors, especially for people with heart or lung disease.
- **Indoor air purifiers** with HEPA filters help in bedrooms if affordable.
- **Indoor plants** don't meaningfully clean indoor air despite popular belief.

## 6. Water, food and farm exposures

- **Arsenic in groundwater** is a known problem in parts of West Bengal, Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, Assam and other states. Long-term exposure causes skin changes, cancers and heart disease. **Fluoride** excess in groundwater (Rajasthan, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana and elsewhere) causes dental and skeletal fluorosis. In affected areas, test the water (government Jal Jeevan Mission testing labs and field kits) and use safe sources or appropriate filters.
- **Pesticides:** farming families handle insecticides and herbicides that can cause acute poisoning and, with long-term exposure, possible links to some cancers and Parkinson's disease. Use gloves, a mask and full-sleeved clothing while spraying; spray with the wind behind you; wash hands and change clothes afterwards; never store pesticides in the house, kitchen, or in food or drink containers; and keep them locked away from children (this is also an important suicide-prevention step, [[15 - Stress, Mental Health & Social Connection]]).
- **Lead:** found in some adulterated spices (turmeric has been adulterated with lead chromate for colour), some traditional medicines and cosmetics (some sindoor, surma or kajal), old paints and battery recycling. Buy spices and medicines from reliable sources.
- **Aflatoxin** in mouldy grain and peanuts damages the liver ([[08 - The Liver & Kidneys - Factories and Filters]]).
- **Heat:** rising temperatures make heat stress a major risk for outdoor workers and older adults ([[25 - Fast Killers - Emergencies & Warning Signs]]).
- **Noise:** prolonged loud noise (machinery, traffic, loudspeakers) damages hearing permanently, and hearing loss is linked to dementia ([[10 - The Brain & Nervous System]]).

---

## The layer, in one line

Tobacco in any form, including smokeless tobacco and areca nut, is one of the biggest preventable causes of cancer, heart attacks and stroke in India, and quitting works best with a quit date, medicines and counselling; alcohol has no truly safe level; and chulha smoke, polluted air, contaminated groundwater and careless pesticide handling are environmental risks families can reduce.
