# Leader Election and Coordination

#backend #distributed-systems #coordination

> **In one line:** Electing a leader means getting a majority to agree, which is what consensus algorithms do — and the practical lesson is to use one rather than build one, and to design so that two leaders briefly existing is survivable.

---

## The Idea

```
20 pods, all running the same code.
Who runs the nightly billing job? Who is the scheduler? Who owns partition 3?
```

| Approach | |
|---|---|
| **Do not elect a leader** | Restructure so every pod can do the work safely |
| **Lease-based election** | Good enough when brief overlap is tolerable |
| **Consensus-based election** | When you need a real guarantee |

💡 **The first is usually available and usually better.** Most "we need leader election" problems are work-distribution problems in disguise, **and `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` solves them without electing anyone** ([[06.6 - Transactions and Isolation Levels]]).

---

## Consensus, Briefly

**Raft** is the algorithm to understand, because etcd, Consul and most modern systems use it.

```
1. Followers wait for a heartbeat from the leader
2. No heartbeat within the election timeout → become a candidate
3. Candidate increments the term, votes for itself, requests votes
4. A majority of votes → leader
5. Leader sends heartbeats to suppress new elections
```

**Terms.** A monotonically increasing number. Every message carries one, and a node seeing a higher term immediately steps down. **The term is a fencing token** ([[07.4 - Distributed Locks Done Right]]) — a stale leader's writes are rejected because its term is old.

**Majority.** Two disjoint majorities cannot exist, so **two leaders in the same term is impossible.**

⚠️ **Two leaders in *different* terms can briefly coexist.** A partitioned old leader does not know it has been replaced and may keep acting until it notices.

💡 **That is the crucial insight, and it generalises beyond Raft: consensus does not prevent a stale leader from existing, it prevents a stale leader from doing damage.** Your application must lean on the same idea.

### Quorum sizing

| Nodes | Majority | Failures tolerated |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 2 | 1 |
| 5 | 3 | 2 |
| 7 | 4 | 3 |

⚠️ **Even numbers give you nothing.** Four nodes need three for a majority and tolerate one failure — exactly like three, with more coordination cost. **Always an odd number.**

**Three or five is right for essentially every deployment.**

## Lease-Based Election

The pragmatic version, and what most systems actually need.

```python
async def campaign(self) -> bool:
    row = await self.conn.fetchrow(
        """
        INSERT INTO leases (name, holder, term, expires_at)
        VALUES ($1, $2, 1, now() + make_interval(secs => $3))
        ON CONFLICT (name) DO UPDATE
            SET holder = $2,
                term = leases.term + 1,
                expires_at = now() + make_interval(secs => $3)
            WHERE leases.expires_at < now() OR leases.holder = $2
        RETURNING holder, term
        """,
        self.name, self.node_id, self.ttl,
    )
    ...
```

**The `WHERE` clause on the upsert.** You can only take the lease if it has expired, or if you already hold it — **this makes the whole thing atomic.**

**The term increments on every takeover.** That is your fencing token.

**Renew at one third of the TTL.**

⚠️ **A lease-based leader can be superseded without knowing it** — a paused process, a slow network, a long GC. **The same pause problem as distributed locks, and it cannot be fixed from inside the process.**

💡 **So the leader's work must be fenced:**

```sql
UPDATE schedules SET last_run_at = now(), run_by_term = $2
WHERE id = $1 AND (run_by_term IS NULL OR run_by_term < $2)
RETURNING id;
```

⚠️ **Without this, "leader election" gives you "usually one leader", which is a fine thing to have and is not a guarantee.**

## Kubernetes Lease Election

```python
lease = client.V1Lease(
    metadata=client.V1ObjectMeta(name="conduit-scheduler"),
    spec=client.V1LeaseSpec(holder_identity=node_id, lease_duration_seconds=15,
                            renew_time=datetime.now(timezone.utc)),
)
```

💡 **Kubernetes runs etcd, and the `Lease` resource is a first-class, Raft-backed primitive.** Every Kubernetes controller uses it for exactly this. **If you are already on Kubernetes, do not run your own etcd or build a Postgres lease — use the one that is there.**

The `resourceVersion` on the Lease serves as a fencing token, and optimistic concurrency on update is built in.

## When You Do Not Need a Leader

**The most valuable section, because the answer is usually here.**

### Work distribution → `SKIP LOCKED`

```sql
UPDATE jobs SET status = 'running', worker_id = $1
WHERE id IN (
    SELECT id FROM jobs WHERE status = 'pending'
    ORDER BY created_at LIMIT 10 FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED
) RETURNING *;
```

**Every pod pulls work. No leader, no coordination, no election.** If a pod dies, its claimed work times out and another picks it up.

💡 **This handles most of what people reach for leader election to do**, and it is strictly more robust — **no single point of failure and no election window during which nothing happens.**

### Scheduled jobs → a claim on the schedule row

```sql
UPDATE schedules
SET last_run_at = now(), run_by = $1
WHERE name = $2 AND (last_run_at IS NULL OR last_run_at < now() - interval '1 hour')
RETURNING id;
```

**Whichever pod's update succeeds runs the job.** All others get zero rows and skip.

⚠️ **This is at-least-once, not exactly-once** — a pod that crashes after claiming means the job does not run this hour. **Add a completion marker if that matters.**

💡 **Far simpler than leader election and it fails better.** There is no leader to lose, no election to wait for, and the job simply runs from whichever pod won the race. **For Conduit's periodic tasks, this is the right answer.**

### Partitioned ownership → consistent hashing

```python
def owns(self, key: str) -> bool:
    return hash_ring.get_node(key) == self.node_id
```

⚠️ **Membership changes need a consistent view**, or two pods briefly believe they own the same key. Kafka's consumer group protocol solves this properly ([[09.1 - Queue vs Log]]).

### Singleton work → make it idempotent

💡 **If the work is idempotent, running it twice is harmless and you do not need a leader at all** ([[07.5 - Idempotency Keys and Deduplication]]).

**This is the strongest answer available.** "Recompute the daily aggregate" run twice produces the same aggregate; "send the daily summary email" run twice does not — **but that can be made idempotent with a dedup record keyed on the date.**

## In Production

### Choosing

```
Does the work need to happen exactly once?
├── NO (idempotent, or duplicates are harmless)
│   └── No leader. Every pod does it, or SKIP LOCKED distributes it
│
└── YES
    ├── Can the resource enforce a fence (term/version)?
    │   └── Lease-based election + fencing. Postgres or K8s Lease
    └── NO
        └── You cannot guarantee it. Restructure so you can,
            or accept at-least-once and deduplicate
```

⚠️ **The last branch is the honest one and it is where most systems land.** Consensus gives you a leader; **it does not give you exactly-once execution of side effects.** That still requires idempotency at the resource.

### What a lease-based leader must have

```
□  TTL
□  Term / epoch, incremented on every takeover — your fencing token
□  Renewal with loss notification
□  Fencing at the resource
□  Graceful handover — release on shutdown
□  Metrics: leadership changes, term, renewal failures
```

```python
leader_changes_total.inc()
is_leader.set(1 if leader else 0)
leaderless_duration_seconds.observe(gap)
fenced_writes_total.inc()          # ← a stale leader tried to write
```

⚠️ **`fenced_writes_total` being non-zero means you had two leaders.** It is the metric that tells you whether your election is actually working, **and almost nobody exports it.**

💡 **`leaderless_duration_seconds` is the availability metric.** Between a leader dying and a new one being elected, the work is not happening. **Tune the TTL against how quickly you want to detect a real failure.**

### Graceful handover

```python
async def shutdown(self):
    if self.is_leader:
        await self.conn.execute(
            "UPDATE leases SET expires_at = now() WHERE name = $1 AND holder = $2",
            self.name, self.node_id)
```

💡 **Releasing the lease on shutdown turns a 15-second leaderless gap into a sub-second one during a deploy.** Three lines, **and it removes most of the observable impact of leader election from your rollouts.**

### Split brain

```python
async def do_leader_work(self):
    while self.is_leader:
        if not await self.renew():
            self.is_leader = False
            log.warning("lost leadership, stopping work")
            return                          # stop immediately
        await self.one_unit_of_work()
```

**Self-demotion narrows the window. Fencing closes it. You want both.**

### Do not run your own etcd

⚠️ **Operating a consensus cluster is a genuine specialisation** — quorum loss, disk performance requirements, member replacement, backup and restore of the Raft log.

| Situation | Use |
|---|---|
| **On Kubernetes** | **The `Lease` resource.** etcd is already there |
| Using Postgres | A lease table, or advisory locks |
| Using Consul or etcd already | Their primitives |
| None of the above | Postgres. **Adding etcd for one lease is not proportionate** |

💡 **A Postgres row with a TTL and a term column gives you the same practical guarantee, with infrastructure you already operate and back up.**

### Conduit's coordination

| Need | Mechanism | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Distributing document processing | `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` | No leader needed |
| Periodic source syncs | Claim on the schedule row | Simpler than election, fails better |
| Celery beat scheduler | K8s `Lease`, or a claim row | The one genuine singleton |
| Kafka partition ownership | Consumer group protocol | Handled by Kafka |
| Cache refresher | Any pod, idempotent | No coordination |

💡 **Exactly one of these needs a leader**, and even that one is a scheduler whose work is individually idempotent. **That distribution is what a well-designed system looks like — coordination is expensive and most work does not need it.**

## Codebase

**Repo:** `etcd-io/raft`, `etcd-io/etcd`, `kubernetes/client-go`

**Question to answer:** In Raft, what prevents two leaders in the same term, and what happens when a partitioned old leader tries to write? Then: how does the Kubernetes leader election library handle the gap between losing a lease and noticing?

**Read, in order:**

1. **The Raft paper** (`raft.github.io`) — **read this before any code.** Deliberately written to be understandable, and **Figure 2 is the entire algorithm on one page**
2. `etcd-io/raft/raft.go` — **the state machine.** `becomeFollower`, `becomeCandidate`, `becomeLeader`, and `Step`. **The answer is in `Step`**: any message with a higher term causes an immediate step-down
3. `etcd-io/raft/log.go` — the log and commit index
4. `kubernetes/client-go/tools/leaderelection/leaderelection.go` — **`renew` and `OnStoppedLeading`.** Note the `RenewDeadline`: **the library stops leading before the lease actually expires**, leaving safety margin
5. `kubernetes/client-go/tools/leaderelection/resourcelock/leaselock.go` — the `Lease` object and its optimistic concurrency

💡 The `RenewDeadline` design is worth copying: **stop leading at, say, 10 seconds even though the lease lasts 15.** The five-second margin means that by the time another node can acquire the lease, you have already stopped. **It converts an unbounded overlap into a bounded one, and it costs nothing.**

**Size:** 2 sessions.


**Part 1 — Build a Postgres lease.** Terms, TTL, renewal with loss notification, graceful handover, full metrics. **Then fencing at the resource.**

**Part 2 — Break it three ways.** Five nodes competing.

| Failure | Measure |
|---|---|
| **Leader crash** (`kill -9`) | Leaderless duration, leadership changes |
| **Leader pause** (`SIGSTOP` past the TTL) | Whether two nodes believe they lead, and for how long |
| **Network partition** | Same, plus behaviour on heal |

**For the pause case, confirm the old leader attempts a write on resume.** Then confirm fencing rejects it and check `fenced_writes_total`.

**This is the experiment that proves your election is safe rather than lucky.**

**Part 3 — TTL tuning.** Sweep 5, 15, 30, 60 seconds.

**Chart failover time after a crash, and false failovers under simulated network jitter (200ms–2s).**

**Find the TTL that fails over quickly without flapping.** Then add the `RenewDeadline` margin and measure the overlap window with and without it.

**Part 4 — Graceful handover.** A rolling deploy of five nodes with a 15-second TTL.

**Measure total leaderless time with and without lease release on shutdown. It should drop from tens of seconds to under a second.** Chart both.

**Part 5 — Three alternatives to election.** The same three requirements without a leader:

- **Work distribution:** `SKIP LOCKED` versus a leader assigning work
- **Periodic job:** a schedule-row claim versus a leader running cron
- **Cache refresh:** idempotent, every pod, versus a leader refreshing

For each measure: correctness under node failure, time to recover, complexity in lines, and behaviour during a deploy.

**The no-leader versions should win on at least two of the four columns each. Write down which and why.**

**Part 6 — A toy Raft.** Election only — terms, votes, heartbeats, election timeouts. Five nodes, in-process, with simulated message loss and delay.

Test: normal election, leader failure, a split vote requiring a retry, and a partition producing two terms.

**Assert that two leaders never share a term.**

⚠️ **Do not implement log replication.** Election alone is enough to understand the mechanism.

**Part 7 — Conduit's coordination audit.** List every place Conduit needs coordination, apply the decision tree, and choose a mechanism.

**For every case where you chose leader election, justify why the no-leader alternatives were insufficient.**

**The target is that at most one requires an actual leader. If more do, look harder at the alternatives.**

**Done when:**
- Your lease survives crash, pause and partition, with fencing proven by metrics
- You know the TTL that balances failover speed against flapping on your network
- Graceful handover measurably shortens deploy leaderless time
- The three-alternative comparison shows no-leader designs winning on most axes
- Your toy Raft never elects two leaders in one term
- Conduit's audit ends with at most one genuine leader requirement
