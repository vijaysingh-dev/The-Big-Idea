# Opportunity Teams and Opportunity Splits

## Two features, two different problems

They are frequently confused because they look similar and are configured near each other.

**Opportunity Teams** answer *who can see and work on this deal*. They are an access mechanism.

**Opportunity Splits** answer *who gets credit for the revenue*. They are a compensation and reporting mechanism.

A person can be on the team without a split, and — depending on configuration — can have a split without being on the team. In practice splits require team membership, which is one of the reasons they are enabled together.

## Opportunity Teams

A set of users attached to an opportunity, each with a role and an access level on that opportunity.

```
Opportunity: Northwind - Press Line Upgrade
  Account Executive       Read/Write
  Solutions Engineer      Read/Write
  Legal Reviewer          Read
  Executive Sponsor       Read
```

This is the mechanism that makes enterprise selling work under a Private opportunity model. A deal genuinely involves four to eight people from different parts of the organization; the role hierarchy cannot express that, and a sharing rule granting all solutions engineers access to all opportunities is far too broad.

Access appears in the share table with `RowCause = 'Team'`.

```sql
SELECT OpportunityId, UserId, User.Name, TeamMemberRole, OpportunityAccessLevel
FROM OpportunityTeamMember
WHERE OpportunityId = :opportunityId
```

**Default opportunity teams** work like default account teams: a user configures the people who should be added to every opportunity they own. This is what makes teams practical, because manual assembly per deal does not happen consistently.

Adding members in code:

```java
public with sharing class OpportunityTeamService {

    public static void addTeamMembers(Map<Id, Id> userByOpportunity, String role, String accessLevel) {
        List<OpportunityTeamMember> members = new List<OpportunityTeamMember>();

        for (Id oppId : userByOpportunity.keySet()) {
            members.add(new OpportunityTeamMember(
                OpportunityId = oppId,
                UserId = userByOpportunity.get(oppId),
                TeamMemberRole = role,
                OpportunityAccessLevel = accessLevel
            ));
        }

        // partial success so an existing membership does not abort the batch
        Database.insert(members, false);
    }
}
```

Inserting a team member with an access level creates the share row automatically — you do not insert `OpportunityShare` separately.

## Opportunity Splits

Splits allocate the opportunity's revenue among team members. Two kinds, and the distinction matters:

**Revenue splits** must total 100 percent. The opportunity amount is divided among the participants, and the sum of splits equals the deal value. Used when several people share the same revenue number for quota purposes.

**Overlay splits** can total any percentage, including more than 100. Used for people who influence deals without owning the revenue — a product specialist, a pre-sales engineer, an overlay team whose quota is measured on influenced revenue rather than owned revenue.

You can define multiple split types. A typical enterprise configuration:

```
Revenue Split (must total 100%)
  Account Executive        70%
  Regional Specialist      30%

Overlay Split (any total)
  Solutions Engineer      100%
  Product Specialist       50%
```

The account executive and specialist share the revenue for quota. The solutions engineer and product specialist are each measured on the deal separately, without diluting anyone's revenue number.

```sql
SELECT OpportunityId, SplitOwnerId, SplitOwner.Name, SplitPercentage, SplitAmount,
       SplitType.MasterLabel
FROM OpportunitySplit
WHERE OpportunityId = :opportunityId
```

## The behaviour that catches people

**A default 100 percent split is created for the owner automatically** when revenue splits are enabled. Every opportunity has at least one split row, whether anyone configured it or not.

**Changing the opportunity owner reassigns the split.** The default split follows ownership.

**Splits recalculate when `Amount` changes.** `SplitAmount` is derived from `SplitPercentage` and the opportunity amount, so an amount change rewrites every split row on that opportunity.

**Enabling splits on an org with many opportunities triggers a substantial background job**, creating default split rows for every existing record. On a large org this takes hours and should be scheduled.

**Splits are a separate object with its own DML cost.** Bulk-updating opportunity amounts cascades into split updates, which is a common cause of exceeding limits during data loads or mass updates.

That last point deserves attention when writing batch jobs. Updating 200 opportunities with splits means the platform also rewrites their split rows, and the CPU and DML cost is not visible in your code.

## When to use each

**Use teams when** several people need access to a deal, and the set varies per deal. This is nearly every enterprise sales organization.

**Use revenue splits when** quota credit is genuinely shared and the compensation plan divides one deal's revenue among people.

**Use overlay splits when** specialists carry a quota measured on influenced revenue.

**Use neither when** deals have a single owner and a simple compensation plan. Splits add real complexity — extra objects, extra DML, extra reporting surface — and enabling them for a business that does not need them is a common over-engineering mistake.

The question to ask the business: *does more than one person's compensation depend on this single deal's revenue?* If no, splits are not needed. Team membership alone handles the access requirement.

## Splits and forecasting

Forecasting can be configured to run on splits rather than on opportunity ownership. That is the point of the feature — a manager's forecast rolls up the split amounts attributed to their team, not the full value of deals their reps happen to own.

Getting this right requires the forecast type, the split type, and the compensation plan to agree. When they disagree, reps see forecast numbers that do not match their commission statement, and trust in the system collapses quickly. See [[04.13 - Forecasting and Revenue Management]].

## Reporting on splits

The most common request is credited revenue per person, which is a split-level query rather than an opportunity-level one:

```sql
SELECT SplitOwner.Name owner, SUM(SplitAmount) creditedRevenue, COUNT(Id) dealCount
FROM OpportunitySplit
WHERE Opportunity.IsWon = true
  AND Opportunity.CloseDate = THIS_FISCAL_QUARTER
  AND SplitType.MasterLabel = 'Revenue Split'
GROUP BY SplitOwner.Name
ORDER BY SUM(SplitAmount) DESC
```

Note that reporting on opportunities and reporting on splits give different totals, legitimately. Summing opportunity amounts counts each deal once. Summing revenue splits also totals the deal value, because they sum to 100 percent. Summing overlay splits does not correspond to any real revenue figure at all, and presenting it as though it does is a reporting mistake that causes real confusion.

## Practical guidance

**Enable teams before splits.** Access is the more universal need, and splits without team access produce people credited for deals they cannot see.

**Encourage default teams.** Manual assembly per deal is unreliable.

**Enable splits only if compensation genuinely requires them.** The complexity is real and permanent.

**Schedule the initial split calculation** for a quiet period if enabling on an existing org.

**Account for split DML in batch jobs** that touch opportunity amounts. The hidden cost catches people at scale.

**Keep split types few.** Two — revenue and one overlay — covers most businesses. Five split types is a reporting problem.
