# Cardio, Mobility & Balance

*Health & Fitness · 2026-10-30*

Strength builds the engine's frame; cardio builds the engine itself. Add mobility and balance, and you have the four qualities that decide whether someone at 75 walks to the market alone or needs help getting out of bed. The good news is that all four can be trained in under an hour a day, most of it while doing things you already do.

---

## 1. What "cardio" actually trains

**Aerobic exercise** ("cardio") is sustained rhythmic activity that raises heart rate and breathing for minutes at a time: brisk walking, cycling, swimming, dancing, climbing stairs, running.

It improves:
- **The heart:** a stronger heart pumps more blood per beat, so resting heart rate falls ([[05 - Blood, Circulation & the Heart]]).
- **Blood vessels:** the lining becomes healthier and more flexible, and BP falls, typically by around 5–8 mmHg in people with high BP — similar to some BP medicines.
- **Muscles:** more mitochondria and capillaries, so muscles burn fat and glucose more efficiently ([[03 - Energy Metabolism - How the Body Makes and Uses Fuel]]).
- **Blood sugar and insulin sensitivity** ([[18 - Blood Sugar, Insulin Resistance & Diabetes]]).
- **Cholesterol:** modestly lowers triglycerides and raises HDL.
- **The brain:** more blood flow, better mood, better sleep, lower dementia risk ([[10 - The Brain & Nervous System]]).

**Cardiorespiratory fitness** (how well the heart, lungs and muscles use oxygen during effort) is one of the strongest predictors of how long people live. In large studies, the least fit people have a substantially higher risk of death than even moderately fit people, and the biggest gain comes from moving out of the least fit group.

## 2. How hard is "moderate"?

Intensity matters, and most daily activity is lighter than people assume. Simple ways to judge it:

**The talk test**
- **Light:** you can sing.
- **Moderate:** you can talk in full sentences but not sing. Breathing is noticeably faster.
- **Vigorous:** you can say only a few words before needing a breath.

**Rate of perceived exertion (RPE)**, on a 0–10 scale:
- 3–4 = moderate
- 6–7 = vigorous

**Heart rate**, as a rough guide: moderate is about 60–75% of maximum heart rate; vigorous is above that. The old formula "220 minus age" gives an approximate maximum. For a 60-year-old: about 160, so moderate is roughly 95–120 beats per minute. People on BP medicines called **beta-blockers** have a blunted heart rate response and should use the talk test instead.

Slow strolling, light housework and pottering in the fields are **light** activity: valuable, but not cardio training. A brisk walk where breathing is clearly faster is **moderate**.

## 3. How much is enough

WHO recommends, for adults including older adults:
- **150–300 minutes a week of moderate aerobic activity**, or **75–150 minutes of vigorous activity**, or a mix.
- Plus **strength training on 2 or more days** ([[12 - Strength Training - Muscle as Medicine]]).
- For older adults, **balance training on 3 or more days** a week.
- **Less sitting:** replace sitting time with any movement.

150 minutes is 30 minutes on 5 days. It can be split into 10-minute bouts. Any amount is better than none: even 10–15 minutes a day of brisk walking is linked to meaningfully lower risk of death compared with doing nothing.

### Steps
Steps are a convenient everyday measure. Large studies suggest the benefit rises up to roughly 7,000–10,000 steps a day for most adults, with diminishing returns above that, and that older adults gain most of the benefit at around 6,000–8,000. A cheap pedometer or a phone app is enough to track it. **Pace matters too:** brisk steps count more than slow ones.

## 4. The highest-value cardio habits for Indian families

- **A brisk morning or evening walk**: 30 minutes at a pace where talking is possible but singing isn't. Pick a flat, safe route; walk with a partner or neighbour to make it social and sticky.
- **Walking after meals:** 10–15 minutes after lunch and dinner noticeably lowers the blood sugar spike. One of the most effective and easy habits for anyone with prediabetes or diabetes.
- **Stairs:** climbing stairs is excellent vigorous-intensity exercise in short bursts.
- **Cycling** for errands where roads are safe.
- **Intervals for the fitter:** alternating 1–2 minutes of fast walking with 1–2 minutes of easy walking. Interval training improves fitness faster than steady exercise and is safe for most people, including many with heart disease under medical guidance.
- **Yoga** improves flexibility, balance and calm; faster flowing styles (such as surya namaskar done continuously) also give some cardio benefit.
- **Dance, sport and active play** count.

### Heat, pollution and safety in India
- In summer, exercise early morning or after sunset; drink water; stop if dizzy. Older adults are more vulnerable to heat illness ([[25 - Fast Killers - Emergencies & Warning Signs]]).
- On high-pollution days (check the AQI, air quality index; above roughly 200 is "poor"), exercise indoors or reduce intensity — heavy breathing draws pollutants deeper into the lungs.
- Walk facing traffic where there's no footpath, wear something bright at dawn and dusk, and use well-lit routes.

## 5. Mobility and flexibility

**Mobility** is the ability to move each joint through its full range with control; **flexibility** is how far muscles can lengthen ([[09 - Muscles, Bones & Joints - How the Frame Works]]). Both are lost quietly when ranges aren't used, and both are regained with regular gentle practice.

A simple daily 10-minute routine, done slowly without bouncing:
1. **Neck:** slow turns side to side, and tilting ear towards shoulder.
2. **Shoulders:** circles forwards and backwards; reaching arms overhead.
3. **Spine:** seated or standing twists; cat–cow on hands and knees (if the knees allow).
4. **Hips:** leg swings holding a wall; a gentle lunge stretch for the front of the hip.
5. **Hamstrings:** sitting on a chair edge with one leg straight, leaning forward gently.
6. **Calves and ankles:** calf stretch against a wall; ankle circles.
7. **Squat practice:** a supported squat holding a doorframe, to a comfortable depth.

**Hold stretches for 20–30 seconds**, breathing slowly. Stretching should feel like gentle tension, never sharp pain.

**Yoga** combines mobility, flexibility, balance and breathing and is widely available in India through community classes and free resources. People with knee or back problems, osteoporosis, glaucoma or uncontrolled BP should avoid extreme bends, inversions and deep knee flexion unless a trained instructor adapts them.

## 6. Balance: fall prevention

Falls are a leading cause of injury and loss of independence in older adults. A hip fracture after 70 often means lasting disability. Balance declines with age, with weaker legs, poor vision, numb feet (from diabetes or B12 deficiency), some medicines (sleeping pills, some BP pills) and inactivity — but it improves quickly with practice.

**Balance exercises**, done near a wall or counter for support, 3 or more days a week:
- **Standing on one leg:** build up to 30 seconds each side.
- **Heel-to-toe walking:** walking in a straight line, placing the heel of one foot directly in front of the toes of the other.
- **Sit-to-stand without hands** ([[12 - Strength Training - Muscle as Medicine]]).
- **Side-stepping** and walking backwards slowly.
- **Tai chi** has strong evidence for reducing falls in older adults.

**Simple tests to track:**
- Can you stand on one leg for 10 seconds or more? Difficulty doing this in middle and older age is linked with higher risk.
- Can you get up from the floor without using your hands, or with only one hand? Practising getting up and down from the floor safely is a valuable skill for older adults.

**Fall-proofing the home** matters as much as exercise: good lighting (especially at night on the way to the toilet), non-slip mats in the bathroom, grab bars near the toilet and bathing area, removing loose rugs and clutter, sturdy footwear, and regular eye checks.

## 7. A full weekly plan for an older adult

| Day | Cardio | Strength | Balance & mobility |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | 30 min brisk walk | 20–30 min home routine | 10 min |
| Tue | 30 min brisk walk | — | 10 min |
| Wed | 30 min brisk walk | 20–30 min home routine | 10 min |
| Thu | 30 min brisk walk or yoga | — | 10 min |
| Fri | 30 min brisk walk | 20–30 min home routine | 10 min |
| Sat | Longer walk or active outing | — | 10 min |
| Sun | Rest or easy walk | — | 10 min |
| Daily | 10–15 min walk after lunch and dinner | | |

Farm and household work counts as light-to-moderate activity on top of this, not instead of it.

## 8. Safety

Moderate walking is safe for nearly everyone. Get a doctor's advice before starting vigorous exercise if you have heart disease, chest pain on exertion, uncontrolled BP, diabetes with complications, or recent surgery ([[12 - Strength Training - Muscle as Medicine]]). After a heart attack or stent, **cardiac rehabilitation** — a supervised exercise programme — reduces deaths and repeat events and is strongly recommended.

**Stop and seek help** with chest pain or pressure, unusual breathlessness, dizziness or fainting, palpitations, or pain spreading to the arm, jaw or back.

---

## The layer, in one line

Aerobic fitness is built by 150+ minutes a week of breath-quickening activity (brisk walks, stairs, walks after meals), mobility by moving joints through their full range daily, and balance by a few minutes of practice most days — and together with strength training they decide whether old age is independent.
