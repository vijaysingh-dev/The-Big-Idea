# Gut Health & Digestion

*Health & Fitness · 2026-10-30*

"Gut health" has become a marketing phrase used to sell probiotic drinks, but underneath the hype is something real and important: a long tube that decides what actually enters your body, a liver that processes all of it, and trillions of bacteria that turn fibre into compounds your body runs on. In India, it also includes some very old problems — worms, contaminated water, and acid tablets taken like sweets.

---

## 1. The tube and its stations

[[01 - Human Body & Nutrition Basics]] introduced the idea that what you absorb matters as much as what you eat. The digestive tract is where that happens, and each section has a job:

- **Mouth:** chewing breaks food down; saliva starts digesting starch.
- **Stomach:** strong acid kills many microbes and starts breaking down protein.
- **Small intestine:** where most nutrients are absorbed. Bile from the liver emulsifies fat (breaks it into tiny droplets so enzymes can reach it), and pancreatic enzymes finish digesting carbohydrate, protein and fat. Its lining has finger-like projections (**villi**) that make the absorbing surface enormous.
- **Large intestine (colon):** absorbs water and is home to most of the gut's bacteria, which ferment what the small intestine couldn't digest, mainly fibre.
- **Liver:** everything absorbed from the gut passes through the liver first via a dedicated vein (the **portal vein**). The liver processes nutrients, stores glucose as glycogen, makes cholesterol and lipoproteins, and detoxifies drugs and alcohol. [[08 - The Liver & Kidneys - Factories and Filters]] goes deeper.

## 2. The microbiome: an organ you didn't know you had

The **gut microbiome** is the community of bacteria, fungi and other microbes living mostly in the colon — tens of trillions of cells, with a combined genetic toolkit far larger than our own. As the first note said, **diversity** (many different species) is the most consistent marker of a healthy microbiome.

What these microbes do for us:

- **Ferment fibre into short-chain fatty acids.** Fibre we can't digest is food for gut bacteria. They turn it into **short-chain fatty acids** (SCFAs), especially **butyrate**, the main fuel for the cells lining the colon. SCFAs strengthen the gut barrier, calm inflammation and help regulate blood sugar and appetite.
- **Guard the barrier.** A healthy microbiome crowds out harmful microbes and helps keep the gut lining tight. When the barrier weakens, bacterial fragments leak into the blood and trigger low-grade inflammation ([[06 - The Immune System & Inflammation]]), one of the background drivers of insulin resistance and heart disease ([[18 - Blood Sugar, Insulin Resistance & Diabetes]]).
- **Train the immune system.** Most of the body's immune tissue sits along the gut.
- **Make vitamins** such as vitamin K and some B vitamins, and transform bile acids and plant compounds.
- **Talk to the brain.** Through the vagus nerve and signalling molecules, gut microbes influence mood and stress responses — the **gut-brain axis** ([[15 - Stress, Mental Health & Social Connection]]).

## 3. What feeds a healthy microbiome

### Fibre and plant variety — the main lever
Gut bacteria eat what you don't digest. A diet of refined grain gives them almost nothing. Different bacteria prefer different fibres, so **variety of plants** matters as much as quantity: dals and pulses, vegetables, fruit, whole grains, millets, nuts and seeds. A rough target some researchers use is 20–30 different plant foods a week, which is easy in a traditional Indian kitchen once vegetables and pulses are rotated (spices and herbs count too).

**Prebiotics** are fibres that specifically feed beneficial bacteria. Onion, garlic, banana (especially slightly unripe), oats, barley, legumes and cooled rice or potato are all good sources.

### Fermented foods
A 2021 Stanford trial published in *Cell* (Wastyk et al.) found that people who ate several servings of fermented foods daily for 10 weeks increased their microbiome diversity and lowered a range of inflammatory markers. India has a strong tradition here:

- **Dahi (curd), chaas (buttermilk), lassi** — the easiest daily habit.
- **Idli, dosa, dhokla, appam** — fermented batters.
- **Kanji** (fermented carrot or beetroot drink), traditional pickles fermented in brine rather than heavily salted and oiled.

Homemade curd set from a previous batch contains live bacteria. Pasteurised packaged curd often still does, but check for "live/active cultures."

### Probiotics (supplements)
**Probiotics** are live bacteria taken as capsules or drinks. The evidence is real for specific situations — for example, some strains reduce antibiotic-associated diarrhoea — but weak for "general gut health" in healthy people. Food first; take a probiotic only when a doctor suggests a specific one.

## 4. What harms the microbiome

- **Unnecessary antibiotics.** Antibiotics kill harmful and beneficial bacteria alike, and the microbiome can take months to recover. India has some of the highest antibiotic use in the world, often for viral colds and fevers where antibiotics do nothing. Over-use also breeds **antimicrobial resistance** (AMR) — bacteria that no longer respond to drugs, a growing cause of death in India. Take antibiotics only when prescribed, and complete the course as directed.
- **Very low-fibre, ultra-processed diets.** Biscuits, namkeen, maida-based snacks, sugary drinks.
- **Tobacco and heavy alcohol** ([[16 - Tobacco, Alcohol & Environmental Exposures]]).
- **Chronic stress and poor sleep** ([[14 - Sleep & Circadian Rhythm]]).

## 5. The Indian gut problems that matter most

### Contaminated water and food
**Typhoid**, **hepatitis A and E**, **cholera** and other diarrhoeal infections spread through contaminated water and food. They're still common, especially in the monsoon. Basics: boiled or filtered drinking water, handwashing with soap before eating and after the toilet, washing raw vegetables and fruit, and care with street food. Typhoid and hepatitis A have vaccines ([[17 - Infections, Hygiene & Vaccines]]).

### Intestinal worms
Worm infections (roundworm, hookworm, whipworm) are common in rural India, especially where people walk barefoot on soil, handle animals and manure, or eat unwashed produce. Hookworms feed on blood and are a major cause of **iron-deficiency anaemia**. A high **eosinophil** count on a blood report (eosinophils are white blood cells that respond to parasites and allergies) can be a clue. The government's National Deworming Day programme gives free albendazole to children; adults in high-exposure settings, such as farming families with livestock, should ask a doctor whether periodic deworming makes sense. Wearing footwear outdoors and washing hands after handling animals or soil cuts reinfection.

### *H. pylori*
***Helicobacter pylori*** is a bacterium that lives in the stomach lining. It's extremely common in India, often picked up in childhood. Most carriers never have symptoms, but it can cause gastritis (inflamed stomach lining) and peptic ulcers, and it raises the risk of stomach cancer. A breath or stool test detects it; it's treated with a combination of antibiotics and acid-reducing medicine.

### Acidity and the antacid habit
**Acidity**, heartburn and indigestion are common complaints, and acid-suppressing medicines called **PPIs** (proton pump inhibitors, such as pantoprazole and omeprazole) are widely taken without a prescription, sometimes daily for years. Short-term they're safe and effective. Long-term unsupervised use is linked to reduced absorption of vitamin B12, magnesium, calcium and iron, and to more gut infections. Common triggers worth fixing first: large late dinners, lying down soon after eating, tobacco, alcohol, excess tea or coffee, very spicy or oily meals, and excess weight around the belly.

**An important caution:** chest discomfort that feels like "gas" or "acidity," especially with sweating, breathlessness, or pain going to the arm, jaw or back, can be a heart attack. Many Indians die at home taking antacids for what was their heart. When in doubt, get an ECG immediately ([[25 - Fast Killers - Emergencies & Warning Signs]]).

### Fatty liver
**Fatty liver** — now formally called **MASLD** (metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease, the new name for non-alcoholic fatty liver) — is fat building up inside liver cells. It's often found on an abdominal ultrasound in people who don't drink. It's closely tied to insulin resistance and a high-refined-carb diet, and it's often the earliest visible sign of metabolic trouble, years before diabetes. Most people have no symptoms. In some it progresses to inflammation, scarring (**fibrosis**), and eventually **cirrhosis** (a severely scarred, failing liver) or liver cancer. The same levers that fix insulin resistance — weight off the waist, less refined carbohydrate and sugar, more activity — are the treatment.

### Constipation
Very common in older adults. Usual causes: too little fibre, too little water, too little movement, and some medicines (iron tablets, some BP and pain medicines). More vegetables, pulses, whole grains, fruit, isabgol (psyllium husk), water and daily walking usually fix it.

## 6. Red flags: when gut symptoms need a doctor

Most digestive complaints are harmless, but these can signal serious disease, including bowel, stomach or oesophageal cancer, and need prompt medical attention:

- **Blood in stool** (red, or black tar-like stool) or vomiting blood
- **Unexplained weight loss**
- **Difficulty or pain swallowing**, or food sticking
- **A persistent change in bowel habit** lasting more than a few weeks, especially after age 45
- **Persistent abdominal pain**, or pain that wakes you up at night
- **Yellowing of the eyes or skin** (jaundice)
- **New-onset anaemia** with no obvious cause
- **Persistent vomiting**

## 7. A gut-friendly day, Indian style

- A katori of curd or a glass of chaas daily.
- Dal or another pulse at both main meals.
- Half the plate vegetables, plus something raw.
- Mixed atta or millets for some of the grain.
- A whole fruit or two.
- A handful of nuts or seeds.
- Enough water across the day.
- Fermented foods (idli, dosa, dhokla, kanji) a few times a week.
- A walk after meals.

None of this costs extra. It mostly overlaps with [[11 - The Indian Plate - Rebuilding the Thali]] — the same plate that fixes blood sugar also feeds the gut.

---

## The layer, in one line

A healthy gut runs on fibre, plant variety and fermented foods, and is harmed by unnecessary antibiotics, ultra-processed food and tobacco; in India it also means clean water, hand hygiene, sensible deworming and not treating possible heart pain as "acidity."
