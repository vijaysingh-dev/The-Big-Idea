# Roles: ML Engineer vs AI Engineer vs Applied Scientist vs Research Engineer

## Role Comparison Table

|Dimension|ML Engineer|AI Engineer|Applied Scientist|Research Engineer|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**Core output**|Training pipelines, model serving infra|AI-powered products and features|Experiments, applied research papers|Research implementations, tooling for researchers|
|**Model training**|Yes, primary focus|Rarely|Yes, core work|Yes, supports researchers|
|**Product integration**|Sometimes|Always|Rarely|No|
|**Programming depth**|High (systems)|High (backend + AI)|Medium (prototyping)|Very high (CUDA, distributed)|
|**Math depth required**|High|Medium|Very high|Very high|
|**Typical output**|Feature store, training cluster, eval pipeline|RAG system, copilot, search feature|Novel method, ablation study|Efficient attention impl, distributed trainer|
|**Closest analogy**|DevOps for models|Backend engineer who speaks AI|Academic with industry constraints|SWE enabling researchers|

---

## ML Engineer

**What they build**: Systems that _produce_ models — data pipelines, feature engineering, distributed training infrastructure, model serving (low-latency inference), A/B testing frameworks for model rollouts.

**Key skills**: PyTorch/JAX internals, CUDA awareness, distributed systems, MLOps (MLflow, Kubeflow), data engineering.

**Does not typically**: Build the end-user product that uses the model.

---

## AI Engineer

**What they build**: Systems that _use_ models to solve user problems — retrieval pipelines, agent orchestration, context management, prompt systems, evaluation harnesses.

**Key skills**: Backend engineering, LLM APIs, RAG architecture, vector databases, system design, prompt engineering, evaluation.

**Does not typically**: Train models from scratch or write CUDA kernels.

**This is the role this guide prepares you for.**

---

## Applied Scientist

**What they build**: Novel applications of existing research, or applied research that advances a product. Bridges gap between pure research and engineering.

**Key skills**: Deep ML theory, statistics, experiment design, enough engineering to prototype. Often publishes papers.

**In practice**: At large companies (Google, Meta, Amazon), this is a distinct role. At startups, AI engineers often absorb applied scientist responsibilities.

---

## Research Engineer

**What they build**: Infrastructure that enables researchers to move faster — training frameworks, experiment tracking, efficient implementations of papers.

**Key skills**: Systems programming, CUDA, distributed training, deep ML internals. Supports researchers rather than building products.

**Typical employer**: Labs (Anthropic, OpenAI, DeepMind), large research teams at companies.

---

## Career Clarity for Backend Engineers

If you're a backend engineer moving into AI, **AI Engineer** is the natural entry point:

```
Backend Engineer
    │
    ├── Learn: LLM APIs, RAG, vector DBs, prompting
    ├── Build: AI features on top of existing backend skills
    └── Become: AI Engineer

    (later, optionally)
    ├── Deepen: ML theory, fine-tuning, training
    └── Shift toward: ML Engineer or Applied Scientist
```

Your existing skills in distributed systems, APIs, reliability, and production operations are **directly applicable** — in fact, they're what most pure ML people lack.

---

## What This Means for Interviews

When interviewing for "AI Engineer / GenAI / RAG" roles (like the one you were preparing for):

- They want **backend engineering + AI system design**, not ML theory
- Expect: RAG architecture questions, retrieval tradeoffs, agent design, LLM failure modes
- Rarely expect: Backprop derivations, custom CUDA, training loss analysis
- Your S&P Global experience (structured data, financial intelligence, reliability requirements) is a genuine differentiator
