# Sharing Rules

## What they solve

The role hierarchy grants access upward. Sharing rules grant access **sideways** — to people who are not above the owner in the hierarchy but still need the records.

The UK sales team and the UK service team are in different branches of the hierarchy. Service needs to see UK opportunities. No hierarchy change achieves that without making service report into sales, which is nonsense. A sharing rule does it directly.

## The two kinds

**Owner-based sharing rules.** Share records *owned by* one set of users with another set.

> Records owned by the "Sales Rep UK" role and subordinates are shared with the "UK Service Team" public group, with Read Only access.

**Criteria-based sharing rules.** Share records *matching a field condition* with a set of users, regardless of who owns them.

> Warranty Claims where `Priority__c = 'Critical'` are shared with the "Escalation Team" public group, with Read/Write access.

Criteria-based rules are the more powerful of the two, and they are the right answer whenever access should follow the data rather than the org structure.

## What they can share to

- Public groups
- Roles
- Roles and subordinates
- Territories and territories with subordinates
- Portal roles, for Experience Cloud

Note what is missing: **individual users.** Sharing rules cannot target a person. If a specific individual needs access, either put them in a public group or use a manual share. Public groups are almost always the better answer, because they survive the person leaving.

## Access levels

Read Only or Read/Write. That is all.

There is **no delete via sharing rule**. Delete access comes only from ownership, the role hierarchy above the owner, or Modify All on the object. This is worth knowing because "the team can edit but not delete each other's records" is the automatic behaviour, not something you configure.

## Criteria-based rules in practice

The criteria are field comparisons on the record, similar to a report filter.

```
Object: Warranty_Claim__c
Rule name: Critical_Claims_To_Escalation_Team
Criteria: Priority__c equals "Critical"
          AND Status__c not equal to "Closed"
Share with: Public Group "Escalation Team"
Access: Read/Write
```

The mechanism worth understanding: when a record's field values change so that it now matches the criteria, the platform adds share rows. When it stops matching, they are removed. This happens at step 13 of the order of execution, after the record is saved. See [[07.13 - Order of Execution]].

Two consequences.

**Recalculation is real work.** Every insert and update on the object evaluates every criteria-based rule on that object. Twenty rules on a high-volume object is twenty evaluations per save.

**Access changes automatically as data changes**, which is usually exactly what you want and occasionally surprising. A claim downgraded from Critical silently disappears from the escalation team's view.

Criteria-based rules have a limit — 50 per object — which is lower than the owner-based limit and is reached in complex orgs.

## A worked design

Requirement for our manufacturing org:

> Sales reps own accounts and opportunities in their territory. Service coordinators must see all machines and claims for accounts in their region. The escalation team must see any critical claim anywhere. Regional directors already see their own region through the hierarchy.

Design:

**OWD:** Account Public Read Only, Opportunity Private, Machine Private, Warranty Claim Private.

**Role hierarchy** covers directors seeing their reports' records. No rule needed.

**Owner-based rule on Machine:** records owned by "Service Coordinator EMEA" role and subordinates shared with "EMEA Sales" public group, Read Only. Sales can see the equipment at their accounts.

**Criteria-based rule on Warranty Claim:** where `Region__c = 'EMEA'`, share with "EMEA Service Team" public group, Read/Write. Coordinators see their region's claims regardless of who owns them.

**Criteria-based rule on Warranty Claim:** where `Priority__c = 'Critical'`, share with "Escalation Team" public group, Read/Write. Cuts across region entirely.

Note that the last two overlap, and that is fine. Sharing is additive, so a critical EMEA claim is accessible through both, at the higher of the two access levels.

## Sharing rules and the hierarchy interact

A subtle and important behaviour: when a sharing rule grants access to a role, users **above** that role in the hierarchy also gain access, provided Grant Access Using Hierarchies is enabled.

So sharing to "Sales Manager UK" also gives Sales Director EMEA and VP Sales access. This is usually desirable and occasionally not, and it is the reason a rule can appear to grant access more widely than its definition suggests.

Sharing to a **public group** does not cascade this way unless the group itself includes roles with subordinates.

## Public groups as the building block

Because sharing rules cannot target individuals, and because targeting roles couples sharing to the org structure, most well-built orgs share primarily to **public groups**.

A public group can contain users, roles, roles and subordinates, other public groups, and territories. That composability is what makes it the right target: the group definition holds the "who", and the sharing rule holds the "what", and the two change independently.

When someone joins the escalation team, you add them to the group. No sharing rule changes, no recalculation of rule definitions, no deployment. See [[03.12 - Public Groups, Queues and Territory Management]].

## Recalculation

Creating, editing, or deleting a sharing rule triggers a recalculation across every record of that object. On a large object this is a background job that can run for hours.

Salesforce provides **Defer Sharing Calculation** in Setup, which suspends recalculation while you make several changes and then runs it once. This is essential during a large reorganization or a bulk data load — without it, each individual change queues its own full recalculation.

The pattern for a major sharing change on a big org:

1. Enable deferred sharing calculation
2. Make all the changes: role moves, rule edits, ownership reassignments
3. Resume and let the single recalculation run
4. Verify with `UserRecordAccess` queries before telling users it is done

See [[23.3 - Sharing at Scale and Sharing Recalculation]].

## Inspecting shares

Every object with restrictive OWD has a share object. For custom objects it is `ObjectName__Share`; for standard ones it is `AccountShare`, `OpportunityShare`, and so on.

```sql
SELECT ParentId, UserOrGroupId, AccessLevel, RowCause
FROM Warranty_Claim__Share
WHERE ParentId = 'a0X5g000001AbCdEAK'
```

`RowCause` values tell you which mechanism granted access:

- `Owner` — the record owner
- `Rule` — a sharing rule
- `Manual` — a manual share
- `Team` — account or opportunity team
- `ImplicitChild` / `ImplicitParent` — platform-granted implicit sharing
- A custom Apex sharing reason

Combined with `UserRecordAccess`, this is the diagnostic toolkit for any access question:

```sql
SELECT RecordId, HasReadAccess, HasEditAccess, MaxAccessLevel
FROM UserRecordAccess
WHERE UserId = :someUserId AND RecordId = :someRecordId
```

Ask the platform rather than reasoning through five layers.

## Limits and design guidance

- 300 owner-based sharing rules per object, of which up to 50 can be criteria-based
- Rules cannot target individual users
- Rules cannot grant delete access
- Every rule adds to recalculation cost on that object

**Prefer criteria-based rules to owner-based ones** where the requirement is really about the data. "Everyone in the EMEA service team sees EMEA claims" is a data statement, and expressing it as ownership makes it brittle.

**Share to public groups, not roles.** It decouples the sharing model from the org structure.

**Watch the rule count on high-volume objects.** Twenty rules evaluated on every save of a two-million-row object is a measurable cost.

**Do not solve every access problem with a sharing rule.** If a large fraction of the org needs access to most records, the OWD is wrong. Sharing rules are for exceptions, not for reconstructing an open model on top of a private one.

**When the requirement is genuinely dynamic and complex**, beyond what criteria can express — access depending on a related record, or on a calculation — that is Apex sharing. See [[03.11 - Apex Sharing and Programmatic Sharing]].
