# The Monthly Indian Meal Plan

*Health & Fitness · 2026-11-01*

[[11 - The Indian Plate - Rebuilding the Thali]] set the rules: half the plate vegetables, protein at every meal, grain as one portion. Rules are easy to agree with and hard to cook from every morning. This note turns them into a month of real north Indian meals — a pantry of dals, grains and millets, a mixed atta built up slowly, and a 4-week rotation where the only things that change are the seasonal vegetables, fruits and salads.

Written for a vegetarian household with its own milk, older adults, and someone managing blood sugar. Eggs, fish or chicken can be swapped in wherever paneer or dal appears.

---

## 1. The fixed daily template

Every day follows the same shape. Only the dishes rotate.

| Slot | What | Typical portion per older adult |
|---|---|---|
| **Morning** | Tea with less sugar + a few soaked almonds or walnuts, or 1 tsp soaked alsi | 1 cup; 4–5 nuts |
| **Breakfast** | A protein-led breakfast from the list in section 4 | 1 plate |
| **Lunch** | Salad first → dal → seasonal sabzi → 2–3 rotis (mixed atta) **or** 1 katori rice/millet → 1 katori curd or a glass of chaas | Dal 1–1.5 katori, sabzi 1–1.5 katori |
| **Afternoon** | A protein snack + a whole seasonal fruit | ~30 g snack + 1 fruit |
| **Dinner** | Salad first → dal or paneer/chana dish → seasonal sabzi → 2 rotis or a millet/dalia dish → 1 katori curd (or raita) | As lunch, slightly smaller |
| **Night** | 200 ml milk (if wanted), plain or with haldi | 1 glass |
| **Daily** | 10–15 minute walk after lunch and dinner | — |

**Grain rule:** rice or millet at **one** meal a day at most; rotis at the other. Never both large rice and many rotis at the same meal.

**Curd and salad:** a katori of curd (or raita/chaas) and a plate of salad at **both** lunch and dinner.

## 2. The mixed atta, built up slowly

Fibre increased too fast causes gas and bloating. Build the mix over about 8 weeks and drink enough water. Percentages are by weight. Approximate amounts per 10 kg of flour are in brackets.

| Weeks | Wheat | Besan / roasted chana | Jau (barley) | Ragi | Alsi (flax) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1–2** | 90% (9 kg) | 10% (1 kg) | — | — | — |
| **3–4** | 80% (8 kg) | 12% (1.2 kg) | 8% (800 g) | — | — |
| **5–6** | 72% (7.2 kg) | 15% (1.5 kg) | 8% (800 g) | 5% (500 g) | — |
| **7–8 onward** | 62–65% (~6.3 kg) | 15–18% (~1.7 kg) | 10% (1 kg) | 7–8% (~750 g) | 2–3% (~250 g) |

**Optional:** replace 5% of the wheat with **soya flour** (more protein) or **oat flour** (more soluble fibre, good for cholesterol and blood sugar).

**Practical tips**
- **Ask the chakki** to grind the grains together, or grind besan separately and mix it in at home.
- **Grind in batches of about 2–3 weeks' use**, store in an airtight container in a cool place. Alsi and chana make flour go stale faster.
- **Alsi is better ground separately.** Grind a week's alsi in a mixer and knead about 1 teaspoon per person into the dough each day. This keeps it fresh and protects its omega-3 fats.
- **Softer rotis:** knead with a little warm water or curd and rest the dough for 20–30 minutes; ragi and jau make rotis slightly firmer.
- **People with thyroid problems:** ragi, jau, besan and wheat are all fine in these amounts. Bajra is left out here, and soya is fine in food amounts.

## 3. The pantry

### Dals and pulses (rotate through these)
| Pulse | Character | Best used as |
|---|---|---|
| **Moong (yellow, split)** | Light, easy to digest | Dal, chilla, khichdi |
| **Moong (whole, green)** | More fibre | Sabut moong dal, sprouts |
| **Masoor (red, split)** | Quick cooking | Dal |
| **Arhar / toor** | The everyday dal | Dal, sambar |
| **Chana dal** | Nutty, filling, lower glycaemic | Dal, with lauki; chana dal chilla |
| **Urad (whole, black)** | Rich | Dal makhani-style (light on butter) |
| **Rajma** | High protein and fibre | Rajma-chawal, rajma with roti |
| **Kabuli chana (chole)** | High protein | Chole, chana salad |
| **Kala chana** | Very high fibre | Sprouts, sookha chana, chaat |
| **Lobia** | Mild | Curry, salad |
| **Moth** | Sprouts well | Sprouts, usal |
| **Soya chunks / granules** | Very high protein | Sabzi, pulao, keema-style |
| **Sattu** (roasted chana flour) | Ready-to-eat protein, a UP–Bihar staple | Sattu drink (salted, no sugar), sattu paratha, mixed into atta |

**Cooking tips:** soak whole pulses 6–8 hours (kala chana, rajma, chole overnight), discard soaking water, and pressure cook. Soaking reduces gas. A pinch of hing and some ajwain or jeera in the tadka also helps digestion.

### Grains and millets
| Grain | How to use |
|---|---|
| **Wheat (mixed atta)** | Rotis, parathas |
| **Rice** | One meal a day at most; pair with plenty of dal and sabzi |
| **Jau (barley)** | In atta; jau dalia (savoury); jau khichdi |
| **Wheat dalia (broken wheat)** | Savoury vegetable dalia; dalia khichdi with moong |
| **Ragi** | In atta; ragi chilla; ragi dosa (fermented) |
| **Jowar** | Jowar rotis (mixed with wheat), jowar upma |
| **Kodo, kangni (foxtail), kutki (little millet), samak (barnyard)** | Rice replacement in khichdi, pulao or upma. Wash, soak 30–60 minutes, cook like rice with about 2–2.5 parts water |
| **Oats (rolled or steel-cut)** | Chilla, upma, khichdi, savoury porridge, overnight oats in curd, oat flour in atta |
| **Poha (flattened rice)** | Breakfast, always with peanuts, vegetables and sprouts or curd |
| **Makhana (fox nut)** | Dry-roasted snack |

### Protein add-ons
Paneer (4–6 times a week), curd (twice daily), milk (200 ml), roasted chana, peanuts, sprouts, soya, sattu, pumpkin and sunflower seeds, alsi, til, and whey if used ([[11 - The Indian Plate - Rebuilding the Thali]]).

## 4. Breakfast options (each with a protein source)

| Code | Breakfast | Protein from |
|---|---|---|
| **B1** | Besan chilla with grated vegetables + curd | Besan, curd |
| **B2** | Moong dal chilla (soaked, ground) with paneer stuffing on paneer days | Moong, paneer |
| **B3** | Oats chilla (oats + besan + vegetables) + curd | Besan, curd |
| **B4** | Vegetable oats upma with peanuts + chaas | Peanuts, chaas |
| **B5** | Vegetable poha with peanuts + a katori of sprouts | Peanuts, sprouts |
| **B6** | Savoury jau or wheat dalia with vegetables and moong dal | Moong |
| **B7** | 2 mixed-atta parathas stuffed with paneer, sattu or mooli (little oil) + curd | Paneer/sattu, curd |
| **B8** | Ragi chilla or ragi dosa + chutney + sambar | Dal, ragi |
| **B9** | Sprouts chaat (moong or kala chana with onion, tomato, lemon) + 1 roti | Sprouts |
| **B10** | Methi or palak thepla (mixed atta + besan) + curd | Besan, curd |
| **B11** | Overnight oats in curd with seeds and fruit (no sugar) | Curd, seeds |
| **B12** | Sattu drink (salted, with lemon, jeera, onion) + 2 mixed-atta rotis with sabzi | Sattu |

If using whey, one scoop in the morning tea-time milk or with breakfast is the easiest slot.

## 5. Snacks (afternoon)

Rotate: **roasted chana**, **peanuts**, **sprouts chaat**, **roasted makhana**, **sattu drink**, **chana–peanut chaat**, **a handful of pumpkin or sunflower seeds**, always with **one whole seasonal fruit** (guava, orange, apple, papaya, pear, jamun, amrood; small banana occasionally). Avoid biscuits, namkeen and fried snacks.

## 6. Salad and curd options

**Salads** (rotate with the season): cucumber, tomato, onion, carrot, radish, beetroot, cabbage, sprouts, chana or lobia, lettuce or greens. Dress with lemon, a pinch of salt, black pepper and roasted jeera powder.

**Curd:** plain dahi, cucumber raita, lauki raita, boondi-free vegetable raita, mint raita, chaas with roasted jeera.

## 7. The 4-week rotation

**S** = seasonal sabzi of the day (whatever is fresh: lauki, tori, tinda, bhindi, gobhi, palak, methi, sarson, baingan, beans, mixed veg, etc.). **R** = rotis (mixed atta). **★** = paneer meal (5 per week).

Every lunch and dinner also has **salad first** and **a katori of curd/raita/chaas**.

## 8. Protein check (per older adult, food only)

A typical day from the plan, with 200 ml milk and paneer about 5 times a week:

| Source | Approx. protein |
|---|---|
| Dal or pulse dish at lunch and dinner (~1.5 katori each) | 18–22 g |
| Curd twice (2 katoris) | 10–12 g |
| Milk 200 ml | ~6.5 g |
| Protein breakfast (chilla/sprouts/paneer etc.) | 8–12 g |
| 4–5 mixed-atta rotis (with ~15% chana) | 16–20 g |
| Afternoon snack (roasted chana/peanuts/sprouts) | 6–8 g |
| Paneer (~50 g, 5 days a week, averaged) | ~6–7 g |
| **Total** | **~60–70 g** |

That meets roughly 1.0 g/kg for a 60–65 kg adult from food alone. A daily scoop of whey adds about 20 g on top.

## 9. Adjusting the plan

- **For blood sugar:** keep rotis to 2–3 per meal and rice or millet to 1 katori; always salad and dal first; walk after meals; fruit as a whole fruit with the afternoon snack, not as juice ([[18 - Blood Sugar, Insulin Resistance & Diabetes]]).
- **For weight loss:** reduce grain by about one roti per meal, not protein or vegetables.
- **For heavy farm work days:** add a roti or a katori of rice/millet; the extra activity uses it.
- **For gas or bloating:** slow the atta ramp-up, soak pulses well, use moong more and rajma/chole less often, add hing and ajwain, and drink water.
- **For kidney disease** or other conditions with diet restrictions: get the plan adjusted by the doctor or a dietitian ([[08 - The Liver & Kidneys - Factories and Filters]]).
- **Seasonal swaps:** winter greens (sarson, bathua, methi, palak) and gajar–mooli salads; summer lauki, tori, tinda, karela, cucumber, more chaas and sattu drinks; monsoon well-washed, well-cooked vegetables and fewer raw leafy salads ([[07 - Gut Health & Digestion]]).
- **Oil:** about 4–6 teaspoons per person for the whole day across all dishes; mustard oil as the base. **Salt:** about one level teaspoon per person per day in total, iodised ([[11 - The Indian Plate - Rebuilding the Thali]]).

## 10. A weekly kitchen routine

- **Sunday:** plan the week's sabzis from the market; soak and sprout moong or kala chana for 2–3 days of use; check atta and grind alsi for the week.
- **Every evening:** soak the next day's pulses; set curd.
- **Every 2–3 weeks:** grind a fresh batch of mixed atta at the chakki.
- **Every week or two:** make paneer from the home milk.
- **Keep stocked:** roasted chana, peanuts, makhana, sattu, oats, seeds, soya chunks, two or three millets, a range of dals.

---

## The layer, in one line

A month of healthy Indian eating is a fixed daily shape — protein breakfast, salad-first lunch and dinner with dal, seasonal sabzi, curd and one grain portion, a protein snack with fruit, and a glass of milk — cooked from a rotating pantry of a dozen pulses, mixed atta built up over eight weeks, and millets and oats as rice and breakfast swaps, with only the seasonal vegetables, fruits and salads changing.
