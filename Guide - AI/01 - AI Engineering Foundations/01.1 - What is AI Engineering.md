# What is AI Engineering

## Definition

**AI Engineering** is the discipline of building reliable, production-grade software systems that incorporate AI models — primarily large language models (LLMs) — to deliver real user value.

It sits at the intersection of:

- Software engineering (system design, APIs, infra, reliability)
- ML knowledge (enough to understand model behavior, not necessarily to train from scratch)
- Product thinking (what problem does this solve, how do we measure success)

The key word is **systems**. An AI engineer builds the _plumbing_ around models — retrieval pipelines, orchestration layers, evaluation harnesses, prompt management, observability — not just the model itself.

---

## What AI Engineering Is NOT

- It is **not** research — you're not advancing the state of the art
- It is **not** just "calling the OpenAI API" — that's the trivial part
- It is **not** pure ML engineering — you rarely train models from scratch
- It is **not** data science — you're not doing exploratory analysis or reporting

---

## The Core Problem AI Engineers Solve

Raw LLMs have three fundamental limitations in production:

|Limitation|Problem|Solution Layer|
|---|---|---|
|Knowledge cutoff|Model doesn't know recent/private data|RAG, retrieval systems|
|No memory|Each call is stateless|Memory systems, context management|
|Unreliable output|Hallucinations, format failures|Grounding, structured outputs, evaluation|

AI engineers build systems that paper over these gaps while keeping latency, cost, and reliability at production-grade levels.

---

## What Production AI Systems Actually Look Like

Most production AI systems have these layers:

```
User Query
    │
    ▼
[Input Preprocessing]        ← sanitization, PII detection, routing
    │
    ▼
[Retrieval Layer]            ← vector search, BM25, reranking
    │
    ▼
[Context Assembly]           ← chunking, prompt construction, token budgeting
    │
    ▼
[LLM Call]                   ← with retry, fallback, model routing
    │
    ▼
[Output Processing]          ← parsing, validation, citation extraction
    │
    ▼
[Response + Logging]         ← streaming, observability, eval triggers
```

Every one of those boxes is an engineering problem, not a model problem.

---

## Where AI Engineering Spends Its Time

In practice, senior AI engineers spend time on:

1. **Retrieval quality** — getting the right context into the prompt is 80% of answer quality
2. **Evaluation** — defining what "correct" means and measuring it at scale
3. **Latency optimization** — LLM calls are slow; every layer adds P99 risk
4. **Prompt engineering** — not magic tricks, but systematic instruction design
5. **Failure handling** — LLMs fail in unpredictable ways; systems must degrade gracefully
6. **Cost control** — token costs compound at scale

---

## Model Building vs Model Usage

|Model Building|Model Usage (AI Engineering)|
|---|---|
|Train/fine-tune weights|Use pre-trained weights|
|GPU clusters, CUDA|APIs or inference servers|
|Dataset curation at scale|Prompt + retrieval design|
|Loss curves, perplexity|Accuracy, latency, cost|
|Research mindset|Engineering mindset|
|Months of compute|Days to weeks|

Most production AI teams do **model usage**, not model building. Fine-tuning exists in between — it's covered in [[../04 - Fine-Tuning and Model Adaptation/04.1 - Fine-Tuning Fundamentals|Section 04]].
