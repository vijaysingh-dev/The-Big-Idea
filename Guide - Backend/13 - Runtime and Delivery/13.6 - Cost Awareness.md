# Cost Awareness

#backend #operations #architecture

> **In one line:** Cost is a non-functional requirement like latency, and the engineer who can say "this feature costs $0.003 per request" is far more useful in a design discussion than one who cannot.

---

## The Idea

⚠️ **Most engineers have no idea what their systems cost**, which means cost decisions get made by finance from a spreadsheet rather than by engineering from a design.

💡 **The single most useful number: cost per unit of business value.**

```
cost_per_document_indexed = monthly_infra_cost / documents_indexed
cost_per_request          = monthly_infra_cost / requests
cost_per_tenant           = attributed_cost / tenant
```

**Once you have it, "should we cache this" becomes arithmetic instead of opinion.**

---

## Where the Money Goes

For a typical backend system:

| Category | Share | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Compute** | 40–60% | Often over-provisioned |
| **Managed databases** | 15–30% | Rarely right-sized |
| **Data transfer** | 5–20% | **Frequently a surprise** |
| Storage | 5–15% | Cheap, and it accumulates |
| **Observability** | 5–15% | **Often exceeds application infrastructure** |
| Third-party APIs | Variable | LLM APIs dominate when present |

⚠️ **Two of these consistently surprise people: cross-AZ data transfer and observability.**

### Cross-zone traffic

```
Same AZ:        free
Cross-AZ:       ~$0.01/GB each way
Cross-region:   ~$0.02/GB
Internet egress: ~$0.09/GB
```

⚠️ **A three-AZ Kubernetes cluster with random pod placement sends roughly two thirds of internal traffic across zones.** At high internal volume that is a real monthly number for traffic that does nothing.

```yaml
apiVersion: v1
kind: Service
spec:
  trafficDistribution: PreferClose      # prefer same-zone endpoints
```

💡 **Topology-aware routing is close to free to enable and can cut cross-AZ transfer substantially.**

⚠️ **It trades against availability** — same-zone preference means a zone failure shifts load abruptly. **Measure both.**

**Internet egress is where the largest surprises live.** Serving files directly from your application rather than through a CDN is often the single largest avoidable line item.

### Observability

⚠️ **Observability data volume frequently exceeds application data volume**, and per-GB ingest pricing means it scales with your traffic ([[12.1 - Observability Recap and Gaps]]).

| Lever | Impact |
|---|---|
| **Drop high-cardinality labels** | Largest single win |
| **Disable `DEBUG` in production** | Logs usually dominate |
| Tail-based sampling | Keep errors and slow traces only |
| Retention tiers | High resolution for days, downsampled for months |
| **Delete unqueried metrics** | Most systems export hundreds nobody reads |

💡 **Audit which metrics appear in a dashboard or alert. Delete the rest.** Typically 40–60% of exported series are unused.

## Compute

### Right-sizing

```promql
quantile_over_time(0.95, container_memory_working_set_bytes[14d])
quantile_over_time(0.95, rate(container_cpu_usage_seconds_total[5m])[14d:])
```

⚠️ **Requests determine what you pay for, because they determine how many pods fit on a node.** A pod requesting 2 CPU and using 200m is paying for ten times what it needs ([[13.4 - Probes Resources and Autoscaling]]).

💡 **Run VPA in recommendation mode for two weeks and compare against your current requests.** The gap is usually large, and closing it is the fastest compute saving available.

### Spot and reserved

| Type | Discount | For |
|---|---|---|
| On-demand | — | Baseline capacity |
| **Reserved / savings plan** | 30–60% | **Predictable baseline** |
| **Spot** | 60–90% | **Interruptible work** |

💡 **Background workers are ideal spot candidates** — a worker killed with two minutes' notice requeues its task and another picks it up ([[08.2 - Queue Semantics]]).

⚠️ **Your grace period must fit inside the interruption notice.** A worker with a 120-second grace period on a 120-second warning gets killed mid-shutdown ([[11.7 - Graceful Shutdown and Draining]]).

```yaml
nodeSelector: {node-type: spot}
tolerations: [{key: spot, operator: Exists, effect: NoSchedule}]
```

**Never put stateful services or the last replica of anything on spot.**

### Scale to zero

💡 **Development and preview environments running 24/7 for a team working 40 hours a week waste roughly 75% of their cost.**

```yaml
# Scale dev to zero outside working hours
schedule: "0 19 * * 1-5"    → replicas: 0
schedule: "0 8 * * 1-5"     → replicas: 2
```

⚠️ **Ephemeral preview environments per PR** ([[13.5 - CI CD Pipelines]]) **must be torn down on merge**, or they accumulate silently. **Set a TTL as a backstop.**

## Databases

⚠️ **Managed databases are usually the second-largest line item and the least right-sized**, because resizing feels risky so nobody does it.

| Lever | |
|---|---|
| **Right-size the instance** | Measure CPU, memory and IOPS over a fortnight |
| **Storage tiering** | Move cold data to object storage |
| **Partition and drop** | `DROP TABLE` beats `DELETE` for retention ([[06.13 - Partitioning and Sharding]]) |
| Reduce bloat | You are paying for dead tuples ([[06.7 - MVCC Vacuum and Bloat]]) |
| Drop unused indexes | Storage, write throughput, and backup size ([[06.3 - Indexes Deep Dive]]) |
| Replica count | Each is a full-price instance |

💡 **Bloat is a direct cost.** A 40% bloated 500GB database is 200GB of storage, backup and replication bandwidth for dead rows.

⚠️ **Provisioned IOPS is frequently over-specified.** Measure actual IOPS before paying for headroom you do not use.

## Cost per Request

```python
def cost_per_request(route: str) -> float:
    return (
        cpu_seconds[route] * CPU_COST_PER_SECOND
      + db_rows_read[route] * DB_COST_PER_ROW
      + bytes_out[route] * EGRESS_COST_PER_BYTE
      + external_calls[route] * API_COST
    )
```

💡 **Track cost per *endpoint*, not just in aggregate.** One expensive endpoint usually dominates spend while being a small fraction of traffic — **and it is the one worth optimising or rate limiting** ([[11.5 - Rate Limiting and Quotas]]).

⚠️ **In an AI-adjacent system, third-party API cost dominates everything else.**

```python
embedding_tokens_total.inc(count)
llm_cost_usd_total.labels(model=..., org_id=...).inc(cost)
```

**Track tokens per organisation in logs, aggregate in metrics** ([[12.3 - Instrumenting Conduit End to End]]).

### Per-tenant cost

```python
sql = f"/* org:{org_id} */ SELECT ..."
```

💡 **Per-tenant cost attribution turns "this customer is unprofitable" from a suspicion into a number**, and it is directly actionable — a pricing change, a quota, or a conversation ([[06.16 - Multi Tenant Data Models]]).

⚠️ **Do not label metrics by tenant** — cardinality. **Logs and traces carry the tenant; metrics carry the aggregate.**

## In Production

### The cost dashboard

```
Total monthly run rate           $12,400
  Compute                         $5,900   (48%)
  Managed Postgres                $2,800   (23%)
  Observability                   $1,600   (13%)   ⚠️
  Data transfer                     $900    (7%)
  Object storage                    $400    (3%)
  Embedding API                     $800    (6%)

Cost per document indexed         $0.0031
Cost per 1,000 requests           $0.042
Most expensive endpoint           POST /exports  ($0.18/request)
Top tenant by cost                org_7 ($1,840/month, plan revenue $499)  ⚠️
```

💡 **The last two rows are what make this dashboard actionable.** An endpoint at $0.18 per request and a customer costing four times their plan are both immediate conversations.

### Alerting on cost

```python
cost_anomaly: current_daily_cost > 1.5 * trailing_7d_average
tenant_cost_exceeds_revenue: attributed_cost > plan_revenue
endpoint_cost_spike: cost_per_request[route] > 2 * baseline
```

⚠️ **Cost anomalies are frequently the first visible symptom of a bug** — a retry loop, a missing cache, an accidental N+1, or a runaway job. **The cost graph sometimes notices before the latency graph does.**

### The cost of a design decision

💡 **Being able to state cost during a design review is genuinely valuable.**

```
"Adding a read replica: ~$280/month, and it lets us offload
 analytics queries. The alternative — a materialised view — is
 free but adds 5 minutes of staleness."

"Elasticsearch for search: ~$450/month plus operational burden.
 Postgres FTS is $0 and measurably worse relevance on 12 of our
 50 golden queries." ([[06.15 - Search - Postgres vs Elasticsearch]])

"Kafka instead of Redis Streams: ~$600/month, and it is what
 lets us rebuild the index from 90 days of history." ([[09.8 - Kafka Recap and Where It Fits]])
```

⚠️ **Note that each of these is a tradeoff statement, not a cost-cutting argument.** The point is not to be cheap — **it is to know what you are buying.**

### The optimisation ladder

```
1. Delete unused things          free, immediate
   → unqueried metrics, unused indexes, idle environments,
     orphaned volumes, stale preview environments
2. Right-size                    low risk, large saving
   → requests, database instances, IOPS
3. Reduce waste                  low risk
   → cross-AZ traffic, DEBUG logs, egress via CDN
4. Commitment discounts          zero risk, requires forecasting
   → reserved instances, savings plans
5. Spot for interruptible work   moderate complexity
6. Architectural change          highest effort, largest ceiling
   → caching, partitioning, algorithmic improvements
```

💡 **Steps 1 and 2 typically recover 20–40% with no architectural change and almost no risk.** Teams frequently skip them and go straight to step 6.

⚠️ **Do steps 1–4 before optimising code.** Engineering time is more expensive than most infrastructure.

### The audit

```
□  Cost per request and per unit of business value, known
□  Cost per endpoint tracked
□  Per-tenant attribution possible
□  Requests right-sized against measured p95
□  Observability volume audited; unqueried metrics deleted
□  Cross-AZ traffic measured; topology-aware routing evaluated
□  Egress served via CDN
□  Non-production environments scaled to zero out of hours
□  Preview environments have a TTL
□  Database instance right-sized; bloat and unused indexes addressed
□  Interruptible workloads on spot
□  Cost anomaly alerting
□  Orphaned resources cleaned up
```

### Conduit's cost model

| Component | Monthly | Lever |
|---|---|---|
| Gateway + document service | $1,900 | Right-sized from VPA |
| Ingestion workers | $1,400 | **Spot** — tasks requeue on interruption |
| Managed Postgres | $2,800 | Partitioned; retention via `DROP` |
| Redis | $340 | |
| Kafka | $600 | 90-day retention, justified by rebuild capability |
| Object storage | $400 | Lifecycle to infrequent access after 30 days |
| Observability | $900 | **After deleting 55% unqueried series** |
| Embedding API | $800 | Cached by content hash |
| **Total** | **$9,140** | |
| **Cost per document indexed** | **$0.0031** | |

💡 **The embedding cache is the highest-leverage single optimisation** — deduplicating by content hash means re-uploading the same document costs nothing, and in practice a meaningful fraction of uploads are duplicates ([[07.5 - Idempotency Keys and Deduplication]]).

⚠️ **The observability line was $2,000 before the audit.** Deleting series nobody queried was a two-hour job with no risk and no downside.

## Codebase

**Repo:** `opencost/opencost`, `kubernetes-sigs/descheduler`, `kubecost` (docs)

**Question to answer:** How does OpenCost attribute cluster cost to individual workloads, and what does it do about shared resources? Then: how would you attribute cost to a tenant rather than a workload?

**Read, in order:**

1. `opencost/pkg/costmodel/costmodel.go` — **the allocation model.** How node cost is divided across pods by requests and usage. **The answer to the first question**
2. `opencost/pkg/cloud/` — how cloud pricing APIs are consumed, and how spot and reserved pricing is handled
3. `opencost/pkg/costmodel/network.go` — **cross-zone traffic attribution.** The part most cost tools do badly
4. `kubernetes-sigs/descheduler` — rebalancing pods for better packing. **Bin-packing is a direct cost lever**
5. **Your own cloud provider's pricing pages** — **read them properly once.** The egress and cross-AZ sections in particular

💡 The OpenCost allocation model is worth understanding because **it makes explicit the choice between charging for requests and charging for usage.** A pod requesting 2 CPU and using 200m occupies 2 CPU of schedulable capacity — **so requests are the honest basis, and that is what makes right-sizing the primary lever.**

**Size:** 1 session.

## Exercise

**Drill 13.6 — Know what everything costs**

```
drills/13-cost/
├── attribution/{per_request.py, per_endpoint.py, per_tenant.py, RESULTS.md}
├── rightsizing/{measure.py, vpa_compare.py, savings.md}
├── observability/{volume_audit.py, unqueried.py, RESULTS.md}
├── network/{cross_az.py, topology_aware.yaml, egress.py, RESULTS.md}
├── spot/{worker_spot.yaml, interruption_test.sh, RESULTS.md}
├── dashboard/{cost.json, alerts.yaml}
└── audit.md
```

**Part 1 — Cost per request, per endpoint.** Instrument CPU seconds, database rows read, bytes out and external API calls per route.

**Compute cost per request for every endpoint** using your provider's actual prices.

**Rank them. Find the endpoint that dominates cost while being a small fraction of traffic.**

**Part 2 — Per-tenant attribution.** Add query tagging and per-tenant resource tracking.

**Compute monthly cost per tenant and compare to plan revenue.**

**Find any tenant costing more than they pay.** Then decide: a quota, a pricing change, or accept it — and write the reasoning.

**Part 3 — Right-sizing.** Measure p95 CPU and working-set memory over two weeks (or simulate it).

**Compare against your current requests. Compute the waste.**

Then run VPA in recommendation mode and compare its numbers to yours. **Apply the changes and measure the actual cost reduction and any latency impact.**

**Part 4 — The observability audit.** Measure total metric series, trace bytes and log bytes per day, with the cost of each.

**Then determine which metric series appear in any dashboard or alert.**

**Report the unqueried percentage.** Delete them and measure the reduction.

Then disable `DEBUG` logging and switch to tail-based sampling. **Report the total saving and what you gave up.**

**Part 5 — Cross-AZ traffic.** Measure inter-pod traffic and how much crosses zones.

**Compute the monthly cost.**

Then enable topology-aware routing and measure again. **Then simulate a zone failure and measure the availability impact** — report both sides of the trade.

Then measure egress: serving a file directly from the application versus through a CDN. **Report the per-GB difference.**

**Part 6 — Spot workers.** Move ingestion workers to spot instances.

**Simulate an interruption with a two-minute warning.** Confirm the worker drains, requeues its task, and another completes it.

**Measure: tasks lost (should be zero), work redone, and the cost saving.**

Then set a grace period longer than the warning and **confirm work is lost** — establishing the constraint.

**Part 7 — The dashboard and alerts.** Build the cost dashboard from this note, including cost per unit of business value and the top tenant.

Then implement the three cost alerts. **Simulate a retry-loop bug that triples external API calls and confirm the anomaly alert fires** — and note whether it fires before your latency alerts would have.

**Part 8 — The ladder.** Work through steps 1–4 against Conduit.

**Report the total saving from steps 1–2 alone**, with no architectural change.

Then, for one architectural option — a cache, a materialised view, or an index — **compute the cost saving and the engineering time, and state whether it is worth doing.**

**Done when:**
- You know cost per request for every endpoint, ranked
- You have found a tenant whose cost exceeds their revenue, or proven none exists
- Right-sizing produced a measured saving with no latency regression
- You deleted unqueried metric series and measured the reduction
- Cross-AZ cost is measured, and topology-aware routing's availability trade quantified
- A spot interruption loses zero tasks, and a wrong grace period loses work
- A cost anomaly alert caught a simulated bug
- Steps 1–2 of the ladder produced a stated percentage saving
