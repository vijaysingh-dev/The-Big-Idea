# Health Across Life Stages

*Health & Fitness · 2026-10-30*

A family isn't one patient. A grandmother, a pregnant daughter-in-law, a teenager preparing for exams and a toddler each need different things from the same kitchen and the same budget. The principles from earlier notes don't change, but the priorities do — and some of the most important health decisions for a 60-year-old were made before they were born.

---

## 1. Pregnancy and the first 1,000 days

The **first 1,000 days** — from conception to a child's second birthday — shape lifelong health more than almost any other period. Undernutrition in the womb and early childhood raises the child's later risk of short stature, lower learning ability, and, through the **thin-fat** pattern, diabetes and heart disease in adulthood ([[18 - Blood Sugar, Insulin Resistance & Diabetes]]).

### Before pregnancy
- **Folic acid** ideally from at least a month before conception, to prevent neural tube defects (serious birth defects of the brain and spine).
- **Check and correct** anaemia, thyroid function, blood sugar and BP.
- **Know thalassaemia carrier status** for both partners ([[27 - The Testing Calendar]]).
- **Vaccinations up to date**, including rubella and hepatitis B.
- **Stop tobacco, areca nut and alcohol.**

### During pregnancy
- **At least four antenatal check-ups**, ideally more (government programmes such as PMSMA provide free check-ups on the 9th of every month).
- **Iron, folic acid and calcium** as prescribed; anaemia in pregnancy is common in India and dangerous.
- **Screening for gestational diabetes** (high sugar in pregnancy) with a glucose test, usually around 24–28 weeks — Indian guidelines recommend testing all pregnant women. Gestational diabetes raises the mother's later diabetes risk substantially.
- **BP checks** for pre-eclampsia (dangerous high BP in pregnancy).
- **Td vaccine.**
- **Good nutrition**: extra protein, a varied plate, no "eating for two."
- **Institutional delivery** with a trained team.

**Danger signs in pregnancy** (go to hospital): bleeding, severe headache, blurred vision, swelling of face and hands, convulsions, high fever, severe abdominal pain, leaking fluid, reduced baby movements ([[25 - Fast Killers - Emergencies & Warning Signs]]).

### After birth
- **Breastfeeding** within the first hour, **exclusive breastfeeding for six months** (no water, honey, ghutti or formula unless medically advised), and continued breastfeeding with other foods up to two years or beyond.
- **Mothers' recovery:** nutrition, rest, iron, and watching for **postpartum depression** ([[15 - Stress, Mental Health & Social Connection]]).
- **Family planning** support and spacing between pregnancies.

## 2. Infants and young children (0–5)

- **Vaccinations** on schedule under the Universal Immunisation Programme ([[17 - Infections, Hygiene & Vaccines]]).
- **Complementary feeding** from six months: mashed dal, khichdi, vegetables, fruits, egg if eaten, with continued breastfeeding. Gradually increase variety and texture. Avoid added sugar and salt, biscuits, packaged juices and instant noodles.
- **Growth monitoring:** weight and height on a growth chart at every visit. Under-weight, stunting (short for age) and wasting (thin for height) are still common in India; overweight is rising.
- **Anaemia prevention:** iron-rich foods, iron syrup as given under Anaemia Mukt Bharat, deworming every six months from age 1 (National Deworming Day).
- **Vitamin A** doses as per the national programme.
- **Diarrhoea:** ORS and zinc tablets; keep feeding.
- **Development:** talking, reading, play and responsive attention build the brain. Avoid screens for children under 2 (except video calls), and keep screen time to under an hour a day for ages 2–5.
- **Safety:** drowning (buckets, ponds), burns, falls, poisoning (medicines, kerosene, pesticides), road safety with child seats or helmets.

## 3. School-age children (6–12)

- **Nutrition:** a balanced thali with protein at each meal ([[11 - The Indian Plate - Rebuilding the Thali]]); limit sugary drinks, packaged snacks and fried street food. Habits formed now tend to stick.
- **Physical activity:** at least **60 minutes a day** of moderate to vigorous activity (WHO), including play that strengthens muscles and bones.
- **Sleep:** 9–12 hours ([[14 - Sleep & Circadian Rhythm]]).
- **Screens:** clear limits, no screens in the bedroom at night.
- **Eyes:** **myopia** (short-sightedness) is rising fast in Indian children, linked to less outdoor time and more near work. Two hours a day outdoors helps protect against it; yearly eye checks catch it.
- **Dental care:** brushing twice daily with fluoride toothpaste, limiting sweets between meals.
- **Deworming**, iron and iodised salt.
- **Weight:** childhood obesity is increasingly common in urban India; the answer is family-wide habits, not dieting the child.

## 4. Teenagers (13–19)

- **Growth spurt:** high needs for protein, calcium, iron (especially girls after periods begin) and energy. This is the main window to build **peak bone mass** ([[09 - Muscles, Bones & Joints - How the Frame Works]]).
- **Sports and strength training** are safe and beneficial.
- **Mental health:** exam pressure, social media, relationships and identity. Depression, anxiety, self-harm and eating disorders often start in the teens. Listen without judgement; seek help early ([[15 - Stress, Mental Health & Social Connection]]).
- **Tobacco, vaping, areca nut, alcohol and drugs:** most lifelong users start as teenagers. Clear family rules and role models matter.
- **HPV vaccine** for girls (and boys where available), ideally by age 14 ([[23 - Cancer - How It Starts and How to Catch It Early]]).
- **Menstrual health** for girls: sanitary products, iron, knowing what's normal (very heavy or painful periods, or irregular periods with acne and weight gain, which may suggest **PCOS**, deserve a doctor's visit).
- **Sleep:** teens need 8–10 hours but their body clock naturally shifts later; early school start times plus late-night screens create chronic sleep debt.
- **Road safety:** two-wheeler accidents are a leading cause of death; helmets always.

## 5. Young adults (20–39)

The years that set up midlife health, often neglected because "I'm young."
- **Build reserves:** muscle, bone, fitness and financial health (including health insurance, [[30 - Health Insurance & Money for Health]]).
- **Know your baseline numbers:** BP, HbA1c, lipids, Lp(a) once, CBC, TSH, vitamin D and B12 ([[27 - The Testing Calendar]]).
- **Watch the drift:** weight gain after marriage, desk jobs, late-night eating, poor sleep and stress commonly push South Asians into prediabetes and high triglycerides in their late 20s and 30s.
- **Cervical screening** from 25–30 for women.
- **Mental health** under career, migration and relationship pressure.
- **Avoid starting tobacco and heavy alcohol habits.**

## 6. Women's health across life

- **Periods:** heavy bleeding is a major cause of iron-deficiency anaemia; it's not something to "just bear."
- **PCOS:** irregular periods, acne, excess hair, weight gain; linked to insulin resistance and later diabetes. Lifestyle is central to treatment ([[04 - Hormones - The Body's Messaging System]]).
- **Thyroid disease** is more common in women; TSH checks matter ([[22 - Thyroid, Anaemia & Common Deficiencies]]).
- **Pregnancy history** matters for later health: gestational diabetes and pre-eclampsia raise future diabetes and heart risk. Tell doctors about them years later.
- **Menopause** (usually 45–55): falling oestrogen speeds bone loss, raises LDL, shifts fat to the belly and can bring hot flushes, sleep problems, low mood and joint aches. Strength training, protein, calcium, vitamin D and attention to BP, sugar and lipids are the priorities. Hormone therapy is an option for troublesome symptoms in some women and is a decision to discuss with a doctor.
- **Heart disease in women** is often missed because symptoms can be atypical ([[25 - Fast Killers - Emergencies & Warning Signs]]).
- **Breast and cervical cancer screening** ([[23 - Cancer - How It Starts and How to Catch It Early]]).
- **Knee osteoarthritis and osteoporosis** are more common in older women ([[21 - Joints & Bones - Osteoarthritis, Osteoporosis & Falls]]).
- **Women often put their own health last** in Indian families, eating last, skipping check-ups and ignoring symptoms. A family health system should deliberately correct this.

## 7. Men's health across life

- **Heart disease** tends to start earlier in men; South Asian men are at particularly high risk in their 40s and 50s.
- **Tobacco and alcohol** use are much higher among Indian men.
- **Prostate:** enlargement with age causes frequent urination, weak stream and night-time waking; discuss PSA testing at 55–69 ([[23 - Cancer - How It Starts and How to Catch It Early]]).
- **Mental health:** men are less likely to seek help; depression may show up as anger, drinking or withdrawal.
- **Low testosterone** with obesity, diabetes and poor sleep; lifestyle fixes much of it.

## 8. Midlife (40–59)

The decade when silent diseases start to surface ([[24 - Silent Killers - Diseases That Hide Until Late]]).
- **Yearly core tests**, ECG, eye pressure checks, cancer screening as due.
- **Strength training** becomes non-negotiable to slow muscle loss.
- **Protect sleep and manage stress** — often the most stressful decades, with children's education, careers and ageing parents.
- **Treat risk factors early and to target.**

## 9. Older adults (60+)

The goal shifts from "preventing disease" to **staying independent, comfortable and connected**:
- **Muscle and balance:** strength and balance exercises at least 3 times a week; enough protein ([[12 - Strength Training - Muscle as Medicine]], [[13 - Cardio, Mobility & Balance]]).
- **Falls prevention** and home safety ([[21 - Joints & Bones - Osteoarthritis, Osteoporosis & Falls]]).
- **Medicine review** yearly to reduce polypharmacy ([[28 - Medicines Without Myths]]).
- **Vaccines:** flu yearly, pneumococcal, shingles, Td boosters ([[17 - Infections, Hygiene & Vaccines]]).
- **Senses:** cataract surgery when vision limits life; hearing aids for hearing loss (linked to lower dementia risk); dental care for chewing and nutrition.
- **Memory:** new or worsening memory problems deserve assessment; treatable causes such as B12 deficiency, thyroid problems, depression, medicine side effects and hearing loss must be ruled out ([[10 - The Brain & Nervous System]]).
- **Nutrition:** appetite falls with age; smaller, protein-rich meals and snacks prevent weight and muscle loss. Unintended weight loss is a warning sign.
- **Heat and dehydration:** thirst weakens with age; drink regularly, especially in summer.
- **Social life and purpose:** loneliness is a major health risk; regular contact, community roles and meaningful activity matter ([[15 - Stress, Mental Health & Social Connection]]).
- **Planning ahead:** conversations about preferences for care in serious illness, financial arrangements, a nominated person who knows their medicines and wishes.

---

## The layer, in one line

The same health principles apply at every age, but priorities shift: nutrition and growth in the first 1,000 days and childhood, bone, activity and mental health in the teens, building reserves and knowing baseline numbers in young adulthood, catching silent disease in midlife, and protecting muscle, balance, senses, medicines and social connection in old age — with women's health deliberately put back at the centre of the family.
