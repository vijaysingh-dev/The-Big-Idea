# Relationships - Lookup vs Master-Detail

## The decision

Every time you connect two objects, you choose between a lookup and a master-detail relationship. It looks like a small choice and it is not: it determines record ownership, sharing, deletion behaviour, whether roll-ups are possible, and how hard the relationship is to change later.

This is one of the highest-consequence decisions in Salesforce data modelling.

## Side by side

| Behaviour | Lookup | Master-Detail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Parent required on child | Optional by default | Always required |
| Child has its own owner | Yes | No, inherits from parent |
| Child has its own sharing | Yes | No, inherits from parent |
| Deleting the parent | Child survives, field cleared | Child is deleted |
| Roll-up summaries | Not supported | Supported |
| Reparenting the child | Allowed | Off by default, can be enabled |
| Max per object | 25 | 2 |
| Child can be a master of another object | Yes | Yes, up to 3 levels deep |
| Can be converted | To master-detail, if all children have a parent | To lookup, if no roll-ups exist |

## Master-detail, in plain terms

A master-detail relationship says: **the child cannot exist without the parent, and the child is part of the parent.**

The child has no independent identity. It has no `OwnerId` field at all. Who can see it is entirely determined by who can see the parent. Delete the parent and the child goes with it.

The classic example is OpportunityLineItem. A line item on a deal is meaningless without the deal. Delete the opportunity and the line items should absolutely go with it.

In our running manufacturing example: `Warranty_Claim_Line__c` under `Warranty_Claim__c`. A claim line is part of the claim.

## Lookup, in plain terms

A lookup says: **these records are related, and each stands on its own.**

The child has its own owner and its own sharing. Deleting the parent leaves the child in place with an empty reference.

Contact to Account is a lookup. A contact relates to a company, but if the company record is deleted the person still exists as a concept, and the contact has its own owner who may differ from the account owner.

In our example: `Warranty_Claim__c` to `Account__c` is a lookup. A claim relates to a customer, but claims are owned by the service team, not by whoever owns the account.

## How to actually decide

Ask these in order.

**1. Does the child make any sense without the parent?** If no, master-detail. Line items, addresses on an order, steps in a process.

**2. Do I need a roll-up summary?** If yes, master-detail is the only native option. But do not let this alone decide it — the alternatives in [[02.6 - Roll-Up Summary Fields]] exist precisely so you are not forced into an inappropriate master-detail.

**3. Does the child need its own sharing or ownership?** If yes, lookup. This is the one that most often overrules the roll-up argument. If claims must be visible to the service team but accounts are visible to sales, inheriting account sharing is wrong.

**4. Should deleting the parent delete the child?** If deleting an account should not delete a decade of warranty history, lookup.

**5. Is the parent optional?** If a claim can exist before it has been matched to a machine, lookup, because master-detail requires the parent on every record.

The pattern most experienced engineers follow: **lookup by default, master-detail when the child is genuinely a component of the parent.** Master-detail is the stronger commitment, and stronger commitments should be justified.

## The sharing consequence, spelled out

This is the part people underestimate.

With master-detail, the child's visibility is the parent's visibility. There is no way to give someone access to a detail record without giving them access to its master. You cannot write a sharing rule on the detail object, because it has no owner to share by.

That is exactly right when the child is a component. It is badly wrong when the child holds sensitive information the parent's audience should not see. Compensation records under an employee object, or medical detail under a patient account, are the kind of case where master-detail creates a data exposure that is difficult to unwind.

Section 03 covers this properly, but the decision is made here, at data modelling time, and reversing it later means migrating every record.

## Relationship fields in code

A relationship gives you two names. The field itself, ending in `__c`, holds the ID. The relationship name, ending in `__r`, is used to traverse.

```java
// Writing: use the __c field, set it to an Id
Warranty_Claim__c claim = new Warranty_Claim__c(
    Account__c = accountId,
    Machine__c = machineId
);
insert claim;

// Reading upward: use __r to traverse to parent fields
List<Warranty_Claim__c> claims = [
    SELECT Id, Claim_Number__c,
           Account__r.Name, Account__r.Owner.Email,
           Machine__r.Serial_Number__c, Machine__r.Product__r.Family
    FROM Warranty_Claim__c
    WHERE Status__c = 'Submitted'
];

for (Warranty_Claim__c c : claims) {
    System.debug(c.Account__r.Name + ' / ' + c.Machine__r.Serial_Number__c);
}
```

For standard relationships the pattern is the same without the suffixes: `Account.Name`, `Owner.Email`.

Traversing downward uses a subquery, and the child relationship name is pluralized by default:

```sql
SELECT Id, Name, Serial_Number__c,
       (SELECT Id, Claim_Number__c, Status__c FROM Warranty_Claims__r WHERE Status__c != 'Closed')
FROM Machine__c
WHERE Account__c = :accountId
```

The child relationship name is set when the relationship field is created and can be edited afterwards. Leaving it as an unhelpful default makes every subquery in the org harder to read.

Upward traversal is limited to **5 levels** in SOQL, and downward subqueries to **1 level**. You cannot query grandchildren in one go. See [[08.2 - Relationship Queries]].

## Converting between them

**Lookup to master-detail** is allowed only if every existing child record has the parent field populated. In practice this means a data cleanup pass first, and often a temporary placeholder parent for orphaned records.

**Master-detail to lookup** is allowed only if no roll-up summary fields exist on the parent that summarize this relationship. Delete the roll-ups first, and accept that you are also deleting their values.

Neither conversion is casual, and both are best avoided by getting the choice right initially.

## Reparenting

By default a master-detail child cannot be moved to a different parent. There is a checkbox to allow it, and enabling it has consequences: the child's sharing changes when it moves, and any roll-up on both the old and new parent must recalculate.

Lookup children can always be reparented.

## Special cases covered elsewhere

- **Many-to-many** requires a junction object with two master-detail relationships. See [[02.8 - Many-to-Many and Junction Objects]].
- **Self relationships** connect an object to itself, for hierarchies. See [[02.9 - Hierarchical and Self Relationships]].
- **Hierarchical relationships** are a special lookup available only on User.
- **External lookups** and **indirect lookups** connect standard objects to external objects. See [[02.14 - External Objects and Salesforce Connect]].

## What goes wrong in practice

**Master-detail chosen for a roll-up, sharing broken as a side effect.** The most common modelling mistake in the ecosystem. Reach for the alternatives in 02.6 first.

**Lookup left optional when the business requires a parent.** Records arrive from an integration with an empty parent, and every downstream report and roll-up silently misses them. Mark the lookup required if the business rule says it is.

**Deep master-detail chains.** Three levels is the maximum, and even two levels means a delete at the top cascades a long way. Cascade deletes ignore some automation and can remove far more than anyone intended. See [[02.10 - Delete Behaviour - Cascade and Restrict]].

**Data skew from a single dominant parent.** If one Account owns 200,000 child records, saving any child locks that Account, and concurrent operations collide. This is an architecture problem, not a code problem. See [[10.9 - Data Skew]].
